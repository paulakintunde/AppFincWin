Two modelling choices D-44's text leaves open are now settled by the user (04-CONTEXT D-59, 2026-10-15):
1. **The daily spread (04-REPLAN-NOTES 2026-10-15 expansion default 1, accepted in D-59).** Undated spending (groceries and everything not in a confirmed series) and the minimum debt payments (debts have no dates in the snapshot) are spread evenly by day, so the path never overstates cash. Saving and investing contributions are not deducted: they are pausable (D-35) and usually stay inside the accounts cashOnHand already counts. The spread is computed in exactly one exported function, `spreadUndatedSpending(spreadMonthly, day)`; `buildCashPath` calls it and nothing else computes it.
2. **The `cash_dip` measure (D-59; replaces the replan's default 2).** `cash_dip` fires only when the purchase causes or deepens the dip: the lowest point with the purchase must be below the reserve (soft) or below 0 (hard) AND lower than the lowest point without the purchase. Its shortfall is the extra depth the purchase adds below the line. Under "one card per cause" (D-24/D-44 amended) the `cash` card shows `max(0, reserve − cashAfter)` with `cashAfter = cashOnHand − my day-one cost`; only part of that gap is the purchase's doing, `max(0, min(reserve, cashOnHand) − cashAfter)` (the day-one cost below the reserve line), because any gap between cashOnHand and the reserve exists before the purchase. The dip carries the purchase's extra depth less that purchase-caused part, so nothing is subtracted twice and nothing the purchase did not cause is subtracted at all (checker W1, 2026-10-15: subtracting the whole cash-card gap double-counted a pre-existing reserve gap — dayOne 0, cashOnHand 500_000, reserve 600_000, lowestWithout 450_000, lowestWith 350_000 gave no dip although the purchase took the low 100_000 deeper below the reserve):
   - `atReserve = max(0, min(reserve, lowestWithout) − lowestWith − max(0, min(reserve, cashOnHand) − cashAfter))`
   - `atZero = max(0, min(0, lowestWithout) − lowestWith − max(0, min(0, cashOnHand) − cashAfter))`
   - `dipShortfall = max(atReserve, atZero)`; fire iff > 0; hard iff `lowestWith < 0`.
   `CashDipInput` therefore carries `cashOnHand` (cash before this purchase on the positioned context; 04-07 and 04-20 pass `context.cashAvailable`, which is read only when a path exists, equals `snapshot.cashOnHand − outflowsDueNow` (D-61) when no other checks are stacked, and after D-41 stacking (04-18) already excludes the other checks' day-one costs, so those are never counted as this purchase's cash-card gap) beside `cashAfter`. Whenever cashOnHand >= the reserve the subtraction equals the earlier `max(0, reserve − cashAfter)`, and whenever cashOnHand >= 0 the zero-line subtraction equals the earlier `max(0, −cashAfter)`, which is why every fixture figure in 04-07 and 04-20 is unchanged (re-derived in Task 2). `atZero` is still needed because the reserve layer alone conflicts with D-59's "below 0 (hard)" in one case: when cash on hand is already below the reserve, the purchase-caused part of the cash card can absorb the whole extra depth while the purchase takes the path below zero (lowestWith −195_000, lowestWithout 5_000, reserve 600_000, cashOnHand 300_000, cashAfter 100_000: the purchase-caused cash-card part is 200_000, atReserve is 0, so no card would make the state `no`). With `atZero`, whenever the purchase takes the low below 0 and lower than it is without the purchase, either the dip is hard or `cashAfter < 0` already makes the state `no`.
   The measure lives in exactly one exported function, `dipShortfall(input: CashDipInput)`. `cashPathMargin(input)` (for 04-20's D-43 `cash_path` line) reads the same terms: `min(roomReserve, roomZero)` with `roomReserve = lowestWith − min(reserve, lowestWithout) + max(0, min(reserve, cashOnHand) − cashAfter)` and `roomZero = lowestWith − min(0, lowestWithout) + max(0, min(0, cashOnHand) − cashAfter)`. Algebraically `dipShortfall = max(0, −min(roomReserve, roomZero))`, so the margin is −dipShortfall when a dip fires and otherwise the room (>= 0) the with-purchase low has before a dip would fire, with the day-one cost unchanged (checker W3: the earlier `lowestWith − reserve` margin read a purchase that does not lower the low as failing).
3. **A dip that exists without the purchase is a fact (D-59).** `cashTightFact(lowestWithout, reserve)` returns `{ code: 'cash_tight_before_payday', date, amount }` (the without-purchase low) when `lowestWithout.amount < reserve`, else null; `cashPathReport` carries it as `tightBeforePayday`, and 04-07 appends it to `Assessment.facts`. It never adds a warning and never changes the state.
