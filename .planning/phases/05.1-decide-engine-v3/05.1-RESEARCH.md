# Phase 05.1: Decide engine v3 - Research

**Researched:** 2026-10-25
**Domain:** Pure TypeScript financial engine (integer minor units), property-based testing, shadow comparison against a legacy engine, in an Expo SDK 57 / Jest 29 repo on Windows
**Confidence:** HIGH for the codebase inventory, gates and test infrastructure (all verified by reading the files in `C:\dev\fincwin-p5`). MEDIUM for spec readings where the Decision Research documents conflict or leave a gap (each one is flagged).

## Summary

The design is settled, so this research looks at **where v3 lands in today's code and how to prove it**. Today's engine already contains much of what v3 needs:

- `payoff/amortise.ts` already amortises per period. It handles 12, 26, 52 or 4 periods a year, pays on the day (`amortiseDue`) or one period later (`amortise`), and works out an exact final payment (D-64 rounds the level payment up and the last payment takes the remainder). That gives D-05's "23 × $85.00, then $84.62" for free.
- `recurring/detect.ts` already groups payments and applies the ±10% amount rule and the minimum repeat counts (3, or 2 for quarterly and yearly).
- `recurring/offer.ts` already filters suggestions through a `dismissedKeys` set backed by a server table.
- `money/rounding.ts` `divideHalfUp` is the only rounding function, and the SQL side mirrors it.

The real gaps are:

1. **Two-fixed-date pay.** Pay on the 1st and 15th gives gaps of 14 to 17 days. Today it is either missed (a 17-day gap falls outside the fortnightly window of 12–16) or mis-detected as fortnightly (26 a year instead of 24).
2. **Yearly plans and "every N weeks or months"** (D-01, D-19). `PeriodsPerYear` is `12|26|52|4`.
3. **A deferred first payment** (D-04). Plans only have `'today' | 'next_period'`.
4. **The quoted payment always wins today.** `plan.ts` says "a typed amount is always the one scored", which breaks I41.
5. **The cash path spreads too little.** It spreads `spendingBasis − seriesMonthly`, which is the B2 near-zero spread. The rest of this month needs Erest and each later month needs E.

Several facts about the codebase **matter more than the plan documents suggest**. The planner has to design around them:

- `MinorUnits` is a **branded safe-integer `number`, not a bigint**. Bigint is used only inside arithmetic, and `minorFromBigInt` brings values back. "Bigint minor units" in CONTEXT means this convention.
- The depcruise rule `decide-engine-barrel-only` **forbids anything outside `engine/` from importing `src/engine/decide/v3/index.ts`**. The S9 seam needs a one-line rule change.
- The two exact-surface barrel tests fail if `v3` is re-exported from the old barrel.
- `public-api.test.ts` forbids `recurring/detect` in the verdict path (D-25). v3 needs the same isolation test so that a candidate never reaches the judge (I34).
- `recurring/schedule.ts` is **mirrored in SQL** (`check:recurring-mirror`). The `RecurringFreq` type must not grow an "every N" variant in this phase.
- `constants.ts` forbids currency amounts ("No currency amount may be added here"). Band edges, the $200 drift threshold and the $1 quoted-payment threshold need their own cited constants module.
- Jest is **29.7.0** (CLAUDE.md says 30.x; that is stale). There is **no `tsx` or `ts-node`**, so the shadow run must run inside Jest.

**Primary recommendation:**
- Build `src/engine/decide/v3/` as its own barrel. It imports only leaf modules: `payoff/*`, `money/*`, `recurring/schedule` and `detect`, `time/*`, `split/*`, and from `decide/` only `share.ts`, `cardRule.ts`, `series.ts` and `bisectLargest` (moved to a leaf file). It does **not** import `assess.ts`, `types.ts` verdict types or `AssessContext`, so the old engine can be deleted at cutover (D-16) without touching v3.
- Run the shadow comparison as Jest tests over a neutral, seeded history model (`fc.sample` with a fixed seed, verified deterministic here) that adapts to both engines.

## User Constraints (from CONTEXT.md)

### Locked Decisions

#### Already locked by the Decision Research (do not re-ask; read the canonical refs)
- **Model:** v3 is the source of truth (`00-REBUILD-PLAN-v3.md` §4).
  - It starts from regular payments.
  - Money in vs money out uses Erest; it never assumes spending stops.
  - The typical month is the median of everyday spending over at most 6 full months.
  - The low month is the second-lowest real month.
  - Unspent money is the user-confirmed figure: balance − bills due − Erest − envelopes − emergency fund. It rolls forward with money in and out and is re-asked on drift (10% or $200).
  - The emergency fund is never counted in ordinary answers. It is used only for purchases marked "a need" or "for work".
  - Envelopes are only available to their own purpose.
- **Engine structure, types, judge precedence, needs-input rules (5.4), invariants I1–I43, tests and slices S0–S9:** `00-UPGRADE-PLAN.md` §5 and §7.2.
- **Pay timing:**
  - "Every 2 weeks" is shown, never "twice a month".
  - Pay on two fixed dates each month (1st/15th) is two monthly series in one `pay_group`, counted 24 a year with no migration (C2).
- **Fixed-rate deposit:** it uses the user's own rate (05 D-07 and D-51 restored). There are no market example rates. For financed purchases, "Instead" compares the payment stream (C11).
- **Quoted payment (C1):** the engine works out the payment, and the user may type the lender's quoted payment. If the two differ by more than $1, a warning shows, and the quoted payment never silently improves an answer (I41).
- **Superseded Phase 5 decisions (record and do not carry forward):**
  - D-35 relief, D-36, D-42 (cushion), D-55 (prompts become preset questions);
  - D-100 hero, D-105 (pots become envelopes), D-106 ("free"/"overspent" wording), D-107 (Judge-on and the three bases);
  - v2's I24 and protection months.
- **Discarded:**
  - worst month (min/max across months), plain-mean average, lender lines as gates;
  - setbacks, stress and income-at-risk inside Decide;
  - the 65% "cheaper" default, the five-step flow, the first-read state machine, size labels as a separate axis.

#### Payment plan timing
- **D-01:** Plan frequencies are weekly, every 2 weeks, monthly, **every 3 months** and **yearly** (v3 open question 1 closed). Each period's payment, interest and count are exact; the monthly equivalent is `divideHalfUp(payment × perYear, 12)`.
- **D-02:** **This month follows each bill's real schedule.**
  - A once-a-year bill paid in one go counts in full in the month it is due.
  - A bill paid in instalments (even one tagged annual) counts each instalment when it falls due.
  - Recurring detection keeps quarterly and yearly cadences (85–95 and 360–370 days, at least 2 repeats).
- **D-03:** **Typical month and yearly or quarterly bills:**
  - Each such bill carries a per-bill choice, "count it monthly in my typical month" (1/12 or 1/3 of the amount) or not.
  - It is labelled a yearly (or quarterly) cycle, so it reads as an outlier.
  - The engine never silently averages or drops it.
  - Until the user answers, the bill is marked "not yet chosen". Planner: pick the safe default (counted monthly for monthly-cost tests) and surface the question as a notice.
- **D-04:** **First payment date is always asked per plan.** The choices are today, one period after buying, or a deferred date ("nothing to pay for 6 months"). There is no silent default. The schedule and every month it touches follow the chosen start.
- **D-05:** **The final payment is exact**, for example "23 × $85.00, then $84.62", so plan totals match the lender to the cent. Rounding is `divideHalfUp` only (05 D-21).

#### Price bands abroad
- **D-06:** The 15 band edges are defined in US$ and converted to each home currency, then rounded to tidy numbers (for example $200 becomes ¥30,000 or ₦300,000).
- **D-07:** The converted edges are a **fixed table per currency** shipped in the engine (constants with a "FincWin choice" tag) and reviewed with app updates. They never follow live rates, so a purchase never changes band overnight. A currency missing from the table falls back to converting the US$ edges at today's rate, rounded tidily. The planner should define the rounding rule.
- **D-08:** Bands are the **same for everyone** and not scaled by the user's income. They only decide how many questions are asked; the answer is personal anyway.
- **D-09:** A foreign-currency purchase is banded by its **home-currency whole value at today's rate**, marked "at today's rate", using the existing `currency.ts`.

#### Limits (28/36/12) and your own lines
- **D-10:** **Lines are facts, never pass/fail.**
  - They are off by default.
  - When a line is on and a purchase crosses it, the result adds one plain fact, for example "This brings your debt payments to 41% of your income; your line is 36%."
  - A line **never changes the answer code** (consistent with "notices never change the code").
- **D-11:** Built-in lines offered: **housing 28%** of take-home income, **all debt payments 36%**, **unsecured debt 12×** monthly income. The defaults come from `src/engine/decide/constants.ts` (DEFAULT_HOUSING_LINE, DEFAULT_DSR_LINE, DEFAULT_UNSECURED_INCOME_MULTIPLE).
- **D-12:** **Your own lines (new), up to 5,** each of one of four kinds:
  1. a monthly cap on a category (category spending plus the purchase's monthly cost), for example "car costs under $600 a month";
  2. a single-purchase cap, for example "nothing over $2,000";
  3. a share of income, for example "subscriptions under 5% of my income";
  4. keep unspent money above an amount, for example "keep at least $1,000".

  The engine evaluates them as facts. Storage is 05.2 and screens are 05.3.
- **D-13:** Existing users' line values from today's Decide **carry over, switched on**, so nothing they chose is lost. The adapter maps them in (S8, `adapters/legacy.ts`).

#### Switch-on rules (shadow run and cutover)
- **D-14:** **Every changed answer** between today's engine and v3 is listed.
  - Each case where v3 says it fits (`fits_month` or `fits_unspent`) and today's engine did not must have a **written reason the owner approves**.
  - All other changes are reviewed in groups by cause, for example "the median ignored a one-off vet bill".
- **D-15:** **Switch-on order:**
  1. named test accounts first (the owner's and the demo account);
  2. everyone, by over-the-air update, after the owner signs off.

  It can be turned off the same way. The per-account switch needs a small data change (05.2). In 05.1 the engine only exposes the v3 path behind one switch read in one place.
- **D-16:** Today's Decide code is kept, unused, for **2 weeks after switch-on**, then deleted in a cleanup change.
- **D-17:** **Shadow-run data:**
  - about 300 generated histories, the 8 personas and the 22 scenarios;
  - the owner's own test accounts, read locally on the owner's machine.

  No other user's data is used (GDPR).

#### Use cases, custom options, and add/edit/delete
- **D-18:** **Extra fixed test cases** beyond the 8 personas and 22 scenarios, each a golden fixture:
  - **Pay-in-4 and 0% deals:** pay-in-4 every 2 weeks, and "nothing to pay for 6 months, then 24 payments".
  - **Returns and refunds:** the item is returned, the money comes back, the plan is cancelled and unspent money is restored.
  - **Shared and foreign buys:** a household purchase split 50/50, and a purchase in another currency.
  - **Pay changes mid-month:** a raise, a job loss, or a missed paycheque.
- **D-19:** **Custom options. Preset lists stay; users can add up to 10 of each:**
  - **Own item type:** named by the user. Its extra-cost questions are ticked from the question library (insurance, fuel, storage, upkeep, subscriptions, tax, fees and so on), plus up to 3 questions of the user's own. The amounts are always the user's; there are no preset amounts.
  - **Own purpose label**, for example "for the kids". **Emergency use stays limited to "a need" and "for work"**; a custom purpose can never use the emergency fund.
  - **Own payment timing:** every N weeks or every N months, N from 1 to 12, counted exactly ("every 4 weeks" is 13 a year, not 12). It applies to both plans and bills.
  - **Own income type**, for example "child benefit", marked available or kept like other income lines (nothing counts until it is marked).
  - Each custom option can be renamed, edited or deleted. Deleting one never changes past answers or recorded decisions.
- **D-20:** **Edits re-answer saved plans:**
  - Editing or deleting data (a transaction, a bill, unspent money, a line) re-answers saved plans (open checks) at once.
  - If an answer changes, the plan is marked "Changed since you looked" with the reason.
  - Recorded decisions are **never** recalculated (I42; `verdict_at_decision` untouched).
- **D-21:** **Deleting a confirmed regular payment asks "ended or mistake?":**
  - "It ended" keeps past months as they were and drops the payment from now on.
  - "It was never regular" removes it from history and the typical month, and remembers the dismissal so it isn't suggested again.

  - the screenshot-sensitive flag on Decide screens (05.3).

## Project Constraints (from CLAUDE.md)

- **Engine purity:** nothing in `engine/` may import `db/`, `state/`, `services/`, `ui/`, `features/` or `react`/`react-native`/`expo*`/`@supabase`/`posthog`. This is enforced by depcruise (`engine-only-internal-src`, `engine-no-reach-impure`), by ESLint `boundaries` plus `no-restricted-imports` in `eslint.config.js:29-104`, by `public-api.test.ts` "purity (DEC-12)", and by the probes in `verify:gates`.
- **Money:** integer minor units everywhere, never floats. `divideHalfUp` is the only rounding (`money/rounding.ts`). `ceilDivide` is allowed only for counts and the D-64 level payment.
- **Quality gate:** a 100% branch, function, line and statement threshold on `src/engine/decide/` (this covers `v3/` automatically) and on `src/features/decide/snapshot/`. An engine change that drops branch coverage fails CI.
- **Coverage-ignore comments** in `money|decide|payoff|split` need `-- reason: <10+ chars>` (`check:ignores`).
- **Compliance wording:** never "advice", "recommendation" or "you should" in verdict copy. The engine emits codes only, and `outputs-are-codes.test.ts:566-576` scans engine source for `advice|recommend|you should`.
- **GSD workflow:** edits go through GSD commands; PR-based flow (main is protected).
- **Supabase:** do not use the Supabase MCP. Production is the only project, and migrations are out of scope for 05.1 anyway (05.2).
- **Stack note:** CLAUDE.md says Jest 30.x, but the repo pins `jest ~29.7.0` (verified 29.7.0 installed). Use 29 APIs.

### Pattern 3: Exact "every N" cadences without touching `payoff/`
The per-period rate in `amortise` is `apr / rateDenominator(ppy)` = `apr / (100 × ppy × SCALE)`.

| Cadence | Payments a year | Rate per period |
|---|---|---|
| every N weeks | 52 / N | apr × N / (100 × 52 × SCALE) |
| every N months | 12 / N | apr × N / (100 × 12 × SCALE) |

So `amortise(P, (apr × N) as Percent, count, 52 | 12)` is **exactly** the same rational as a native N-period ppy. Every bigint step in `solve`/`walkLevel` is a ratio of the same rational, so the ceilings and half-up roundings are identical. This covers:
- weekly = {week, 1};
- every 2 weeks = {week, 2} (equals ppy 26);
- monthly = {month, 1};
- every 3 months = {month, 3} (equals ppy 4);
- yearly = {month, 12};
- D-19 "every 4 weeks" = 13 a year, without changing `PeriodsPerYear`.

Prove it with a property test: for N ∈ {2 weeks, 3 months}, the result equals `amortise(P, apr, n, 26 | 4)` field for field.

Monthly equivalent, as one `divideHalfUp` (D-01, I37):
- weeks: `divideHalfUp(payment × 52, 12 × N)`;
- months: `divideHalfUp(payment, N)`.

Count from a term (§4.4): `divideHalfUp(perYearNum × termMonths, 12 × perYearDen)`. Prefer the user typing the **number of payments** (pay-in-4 = 4), so nothing is invented.

```ts
// v3/plan/cadence.ts (sketch)
export type Cadence = { readonly unit: 'week' | 'month'; readonly every: number }; // every ∈ 1..12
export const WEEKLY = { unit: 'week', every: 1 } as const;          // 52/yr
export const BIWEEKLY = { unit: 'week', every: 2 } as const;        // 26/yr
export const MONTHLY = { unit: 'month', every: 1 } as const;        // 12/yr
export const QUARTERLY = { unit: 'month', every: 3 } as const;      // 4/yr
export const YEARLY = { unit: 'month', every: 12 } as const;        // 1/yr
const base = (c: Cadence): 52 | 12 => (c.unit === 'week' ? 52 : 12);
export function scaledApr(apr: Percent, c: Cadence): Percent { return (apr * BigInt(c.every)) as Percent; }
export function monthlyEquivalentOf(payment: MinorUnits, c: Cadence): MinorUnits {
  return minorFromBigInt(divideHalfUp(BigInt(payment) * BigInt(base(c)), 12n * BigInt(c.every)), 'monthlyEquivalentOf');
}
// amortise(financed, scaledApr(apr, c), count, base(c))  /  amortiseDue(...) for 'today'
```
The sketch follows the source of `payoff/amortise.ts:84-165` and `types.ts:18-22`. Its exactness is argued above and is not yet tested.

Occurrence dates for "every N" use `occurrenceDate(anchor, 'weekly', n × N)` for weeks (day-based, exact), and for months the same month arithmetic with `every × n` months. Re-implement that ~10-line month step in v3, because `MONTHS_PER_OCCURRENCE` is private and SQL-mirrored.

### Pattern 4: First-payment offset (D-04)
- `{ kind: 'today' }` → `amortiseDue`. The day-one cost includes the first payment (as `plan.ts:367`).
- `{ kind: 'one_period' }` → `amortise`.
- `{ kind: 'deferred', firstDate }` → the schedule starts at `firstDate`. - Every month the schedule touches is computed from real occurrence dates, so this month's money out and the cash path see the payment in its real month (D-02).

### Pattern 5: Cash path without zero spend (S3, B2, I2, I19)
`buildCashPath` subtracts `spreadUndatedSpending(spreadMonthly, d)`, which is linear in d at one monthly rate. v3 needs Erest over the rest of this month, then E per month. Do one of:
1. call `buildCashPath` with `spreadMonthly = E` and add a dated outflow for `max(0, Erest − E×(N−d)/N)` (the extra of `E − everydayLogged`) on day 0; or
2. write `v3/cash/path.ts` with a piecewise spread (Erest across days 0..(N−d), then E/month), reusing the cumulative half-up idea.

Option 1 keeps a single spread source. Either way, property I2 says the path's cumulative spread from day 0 to the end of this month must be at least Erest, and each later full month at least E. I19 says the cash path must not also subtract Erest inside money-out-derived opening cash. Today's code has the same trap and avoids it with `seriesMonthly` (`cashPath.ts:181-184`).

```ts
  | { kind: 'unspent_floor'; floor: MinorUnits };                            // D-12 (4)
type Limits = { readonly lines: readonly { id: string; on: boolean; spec: LineSpec }[] }; // ≤ 3 built-in + ≤ 5 own
// judge(model, funds, purchase, limits) → Result.facts gets { code: 'line_crossed', lineId, value, line } — never changes Result.code
```
- Property: for any `Limits`, `judge(...).code` and `split` are identical to `judge(..., {lines: []})`. This extends "notices never change the code" (D-10).
- **D-13 legacy mapping:** `decide_settings.dsr_line` (text decimal), `housing_line` (text, ≤ 100), and `unsecured_multiple` (int 1–120) map to the three built-in lines. A **non-null** value means `on: true` (the user chose it). Null means off with the default value offered. `reserve` is the emergency fund amount (C3). The SQL columns live in `supabase/migrations/20261016000100_decide_tables.sql:99-145` and `20261024000200_decide_settings_housing_line.sql`. The parse rules already exist in `buildSnapshot.ts` (`DSR_PATTERN`, `MAX_UNSECURED_MULTIPLE = 120`, `MAX_LINE_PERCENT = 100`).
- **Custom options (D-19)** enter as plain data:
  - `ItemTypeSpec = { id, name?, base: PresetItemType | 'custom', questionIds: QuestionId[], ownQuestions: {id}[] (≤3) }`;
  - `Purpose = PresetPurpose | { custom: id }`, where `canUseEmergency(p) = p === 'necessity' || p === 'for_work'`;
  - `Cadence` from Pattern 3;
  - `IncomeKind = preset | { custom: id }` with `countsAs: 'available'|'kept'|null`.

  The engine never reads custom names (as today: "the name (1-40) never reaches the engine", `checkInputs.ts` D-81). Deletion never changes past answers, because recorded decisions are not recomputed (I42).

### Pattern 8: Re-answer saved plans (D-20)
Provide a pure `changeSinceLooked(prev: StoredSeen, next: Result) → { changed: boolean; reasons: ChangeReason[] }`, where reasons are codes such as `code_changed`, `unspent_changed`, `bill_changed`, `line_changed`. The seen state today is the legacy 4-state `last_seen_state`. C7 says "re-baselined once": the first v3 read writes the v3 seen state with no "changed" marker. `verdict_at_decision` is never an input to any recompute (I42).

### Anti-Patterns to Avoid
- **Calling the old `resolvePlan`/`buildWaitPlan`/`affordablePrice`/`totalCost` from v3.** They take old `BuyCheck`/`AssessContext`/`Verdict`, so v3 would silently inherit B-bugs and break at D-16 deletion.
- **Adding `semimonthly` or "every N" to `RecurringFreq`.** It breaks the SQL mirror and C2's "no migration".
- **Putting band edges or the $200/$1 thresholds in `decide/constants.ts`.** That violates its "no currency amount" header rule. Use `v3/constants.ts`/`bandEdges.ts` with `// Cite:` lines and test them the way `constants.test.ts` does.
- **Shadow results reaching UI state.** Upgrade plan §8: "shadow results never shown to users".
- **Reading `Date.now()`/`new Date()` in v3 (I16).** `asOf` is an input. Today's code uses `new Date(Date.UTC(...))` only for pure calendar maths; that is allowed. Ban zero-argument `new Date()` and `Date.now` with a static test.

## Don't Hand-Roll

| Problem | Don't Build | Use Instead | Why |
|---------|-------------|-------------|-----|
| Level payment, exact final payment | A new amortiser | `amortise`/`amortiseDue` (+ scaled APR) | D-64 rounding-up loop, 600-period cap, residual handling and golden parity against the prototype are already proven |
| Calendar occurrences, month-end clamp | Stepping dates | `occurrenceDate`/`occurrencesBetween`/`projectOccurrences` | Clamping drift is a known trap; SQL-mirrored |
| Rounding | `Math.round`, `/` on numbers | `divideHalfUp` (bigint) | Single rounding authority (D-21); float drift |
| Largest affordable price | A new bisection | `bisectLargest(upper, fits)` | Proven termination and bounded-error properties |
| Household share | Percent maths | `resolveShare` + `myPart` (`split/allocate` largest-remainder) | The Alabama-paradox handling is already documented and tested |
| Deposit growth of a payment stream | A loop with floats | `futureValueExact` / `futureValuePathExact` | Exact bigint compounding |
| Implied rate of a quote (C1) | Newton on floats | `impliedApr({timing})` | Exists, integer-safe, MAX_APR handling |
| Sales tax on the advertised price | Ad-hoc percent | `salesTaxAmount` | Same rounding as the old engine, so the shadow diff stays clean |
| Deterministic random histories | `Math.random` with a seed shim | `fc.sample(arb, { seed, numRuns })` | Verified deterministic with fast-check 4.10.2 |
| Recurring grouping and normalisation | A new payee normaliser | `normaliseDescription` (`categorize/guessCategory`) via `detect.ts` | Same keys as `offerKey`, so dismissals keep working |

**Key insight:** the dangerous parts of this domain (rounding, schedules, amortisation residuals) are already solved and SQL-mirrored. v3's risk is in **definitions** (what counts as everyday, roll-forward, which median) and in **wiring** (which engine a screen reads). Spend the effort on properties and golden fixtures, not on new maths.

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: 1st/15th pay detected as fortnightly, or not detected
**What goes wrong:** the gaps for 1st → 15th → 1st are 14, then 13–16 depending on the month (for example Jan 15 → Feb 1 is **17**). `detect.ts` requires **every** gap inside 12–16, so the group fails, or (when no 31-day month is involved) it passes as fortnightly at 26 a year instead of 24.
**How to avoid:** in `v3/month/recurring.ts`, test the "two fixed days of month (±3)" pattern **before** the fortnightly pattern: cluster days-of-month into two clusters, each repeating monthly. Emit two monthly candidates sharing a `payGroupKey`. Fixture: 6 months of 1st/15th pay including a 31-day month and February.
**Warning signs:** a pay candidate with `freq: 'fortnightly'` whose dates' day-of-month values form two tight clusters.

### Pitfall 2: The quoted payment silently improves the answer
`plan.ts:347-349` scores a typed instalment amount over the computed one. In v3, with a rate known, score `max(computed, quoted)` and emit `quoted_differs` when `|quoted − computed| > QUOTED_TOLERANCE` ($1 in home minor units, a v3 constant). With no rate, the quote is the only figure; mark it `from_quote` and keep the I36 rule that the rate is never invented.

### Pitfall 3: The even-count median rule changes Sam's numbers
Sam's v3 typical month of $425 needs E = $1,475:
- everyday by month (everyday includes big buys, because it is all non-recurring, non-transfer outflow): Apr 1,100 · May 1,600 · Jun 3,600 · Jul 1,300 · Aug 1,350 · Sep 1,850;
- sorted, the middle two are 1,350 and 1,600; their **mean** is 1,475;
- typicalLeft = 4,000 − 1,800 − 1,475 − 300 = **425** ✓.

`detect.ts`'s `lowerMedian` (the lower of the two middle values) would give 1,350 and a typical month of $550, which contradicts the mockup (C15). **Rule: E = divideHalfUp(a + b, 2) for an even count.** Pin it with the Sam fixture.

Checked against the same data:
- **October on Oct 3** with $100 logged: Erest = max(⌊1475×28/31⌉ = 1,332, 1475 − 100 = 1,375) = 1,375. Money out = 1,800 + 100 + 1,375 + 300 = **3,575**, and monthLeft = **425** ✓ (matches the v3 home bar "$4,000 in · $3,575 out").
- **Low month:** leftovers at today's pay are 800, 300, −1,700, 600, 550, 50. The second-lowest is **$50** (September), and I15 holds.
- **Sofa $4,000:** ready = ceilDiv(4,000 − 2,400, 425) = 4 months → **February** ✓ (v3 §12 #10).
- **Phone $85:** 425 − 85 ≥ 0, but 50 − 85 < 0 → fits plus the low-month clause ✓ (#7).
- **Dog #22:** the $120 a month also gives lowLeft − M < 0, so the low-month variant applies. v3 §12 says only "passes the typical month". Confirm in S0.

### Pitfall 4: `00-SCENARIOS.md` numbers are the v1 model
That document's shared steps use the big-buy allowance, the tight month and safety money. Sam's "normal month $400" and "safety money $9,000" there are **v1** figures. The **v3 truth is `00-REBUILD-PLAN-v3.md` §12** (typical $425, unspent $2,400, emergency fund $9,000 named by the user). Golden fixtures must encode v3 §12 expectations. Use SCENARIOS only for the people and situations, not for expected numbers.

### Pitfall 5: `MinorUnits` is a number; band edges can exceed the DB cap
- Top band edges converted to some currencies exceed `MAX_ABS_AMOUNT_MINOR` (1e13):
  - IDR (exponent 2): $10M ≈ 1.65e13;
  - IQD (exponent 3): ≈ 1.3e13;
  - LBP: ≈ 9e13.
- The snapshot's own input bound is `MAX_INPUT_MINOR = 1e12` (`features/decide/snapshot/checkInputs.ts:62`), so the high bands are unreachable in those currencies.
- **Rule:** clamp table edges at `MAX_ABS_AMOUNT_MINOR`. Whole values above the last finite edge are band 15. Test that edges are strictly increasing per currency.
- Also: never `Number(bigint)` without `minorFromBigInt`, because it silently loses precision.

### Pitfall 6: Tidy rounding must keep the edges strictly increasing
**Recommended (Claude's discretion):**
- Fallback edge = USD edge × rate, then **round half-up to 1 significant figure** in major units, then multiply by 10^exponent. This reproduces CONTEXT's examples ($200 → ¥30,000 at ~149, → ₦300,000 at ~1,530).
- Adjacent USD edges differ by ×2 or more, so 1-sf rounding cannot merge two edges. Still assert strict increase.
- The **shipped table** uses the same rule against a reference rate whose date sits in the `// Cite:` comment (D-07 "reviewed with app updates").
- **Fallback with no stored USD rate:** band = **the highest band the price could be in**, which asks more questions and is the cautious choice, with reason code `band_rate_missing` (I29). The snapshot already has the pattern: `cardMaxPurchase` null means no stored USD rate.

### Pitfall 7: The emergency flag leaks through the funding split
I31 needs **every figure** identical for an emergency fund of $0 or $1M with the flag off, not just the code. That includes notices ("An emergency fund goal isn't set yet" changes between null and set). **Read I31 as:** for a fund of $0 vs $1M (both *set*), the outputs are identical. Test null and set separately.

### Pitfall 8: Two engines mixing (upgrade plan §8)
Read the switch in exactly one function (`decideEngine()` in `availability.ts`) and the result type in one adapter (`computeV3`). Add a static test that no file other than `availability.ts` reads `DECIDE_V3`, the same way `DECIDE_OPEN` is read today in `app/(app)/...` and `SetupAccountScreen.tsx`. That test is a little brittle; a grep-based Jest test is the existing style (`public-api.test.ts`).

### Pitfall 9: Windows Jest performance
- The full suite is about 619 suites, and phase summaries always run `npm run test:coverage -- --maxWorkers=4`.
- **Shadow:** 300 histories × several purchases × two engines. Keep it under ~20 s:
  - run the old engine once per history;
  - cap `fc.assert` `numRuns` at 50–150 like the existing tests;
  - keep `amortise` term ≤ 600 (fast).
- Put the full 300-history report behind an env flag (`SHADOW_REPORT=1`). The default CI run uses the committed approved-changes file plus a smaller seeded subset, or all 300 if fast enough.
- Worktree note (jest.config.js): `roots` confines the crawl, so tests in nested `.claude/worktrees` are not picked up.

### Pitfall 10: Old `ItemType` exhaustiveness
`RUNNING_COST_PROMPTS … satisfies Record<ItemType, …>` and the `assertNever` switches in `cardRule.ts` and `runningCostPrompts.ts` mean that adding electronics, subscription or emergency_repair to `ITEM_TYPES` breaks the old engine and changes old answers mid-shadow. Keep a v3-only superset type (Reuse Inventory).

### Pitfall 11: The everyday definition and D-21 "never regular"
Everyday = non-recurring, non-transfer outflow (§4.4). After "It was never regular", that series' past lines must count as **everyday** spending (they were real spending), not vanish. "It ended" keeps past months classified as recurring up to `endDate`. Engine input: per-series `status: 'live' | 'ended' | 'dismissed'`. Lines whose `seriesId` names a dismissed series, or no series, are everyday. See Open Question 4.

### Pitfall 12: A yearly bill in this month vs the typical month (D-02, D-03)
Today's `series.ts` smooths every non-monthly bill (D-23) and deducts quarterly and yearly bills 90 days ahead (D-24).
- **v3 this month:** the full amount in the due month.
- **v3 typical month:** a per-bill `typicalChoice: 'monthly' | 'excluded' | null`. Null means **counted monthly** (cautious for monthly-cost tests), plus a notice code `yearly_bill_choice_needed` (Claude's discretion: the cautious default).
- **Cash path:** real dates.
- **Unspent "bills still due":** what about a yearly bill due in 2 months? `suggested` subtracts only *this month's* bills due (§4.4), so a $1,200 insurance bill due next month is not reserved. The 90-day cash path catches it (`not_yet` until payday / not covered). Flag in the fixtures (scenario 19).

## Open Questions (RESOLVED)

All seven were answered by the owner on 2026-10-25/26 and recorded in 05.1-CONTEXT.md. The CONTEXT decisions override the recommendations below where they differ (notably Q1: D-22, D-30 and D-31 replace the S(now) − S(anchorAt) formula).

1. **RESOLVED → D-22, D-30, D-31.** **Roll-forward definition (I40, §4.9).**
   - What we know: "unspent now = anchor + money in − money out since the anchor date", leaving out envelope moves, emergency-fund moves and own transfers. Suggested = balance − billsDue − Erest − envelopes − emergency.
   - What's unclear: paying a bill lowers the balance *and* `billsDue`, so a raw Σ(in − out) drifts from suggested even when nothing moved outside the records. Read literally, I40 is false.
   - Recommendation: define `rolled(now) = anchor + [S(now) − S(anchorAt)]`, where S is the suggested-sum function evaluated at both dates from records (the balance at `anchorAt` = balance now − Σ paid cash lines since). Then I40 holds by construction when anchor = S(anchorAt). Drift = `|rolled − S(now)|` = `|anchor − S(anchorAt)|` plus any unrecorded balance change (opening-balance edits, reconciliation). The user's own-figure offset is preserved. Confirm with the owner before S4.
2. **RESOLVED → D-23.** **The `legacyState` mapping (A2).** The owner should confirm before S8; it shapes D-14.
3. **RESOLVED → D-24.** **Deferred first payment with interest (D-04).** Does "nothing to pay for 6 months" accrue interest at the entered rate (capitalised), or only when the user marks deferred interest? Recommendation: the user picks, nothing is defaulted (D-07). The D-18 fixture uses 0% throughout.
4. **RESOLVED → D-25.** **D-21 "never regular" (A6):** reclassify past lines as everyday (recommended) or exclude them?
5. **RESOLVED → D-27.** **Owner-local shadow data (D-17):** how the owner's accounts become a `SnapshotSource` JSON on the owner's machine. Options:
   - (a) a dev-only app action that serialises `useDecideSnapshot().source`;
   - (b) a local SQL export via the Supabase CLI with the owner's household id.

   Recommendation: (a), behind `__DEV__`, writing to a local file. No other user's data is touched.
6. **RESOLVED → D-26.** **Currency-amount thresholds abroad.** The $200 drift threshold and the $1 quoted-payment tolerance are US$ amounts. Convert them through the same D-07 table mechanism (recommended) for consistency.
7. **RESOLVED → D-22 (stale and drifted flags) and D-30.** **Upgrade plan vs setup doc on re-asking unspent.** The setup doc's "re-asked every 30 days" (00-DECIDE-SETUP choices 4) conflicts with UP §4.9 (re-ask on drift, or 30 days plus a check over band 5). CONTEXT locks drift. The engine should expose both `stale` (30 days, I35 confidence cap) and `drifted` flags, and needs-input should fire on drift or on stale plus band ≥ 5.
