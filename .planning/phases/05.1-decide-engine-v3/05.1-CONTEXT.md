# Phase 05.1: Decide engine v3 - Context

**Gathered:** 2026-10-25
**Status:** Ready for planning

<domain>
## Phase Boundary

A pure, rule-based engine (no AI, no React, no database imports; bigint minor units; 100% branch coverage) answers "If I buy this, am I still OK this month and after?". It is built in `src/engine/decide/v3/` beside today's Phase 4/5 engine and compared with it before anything old is removed. It delivers:

- confirmed regular payments;
- money in vs money out this month, never assuming zero everyday spending;
- the typical month (median), the low month, unspent money (confirmed and rolled forward), the emergency fund and envelopes;
- payment plans with exact amortisation;
- the band and layer model;
- one judge with six codes, its funding split and notices, and the lines as facts;
- "What could I do instead?" using the payment stream;
- the v1 → v2 input adapter, the shadow run and the back-test;
- wiring of the snapshot inputs (S9).

Screens are phase 05.3, and data (migrations, setup, Activity prompts) is phase 05.2. This phase defines the engine inputs and outputs those phases use. Decide stays hidden (`DECIDE_OPEN = false`) throughout.

</domain>

<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### Already locked by the Decision Research (do not re-ask; read the canonical refs)
- **Model:** v3 is the source of truth (`00-REBUILD-PLAN-v3.md` §4).
  - It starts from regular payments.
  - Money in vs money out uses Erest; it never assumes spending stops.
  - The typical month is the median of everyday spending over at most 6 full months.
  - The low month is the second-lowest real month.
  - Unspent money is the user-confirmed figure: balance − bills due − Erest − envelopes − emergency fund. It rolls forward with money in and out and is re-asked on drift (10% or $200).
  - The emergency fund is never counted in ordinary answers. It is used only for purchases marked "a need" or "for work".
  - Envelopes are only available to their own purpose.
- **Engine structure, types, judge precedence, needs-input rules (5.4), invariants I1–I43, tests and slices S0–S9:** `00-UPGRADE-PLAN.md` §5 and §7.2.
- **Pay timing:**
  - "Every 2 weeks" is shown, never "twice a month".
  - Pay on two fixed dates each month (1st/15th) is two monthly series in one `pay_group`, counted 24 a year with no migration (C2).
- **Fixed-rate deposit:** it uses the user's own rate (05 D-07 and D-51 restored). There are no market example rates. For financed purchases, "Instead" compares the payment stream (C11).
- **Quoted payment (C1):** the engine works out the payment, and the user may type the lender's quoted payment. If the two differ by more than $1, a warning shows, and the quoted payment never silently improves an answer (I41).
- **Superseded Phase 5 decisions (record and do not carry forward):**
  - D-35 relief, D-36, D-42 (cushion), D-55 (prompts become preset questions);
  - D-100 hero, D-105 (pots become envelopes), D-106 ("free"/"overspent" wording), D-107 (Judge-on and the three bases);
  - v2's I24 and protection months.
- **Discarded:**
  - worst month (min/max across months), plain-mean average, lender lines as gates;
  - setbacks, stress and income-at-risk inside Decide;
  - the 65% "cheaper" default, the five-step flow, the first-read state machine, size labels as a separate axis.

### Payment plan timing
- **D-01:** Plan frequencies are weekly, every 2 weeks, monthly, **every 3 months** and **yearly** (v3 open question 1 closed). Each period's payment, interest and count are exact; the monthly equivalent is `divideHalfUp(payment × perYear, 12)`.
- **D-02:** **This month follows each bill's real schedule.**
  - A once-a-year bill paid in one go counts in full in the month it is due.
  - A bill paid in instalments (even one tagged annual) counts each instalment when it falls due.
  - Recurring detection keeps quarterly and yearly cadences (85–95 and 360–370 days, at least 2 repeats).
- **D-03:** **Typical month and yearly or quarterly bills:**
  - Each such bill carries a per-bill choice, "count it monthly in my typical month" (1/12 or 1/3 of the amount) or not.
  - It is labelled a yearly (or quarterly) cycle, so it reads as an outlier.
  - The engine never silently averages or drops it.
  - Until the user answers, the bill is marked "not yet chosen". Planner: pick the safe default (counted monthly for monthly-cost tests) and surface the question as a notice.
- **D-04:** **First payment date is always asked per plan.** The choices are today, one period after buying, or a deferred date ("nothing to pay for 6 months"). There is no silent default. The schedule and every month it touches follow the chosen start.
- **D-05:** **The final payment is exact**, for example "23 × $85.00, then $84.62", so plan totals match the lender to the cent. Rounding is `divideHalfUp` only (05 D-21).

### Price bands abroad
- **D-06:** The 15 band edges are defined in US$ and converted to each home currency, then rounded to tidy numbers (for example $200 becomes ¥30,000 or ₦300,000).
- **D-07:** The converted edges are a **fixed table per currency** shipped in the engine (constants with a "FincWin choice" tag) and reviewed with app updates. They never follow live rates, so a purchase never changes band overnight. A currency missing from the table falls back to converting the US$ edges at today's rate, rounded tidily. The planner should define the rounding rule.
- **D-08:** Bands are the **same for everyone** and not scaled by the user's income. They only decide how many questions are asked; the answer is personal anyway.
- **D-09:** A foreign-currency purchase is banded by its **home-currency whole value at today's rate**, marked "at today's rate", using the existing `currency.ts`.

### Limits (28/36/12) and your own lines
- **D-10:** **Lines are facts, never pass/fail.**
  - They are off by default.
  - When a line is on and a purchase crosses it, the result adds one plain fact, for example "This brings your debt payments to 41% of your income; your line is 36%."
  - A line **never changes the answer code** (consistent with "notices never change the code").
- **D-11:** Built-in lines offered: **housing 28%** of take-home income, **all debt payments 36%**, **unsecured debt 12×** monthly income. The defaults come from `src/engine/decide/constants.ts` (DEFAULT_HOUSING_LINE, DEFAULT_DSR_LINE, DEFAULT_UNSECURED_INCOME_MULTIPLE).
- **D-12:** **Your own lines (new), up to 5,** each of one of four kinds:
  1. a monthly cap on a category (category spending plus the purchase's monthly cost), for example "car costs under $600 a month";
  2. a single-purchase cap, for example "nothing over $2,000";
  3. a share of income, for example "subscriptions under 5% of my income";
  4. keep unspent money above an amount, for example "keep at least $1,000".

  The engine evaluates them as facts. Storage is 05.2 and screens are 05.3.
- **D-13:** Existing users' line values from today's Decide **carry over, switched on**, so nothing they chose is lost. The adapter maps them in (S8, `adapters/legacy.ts`).

### Switch-on rules (shadow run and cutover)
- **D-14:** **Every changed answer** between today's engine and v3 is listed.
  - Each case where v3 says it fits (`fits_month` or `fits_unspent`) and today's engine did not must have a **written reason the owner approves**.
  - All other changes are reviewed in groups by cause, for example "the median ignored a one-off vet bill".
- **D-15:** **Switch-on order:**
  1. named test accounts first (the owner's and the demo account);
  2. everyone, by over-the-air update, after the owner signs off.

  It can be turned off the same way. The per-account switch needs a small data change (05.2). In 05.1 the engine only exposes the v3 path behind one switch read in one place.
- **D-16:** Today's Decide code is kept, unused, for **2 weeks after switch-on**, then deleted in a cleanup change.
- **D-17:** **Shadow-run data:**
  - about 300 generated histories, the 8 personas and the 22 scenarios;
  - the owner's own test accounts, read locally on the owner's machine.

  No other user's data is used (GDPR).

### Use cases, custom options, and add/edit/delete
- **D-18:** **Extra fixed test cases** beyond the 8 personas and 22 scenarios, each a golden fixture:
  - **Pay-in-4 and 0% deals:** pay-in-4 every 2 weeks, and "nothing to pay for 6 months, then 24 payments".
  - **Returns and refunds:** the item is returned, the money comes back, the plan is cancelled and unspent money is restored.
  - **Shared and foreign buys:** a household purchase split 50/50, and a purchase in another currency.
  - **Pay changes mid-month:** a raise, a job loss, or a missed paycheque.
- **D-19:** **Custom options. Preset lists stay; users can add up to 10 of each:**
  - **Own item type:** named by the user. Its extra-cost questions are ticked from the question library (insurance, fuel, storage, upkeep, subscriptions, tax, fees and so on), plus up to 3 questions of the user's own. The amounts are always the user's; there are no preset amounts.
  - **Own purpose label**, for example "for the kids". **Emergency use stays limited to "a need" and "for work"**; a custom purpose can never use the emergency fund.
  - **Own payment timing:** every N weeks or every N months, N from 1 to 12, counted exactly ("every 4 weeks" is 13 a year, not 12). It applies to both plans and bills.
  - **Own income type**, for example "child benefit", marked available or kept like other income lines (nothing counts until it is marked).
  - Each custom option can be renamed, edited or deleted. Deleting one never changes past answers or recorded decisions.
- **D-20:** **Edits re-answer saved plans:**
  - Editing or deleting data (a transaction, a bill, unspent money, a line) re-answers saved plans (open checks) at once.
  - If an answer changes, the plan is marked "Changed since you looked" with the reason.
  - Recorded decisions are **never** recalculated (I42; `verdict_at_decision` untouched).
- **D-21:** **Deleting a confirmed regular payment asks "ended or mistake?":**
  - "It ended" keeps past months as they were and drops the payment from now on.
  - "It was never regular" removes it from history and the typical month, and remembers the dismissal so it isn't suggested again.

### Planning clarifications (owner, 2026-10-25, after research)
- **D-22 Unspent money roll-forward (defines I40).** It is the confirmed figure plus only the **unplanned** change since the anchor date, worked out from the whole money picture, never from one balance on its own:
  - **Adds:** money in.
  - **Subtracts:** everyday spending, loan and debt payments, and money put into investments or savings.
  - **Then:** plus or minus other money in or out (refunds, gifts, transfers to other people).

  Expected bills already counted as "still due" do **not** move it.
  - **Sources:** the transactions come from Activity. The saving and investing side comes from Grow, which arrives in Phase 6; until then, savings goals and envelopes stand in.
  - The engine returns the breakdown behind any change (in, everyday, loans, invested/saved, other).
  - It exposes both a `stale` flag (older than 30 days) and a `drifted` flag (more than 10% or $200 from the suggested sum). Screens (05.3) decide when to re-ask.
- **D-23 `legacyState` mapping (C7):**

  | v3 code | legacyState |
  |---|---|
  | `fits_month` | `fits` |
  | `fits_unspent` | `fits` |
  | `monthly_short` | `adjust` |
  | `emergency_use` | `adjust` |
  | `not_yet` | `no` |
  | `needs_input` | `missing` |
- **D-24 Deferred payment plans.** A plan with a deferral (D-04) asks "Does interest build up during the pause?":
  - **Yes:** interest accrues and the payments are worked out on the larger balance.
  - **No:** a true 0% pause.

  A "deferred interest charged back if not paid in time" term is shown as a fact, never assumed.
- **D-25 "It was never regular" (D-21).** Its past payments **become everyday spending**. They stay in the history and the typical-month median, and stop being treated as a fixed bill.
- **D-26 Thresholds in other currencies.** The $200 drift threshold and the $1 quoted-payment threshold use the same fixed per-currency table as the band edges (D-07).
- **D-27 Shadow-run input for the owner's own accounts (D-17).** A development-only export, unavailable in release builds, writes the owner's test-account snapshot to a local file that the shadow test reads. No other user's data is used.
- **D-28 Numbers.** Golden fixture expectations come from `00-REBUILD-PLAN-v3.md` §12 (and the mockup), **not** from `00-SCENARIOS.md`, which still holds v1 numbers.
- **D-29 Median with an even count** averages the two middle months (half-up). This is the rule that reproduces the owner-approved Sam figures: typical month $425, sofa ready in February.

### Plan-check clarifications (owner, 2026-10-26)
- **D-30 Drifted unspent money.** Answers keep using the **user's confirmed figure**. They add the notice `unspent_drifted` ("Your unspent money may be out of date. The app's sum is $X") and cap confidence at rough. The engine never silently replaces the user's figure with the lower one, and drift alone is never `needs_input`. This overrides the planner's min(rolled, suggested) choice.
- **D-31 Bills line in the breakdown.** The unspent breakdown has six lines: **In, Everyday, Bills, Loans, Saved/invested, Other**. Bills are regular bills falling due after the anchor that were not already counted as "still due" at the anchor. They lower unspent money, so it doesn't grow by the rent each month. This extends D-22's list with the owner's acknowledgement.
- **D-32 Rough figures only (no history).** The typical month = rough income − rough spending − rough loans/debt − rough saving/investing. A monthly cost is judged against it, and the answer is marked "from your figures" with confidence rough.
- **D-22 stand-in made explicit.** Until Grow (Phase 6), money moved **into** an envelope or a savings goal after the anchor counts in **Saved/invested** and lowers unspent money; a withdrawal **from** one adds back. Envelope moves are therefore not ignored. This applies the existing D-22 text; nothing is new.

### Gap-closure decisions (owner, 2026-10-26, after the 05.1-32 review and 05.1-VERIFICATION.md)
These override earlier text wherever they conflict. `types.ts` was marked fixed after 05.1-16; the owner explicitly reopens it for the fields named here.

- **D-33 Variable and seasonal income: a longer history.**
  - A user whose pay is variable or seasonal (D-34) uses **up to 12 months** of history.
  - Steady salaried users keep `V3_HISTORY_MONTHS = 6`, so their figures react quickly to real changes such as a raise.
  - Each window is a cited constant.
- **D-34 Who counts as variable or seasonal: the user says, the engine checks.**
  - The income profile gains a user-stated income pattern, `'steady' | 'variable' | 'seasonal'`. This is a new `IncomeProfile` field; setup asks "Does your income change by season?" in 05.2.
  - If the user hasn't said, or said steady, but recorded income swings a lot across the available months, the engine treats them as variable. It then returns `needs_input`, asking the user to confirm their pattern. It never silently relabels them.
  - The swing threshold is a cited constant.
- **D-35 Reconfirm when history can't tell.**
  - For a variable or seasonal user, the engine asks the user to confirm expected income (`needs_input`, with a new missing code) when either is true:
    - (a) there are fewer than 12 months of records;
    - (b) the months the purchase depends on fall in a season with no history.
  - Otherwise it uses the records.
  - The confirmed figure is an engine input (saved in 05.2 and reused across checks, not asked on every check).
  - Confirmed figures are used as given and never guessed.
- **D-36 How a monthly payment is judged for a seasonal user: lean months, then unspent bridges.**
  - **Step 1:** if what's left in the **lean months** covers the payment, the monthly part fits. The lean months are the low season in the 12-month window, a cited rule for picking the low months.
  - **Step 2:** if not, the bridge is checked: shortfall = payment − lean-month leftover; bridge = shortfall × the lean months to carry (D-37). If unspent money (after the purchase's own one-off part and any held-back bridges, D-38) covers the bridge, it fits on unspent. The answer says so: new fact or variant, declarative copy.
  - **Step 3:** otherwise, not yet.
  - For gig and other variable (non-seasonal) users, the monthly part is judged against the low month (second-lowest, as today) instead of the typical month, with the same unspent bridge for the stretch ahead.
- **D-37 Lean months to carry: until the next good season.**
  - Last year's pattern gives the count of lean months between now and when income usually picks up again.
  - Without about a year of records, D-35 applies: ask.
- **D-38 A bridge holds money back.**
  - When a payment plan relies on unspent money to get through the lean months and the user goes ahead, that bridge amount is spoken for, and later checks cannot count the same money.
  - The engine takes held-back bridges as an input and subtracts them from available unspent money. Storing them at "Go ahead" is 05.2.
  - The amount appears in the unspent breakdown as its own line, declarative.
- **D-39 Spending habits are seasonal too.**
  - With 12 months of records, everyday spending for the months a purchase depends on is judged against the **same months last year**, not one average. December looks like last December.
  - With fewer months, the typical figure applies as today.
- **D-40 Per-family I28 gate.**
  - The back-test asserts the false-fit gate for **each family**, not only in aggregate; `byFamily` already exists in `backtest.ts`.
  - Every family's v3 false-fit rate must be ≤ its legacy rate, or ≤ a cited absolute ceiling where legacy rarely says fits.
  - The generator's lean-month pay assumption (60% and 62% of mean income) is replaced by histories that follow D-33 to D-39. Reported figures are never tuned to land inside the band.
- **D-41 'Ended' with no end date = ended as of today.** `isCounting` and `needsInput` use one rule: past payments stay in history, and no future occurrence counts. Supersedes the pinned evaluate.test.ts case.
- **D-42 Partial thinning never helps.** Partially thin history must never improve the code or the confidence compared with full data (I1 for any subset of thin months). The `it.failing` test becomes a passing test.
- **D-43 Drift ignores the emergency fund.**
  - Drift compares the user's unspent figure with the suggested figure with the fund added back.
  - Changing the fund never switches drift or confidence. I31 asserts every figure except the fund's own lines.
- **D-44 Code-review findings are in scope.** 05.1-REVIEW.md H-01 to H-03 and M-01 to M-09 are fixed in this gap closure, each with a regression test, so the shadow re-review covers one final engine. Low findings (L-01 to L-09) are fixed where cheap; the rest are listed for 05.3.
- **D-45 Re-review then sign-off.**
  - After D-33 to D-44: re-run the shadow and the back-test, and regenerate SHADOW-REPORT.md.
  - Draft reasons for any new fits whose answer changed, and get the owner to re-review the changed entries only. This is a blocking human checkpoint.
  - Then set `reviewed: true`, `reviewedBy` and `reviewedAt`, and run the strict shadow gate and the switch guard. DECIDE_V3 stays false.

- `src/engine/recurring/detect.ts`: already groups by normalised name, currency and sign, finds weekly/fortnightly/monthly/quarterly/yearly intervals and applies the ±10% median amount rule. v3's `month/recurring.ts` should extend it, adding the two-fixed-dates pay group, confirm states and drift, not rewrite it.
- `src/engine/recurring/schedule.ts`: the `RecurringFreq` type and day maths (`daysInMonth`). The own-timing "every N weeks or months" (D-19) extends it.
- `src/engine/decide/`, reused per upgrade plan §5.1:

  - `cardRule.ts`, `marketDefaults.ts`, `share.ts`, `currency.ts`, `series.ts`, `ownership.ts`, `payback.ts`;
  - the `wait.ts` logic and `bisect.ts`.
- `src/engine/decide/constants.ts`: the 28/36/12 line defaults with citations (D-11).
- `src/engine/decide/runningCostPrompts.ts`: today's category prompts, the seed of the question library (D-19).
- `src/engine/decide/cashPath.ts`: the 90-day path. S3 must fix it so it never assumes zero spending (B2, C9).

### Established Patterns
- The engine is pure, enforced by depcruise: nothing in `engine/` imports from `db/`, `state/`, `services/`, `ui/` or `react`.
- bigint minor units, with `divideHalfUp` as the only rounding (05 D-21). fast-check property tests; 100% branch coverage on the engine and snapshot is a CI gate.
- Constants carry citations and "FincWin choice" tags (see `constants.ts`).
- Stored check `inputs` JSON gains a version (`v: 2`) with a v1 → v2 upgrade-on-read parser (C6). Stored verdicts map to `legacyState` (`fits / adjust / no / missing`) (C7).

### Integration Points
- `buildSnapshot` / the snapshot layer (S9) feeds the v3 inputs. Home, the check and saved plans read one `Result`.
- The switch: `src/features/decide/availability.ts` (`DECIDE_OPEN`) already gates the UI. The v3 path sits behind one `decideV3` switch read in one place (D-15; per-account in 05.2).
- Data the engine needs but 05.2 stores:
  - income profile, unspent anchor, emergency account and the per-bill typical-month choice (D-03);
  - lines and own lines (D-12), and custom options (D-19);
  - `pay_group`, `counts_as`, `income_kind`, recurring dismissals (D-21).
