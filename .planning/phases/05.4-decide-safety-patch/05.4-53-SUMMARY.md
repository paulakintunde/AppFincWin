# Phase 05.4 Plan 53: D-68 short plans judged by cash Summary

**A plan whose every payment lands inside the 90 days, with no running cost and a monthly part that does not fit, is now judged as a cash buy of its whole cost when money on hand covers that cost on the dated cash path, without the overdraft or any safety-net money. The answer carries one declarative caution line.**

## How the rule works, in plain words

Before D-68, a plan whose monthly payment was more than the usual month leaves was always "Short each month" (the QE-12 rule). That held even when every payment was due within three months and the person had the money now.

Now the engine asks five questions. All five must be yes:

1. Is it a plan, with no running monthly cost?
2. Is every payment dated before day 90 (days 0..89, the same days the cash path reads)?
3. Does the monthly part fail (Short each month, or Not yet for a lean-month earner)? A monthly part that already fits is left alone.
4. Is the usual month known?
5. Is the whole cost (deposit, fees and every payment once, my share; the same figure as the interest line's total) a plain cash fit? That means this month's room, spare money and extra savings above the net's size cover it, and the 90-day path with every payment on its own date never goes below $0. No overdraft and no safety-net money count, whether the net is locked or not.

When all five hold:
- The answer is the cash one-off's: Fits this month, or Fits from spare money / Fits from extra savings. The split sums to the whole cost.
- `parts` still records the monthly code.
- The line "This plan's {{monthly}} a month is more than your usual month can cover, so it is paid from money on hand." comes first, or second after a D-65 tier line.
- plan_makes_short and depletion_payment are left out.
- An already-short usual month gets the D-65 tier a cash buy of the same total would get (refinement a).
- When any answer is no, nothing in the judge changes.

The ladder's cash check is the prepared dated path (`JudgeInput.cash`, built in prepare.ts from `purchaseCashFlows`). `oneOffPart` reads that path and never rebuilds one from the price, so nothing had to be passed through.
