## 1. Plain summary (grade-10 English)

**What the owner asked for:** a disclaimer with every Decide answer. It should say that some situations, simple or complex, may not be read right; that the final decision is the user's; that "we do not accept any liability due to the use or not use"; and that the tool gives an idea and works for most people but not everyone.

**What the research says:**

1. **A blanket "we accept no liability" line will not hold up, and it can backfire.** In the UK, the EU and Quebec, the law decides what a business can and cannot escape. A term or on-screen notice that tries to escape everything is "unfair". An unfair term does not bind the user (UK Consumer Rights Act 2015 s.62; EU Directive 93/13; Quebec Consumer Protection Act s.10). The UK competition regulator's July 2026 guidance warns against broad wording such as "as far as the law permits". Since April 2025 that regulator can fine up to 10% of turnover. In the US, the consumer-protection regulator (the FTC) judges the overall impression, so fine print cannot fix a misleading main message. On a screen meant to give a "trustworthy answer", a no-liability line also reads as "don't trust this", which works against the app's core value.
2. **What does protect FincWin is mostly how the answer is presented, not a legal sentence.** Claims against the app are weakest when three things are true. The app shows information worked out from the user's own figures and does not tell them what to do. It clearly states what it used and what it cannot see. The user can look back at what they entered and what they saw. The legal sentence belongs in the Terms of Use, written as a fair, limited clause that a lawyer has checked.
3. **People skip general disclaimers and notice specific ones.** Studies find that boilerplate warnings change little, or even confuse people. Short, specific warnings tied to the situation do change what people do. So the design is:
   - **one short general line** under every answer;
   - **one specific line** when the app can detect a reason to be careful (quick estimates, old records, no debts recorded, irregular income, shared money, a big purchase);
   - **an "About this answer" sheet** on tap, listing what the answer used and what it cannot see.
4. **The words "not financial advice" do not change what the app legally is.** Regulators look at what the app does, not at its labels. The UK and US tests ask whether the output works like a recommendation for that person. Keeping every answer as a statement of fact matters more than any label. The label still helps App Review and users, so use it once, in the About sheet.
5. **"Works for most but not all" is a claim that has to be backed up.** A claim about how often the app is right needs evidence (UK and US rules on misleading claims). Describe what the app covers instead of claiming a success rate: "Made for everyday situations, not every one."

**Recommended wording, in short:**

- **Under every answer:** "Worked out only from what's recorded here. The choice is yours." Then a link: **What this can't see**.
- **The footer stays:** "FincWin is a planning tool, not a lender."
- **On tap:** an "About this answer" sheet. It opens with what was detected for *this* answer, then the general list of what any answer cannot see (sections 6b and 6c).
- **The liability sentence:** goes only in the Terms of Use, in fair form, after a lawyer's review (section 8).

**Four owner decisions** are in section 10.

## 3. Law: can a consumer app exclude liability for its outputs?

### 3.1 United Kingdom

| Rule | What it says | What it means for FincWin |
|---|---|---|
| CRA 2015 s.62 | "An unfair term of a consumer contract is not binding on the consumer." "An unfair consumer notice is not binding on the consumer." A term is unfair if, against good faith, it causes "a significant imbalance … to the detriment of the consumer." [VERIFIED: legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/62] | The test covers **notices**, so an on-screen disclaimer is tested too, not only the Terms |
| CRA s.65 | Liability for "death or personal injury resulting from negligence" can never be excluded by a term or a notice. Agreeing to a notice does not mean the person accepted the risk (s.65(2)) [VERIFIED: legislation.gov.uk/.../section/65] | Rarely relevant to a money app, but every UK limitation clause must carve it out. Showing someone a disclaimer is not consent |
| CRA s.57 (services) | A term excluding s.49 (reasonable care and skill) does not bind. A restriction cannot stop recovery of the price paid [VERIFIED: legislation.gov.uk/.../section/57] | Once a user pays for Pro, FincWin owes reasonable care and skill in how Decide is built, and that duty cannot be excluded. Plan to meet it (the right-answer table, Stryker, coverage gate), not to disclaim it |
| CRA s.33 (digital content) | Chapter 3 applies to content supplied for a price, or supplied free with paid items that are not generally available otherwise [VERIFIED: legislation.gov.uk/.../section/33] | Free-tier Decide may sit outside the statutory digital-content rights. Pro does not. Negligence and the s.62 fairness test still apply to everyone |
| CRA Sch 2 Part 1 ("grey list") | Para 1: excluding liability for death or injury. Para 2: "inappropriately excluding or limiting the legal rights of the consumer". Para 20: hindering legal action [VERIFIED: legislation.gov.uk/.../schedule/2] | "We do not accept any liability" is the textbook para-2 term |
| CMA37 guidance, updated 22 July 2026 after a January 2026 consultation | Exclusion clauses "should be narrowly drafted and come with clear qualifications". Broad wording such as "as far as the law permits" may be unfair. Notices are tested like terms. An unfair term cannot be rewritten after the event [VERIFIED: gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-contract-terms-cma37; CITED: Lewis Silkin summary of the draft, Feb 2026] | Rules out "to the fullest extent permitted by law, we accept no liability" as the main clause. The lawyer should check against the final July 2026 PDF |
| DMCC Act 2024 | The CMA enforces consumer law directly, with fines up to 10% of global turnover, from 6 April 2025 [VERIFIED: multiple law-firm sources, e.g. cms.law, cooley.com] | Unfair terms and misleading claims are now directly finable. "Works for most" needs evidence |
| Negligent misstatement for pure economic loss (*Hedley Byrne v Heller* [1964]) | Liability needs an "assumption of responsibility". In that case a disclaimer defeated it [ASSUMED: from training; the case is standard law] | This is why the presentation matters more than a liability line. An app that shows information worked out from the user's own figures, names its limits and leaves the decision to the user is less likely to be found to have assumed responsibility. A disclaimer alone is now subject to the s.62 fairness test |

**FCA boundary (regulated advice).** PERG 8.28: giving information "without making any comment or value judgment on its relevance to decisions" is not advice. Information can still "take on the nature of advice if the circumstances … give it the force of a recommendation". Generic advice such as "financial planning" is outside art. 53 when it does not concern a particular investment [CITED: handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/8/28.html, via search excerpts]. PERG 17 (debt counselling, RAO art. 39E): advice about a particular debt and debtor is regulated. Budgeting advice given "in connection with liquidating" credit debts can be caught (PERG 17.3) [CITED: handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/17]. **Consequence:** D-46, D-54 and D-63 lines about debts ("clearing what you owe first lowers the cost") must stay as facts and must never become "pay off card X first". The FCA's targeted-support regime (from 6 April 2026) needs FCA permission and covers investment and pension suggestions to groups of consumers [CITED: brodies.com, freshfields.com]. FincWin is not in it and should not drift towards "people like you usually…" copy.

### 3.2 European Union

- **Unfair Contract Terms Directive 93/13/EEC.** Art. 3(1) uses the same "significant imbalance, contrary to good faith" test. Art. 5 requires plain, intelligible language, with doubt read in the consumer's favour. Art. 6(1) says unfair terms do not bind. Annex 1(b) lists terms "inappropriately excluding or limiting the legal rights of the consumer" [ASSUMED: wording from training; the EUR-Lex fetch returned empty. The UK Sch 2 is the near-verbatim UK version and was verified].
- **Digital Content Directive (EU) 2019/770.** It covers content supplied for a price **or for personal data** (Art. 3(1)), and its rights cannot be contracted out of (Art. 22) [ASSUMED: from training; fetch failed]. Unlike the UK, this can reach the free tier, because the user gives personal data.
- **Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC.** Misleading actions and omissions are judged by the overall impression on the average consumer, and on vulnerable groups where the practice targets them [ASSUMED].
- **Digital Services Act.** FincWin is not an intermediary service in the DSA sense, because no user content is published to others. It is largely not applicable [ASSUMED; LOW]. The household sharing feature does not make it a hosting platform for the public.
- **New Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853.** It brings software into scope, but compensable damage is death or injury, property and data loss, not pure financial loss [ASSUMED; MEDIUM].
- **AI Act.** Decide is a deterministic rules engine, not an AI system as defined, and it is not credit scoring of a person by a lender. Out of scope [ASSUMED; LOW-MEDIUM; flag for the lawyer only if any ML is added].

### 3.3 United States

- **FTC Act s.5 (deception).** The FTC's 1983 Deception Policy Statement judges the "net impression". Fine print and later disclaimers generally do not cure a misleading main claim [ASSUMED: well-established; the PDF fetch was unreadable]. Practical rule: the headline ("Fits") must not promise more than the caveat allows. CMP-F12 already enforces this ("never more confident than the answer").
- **CFPA, 12 U.S.C. 5481(15)(A)(viii).** A "covered person" includes one "providing financial advisory services … to consumers on individual financial matters". There is an exclusion for publications whose "information or recommendations … are not tailored to the individual needs of a particular consumer" [VERIFIED: law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/5481]. Decide is tailored, so the "not financial advice" label does not by itself place FincWin outside CFPB unfair, deceptive or abusive practices (UDAAP) jurisdiction. Whether a budgeting tool is "financial advisory services" is a **lawyer question** [LOW confidence on the outcome]. Either way it argues for clean, non-deceptive copy, not a bigger disclaimer.
- **Investment Advisers Act s.202(a)(11).** This covers advice about securities. Decide does not name or rank securities, and CMP-F17 forbids investment forecasts. Out of scope as long as that holds [ASSUMED; MEDIUM].
- **State law on limitation clauses.** These are generally enforceable in clickwrap Terms if conspicuous and not unconscionable. Many states refuse to enforce exclusion of liability for fraud, wilful injury or violation of law. California Civil Code s.1668 is the usual example [ASSUMED: wording from training; leginfo returned 403]. New Jersey's TCCWNA penalises consumer terms that violate clearly established rights. Vague "where permitted by law" savings clauses have been litigated there [ASSUMED; LOW-MEDIUM]. Clear assent at sign-up matters for enforceability (US courts examine the sign-up screen design) [ASSUMED].
- **US norm in this category:** a cap of "the greater of $100 or what you paid in the last 12 months" (Monarch), fees paid in the last 12 months (YNAB), or $500 (Rocket Money) [VERIFIED: their live Terms, section 4.2].

### 3.4 Canada

- **Quebec CPA s.10:** "Any stipulation whereby a merchant is liberated from the consequences of his own act or the act of his representative is prohibited." [VERIFIED: legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca P-40.1, via search excerpt]. A general liability exclusion is void against Quebec consumers. Quebec also has French-language rules for adhesion contracts (Charter of the French Language as amended by Bill 96) [ASSUMED; the lawyer must confirm the French-first Terms requirement].
- **Ontario.** Under the CPA 2002, rights apply despite any waiver (s.7) [ASSUMED]. The CPA 2023 received Royal Assent on 6 Dec 2023. I could not confirm whether it has been proclaimed in force [VERIFIED: status pending as of late-2024 sources; current status UNKNOWN].
- Other provinces have similar no-waiver rules [ASSUMED; LOW].

### 3.5 What is unenforceable or counterproductive

| Wording | Problem | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| "We do not accept any liability due to the use or not use of this app" | Unfair and not binding in the UK, EU and Quebec (s.62, Sch 2 para 2, 93/13 Annex 1(b), Quebec s.10). Grammatically unclear, and unclear terms are read against the trader (CRA s.69, 93/13 Art. 5). On the answer screen it says "don't trust this", which contradicts the Core Value | Terms only: a fair clause, foreseeable-loss model in the UK and EU, capped in the US (section 8). On screen: "The choice is yours." |
| "To the fullest extent permitted by law…" as the main clause | Named as possibly unfair by CMA37 (2026). Risky under NJ TCCWNA | State the actual limits, and list what is never limited |
| "FincWin is not responsible for any errors" | Excludes the s.49 care-and-skill duty for Pro users (s.57 makes that not binding). Invites the response "then why should I pay?" | "Answers depend on the figures you enter. If our own calculation is wrong, our responsibility is set out in [Terms]." |

| "Not financial advice" placed right next to a line that works as a recommendation | The label does not change what the content is (PERG 8.28 "force of a recommendation"; FTC net impression) | Keep every line a fact. Use the label once, in the About sheet |
| "You agree that you use Decide at your own risk" checkbox before each answer | Not consent under s.65(2). Training people to tick boxes is the opposite of informed use | A one-time "How Decide answers" card with Got it (section 7.3) |

## 5. Over-reliance and automation bias: what works

| Finding | Source | Confidence | Design implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mandated general disclaimers increased confusion in all 18 experiments reviewed, and were ineffective or harmful for decisions in 15 | Green & Armstrong (2012), "Evidence on the effects of mandatory disclaimers in advertising" | MEDIUM [CITED: digital.library.adelaide.edu.au, via search] | Do not rely on a long, general line |
| Current MLM earnings disclaimers had no effect. Specific, prominent disclaimers did change judgments | Bosley et al., *Journal of Public Policy & Marketing* (2026) | MEDIUM [CITED: via search] | A specific line tied to the situation ("no debts are recorded") does work |
| Cognitive forcing (making people think before accepting) reduced over-reliance more than simple explanations did, but users liked it least. It helped high "need for cognition" users most | Buçinca, Malaya & Gajos, CSCW 2021 | HIGH [CITED: arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692] | Use light forcing only where the stakes are high (a big purchase), not on every answer |
| Explanations reduce over-reliance when they make the output easier to check than to accept blindly | Vasconcelos et al. 2023, "Explanations Can Reduce Overreliance on AI Systems During Decision-Making" | HIGH [CITED: arxiv.org/abs/2212.06823] | Show the few figures the answer rests on, so checking them is quick |
| Explanations sometimes did not reduce automation bias, or increased it | Vered et al. 2023 | LOW-MEDIUM [CITED: via search] | Long "how it works" text is not a cure. Keep it short and checkable |
| Most users skip Terms and privacy policies | Obar & Oeldorf-Hirsch (2020), "The biggest lie on the internet" | [ASSUMED: from training] | The Terms protect the legal position. They do not inform users. Informing users happens on the screen |
| Misuse (over-reliance) and disuse (ignoring useful automation) are both failures | Parasuraman & Riley (1997) | [ASSUMED] | Too much warning text pushes people to ignore true "Not yet" answers. Keep the line count low |

**Design rules that follow, all within the no-advice, declarative voice:**
1. **Specific beats general.** A detected caveat ("This uses your quick estimates") outranks the general one.
2. **Show the basis in numbers.** The CMP-F12 basis sentence already does this ("Based on 4 months of your records, up to Oct 29").
3. **Make checking cheap on big purchases.** List the 3 or 4 figures the answer rests on. State that a wrong figure changes the answer. Do not use imperatives ("Check…"), because the copy guard's IMPERATIVE_LEAD rule applies.
4. **Cap the warning load.** At most one contextual line on the answer itself. Everything else goes in the About sheet.
5. **Leave a record the user can see.** What they entered, what the answer said and when. That serves the user and is also the best evidence if blame is ever disputed (section 7.4).
6. **No false precision.** Round where the engine allows. Never "exactly", "sure", "certain" or "accurate" (the guard already bans these).

### 7.2 Why not more on the answer itself
The prototype's restraint and the Core Value both argue for short. The research (section 5) says one specific line plus a short general line does more than a paragraph. The About sheet carries everything else.

### 7.3 Onboarding card text (draft)
- Title: "How Decide answers"
- "Decide works from what you record in FincWin. It can't see what isn't recorded."
- "Missing or wrong figures can make an answer look better or worse than it is."
- "It gives you the facts. The decision is yours."
- Button: "Got it". Store `decide_intro_ack_at` and `decide_intro_version`.

This is an informed-use step, not a liability waiver. Under s.65(2), a ticked box does not make the risk the user's anyway. Its value is that the user saw the limits before the first answer.

### 7.4 The record (dealing with blame)
`decide_checks` already stores `inputs`, `verdict_at_decision` and `decided_at`. It does not store which engine build answered, the answer variant, the reason codes shown or the basis date. **Proposal:** at "Record as bought / waiting / passed", also store `answer_shown` (jsonb: verdict code, variant, reason and caveat codes, basis months and date, engine build), shown back to the user as "What Decide said on {{date}}". This is the user's own record. It follows CMP-F06 purge-after-deletion and account deletion. **Do not** keep copies after deletion "for defence". GDPR Art. 17(3)(e) (legal claims) exists, but using it here would undercut the "delete really deletes" promise [ASSUMED: legal reading; lawyer to confirm]. Analytics must not carry these codes linked to amounts or the person (CMP-F03).

## 10. Owner decisions (plain English, recommended option first)

**1. Where does the "we accept no liability" sentence go?**
- **Recommended: only in the Terms of Use, rewritten by a lawyer in a fair form.** On the answer screen we say "The choice is yours." A blanket no-liability line is not legally binding in the UK, the EU or Quebec. It can be treated as an unfair notice. And on the screen it tells people not to trust the answer.
- Other option: keep a short "FincWin isn't responsible for decisions you make" line on screen. Not recommended for the reasons above.

**2. Which one-line caveat goes under every answer?**
- **Recommended: "Worked out only from what's recorded here. The choice is yours."** followed by "What this can't see".
- Other options: "Built from your figures. Missing or wrong figures change the answer." or "Made for everyday situations, not every one. The choice is yours."

**3. May the About sheet say "not financial advice"?**
Our word guard bans "advice" in Decide text. Regulators and App Review recognise the phrase, but the label does not change what the app legally is.
- **Recommended: yes, once, in the About sheet only.** Text: "This is information to help you plan, not financial advice." It sits under the legal keys with a single named exception in the guard, like the existing "not tax advice" exception.
- Other option: avoid the word with "It gives you the facts. It doesn't tell you what to do."

**4. Big purchases: add a figure check and keep a record?**
- **Recommended: yes to both.**
  - (a) When the price or total cost is at least one usual month's money in, or it is borrowed, the answer shows the 3 or 4 figures it rests on, with "A wrong figure here changes the answer."
  - (b) When a check is marked bought, waiting or passed, the app saves "What Decide said on {date}" in the user's own check history. It is deleted with the account.
- Choices inside this decision: the threshold (one month's money in is the suggestion), and whether (b) lands in 05.5 or 08.1.

## 12. Common pitfalls

1. **Caveat and basis sentence say the same thing twice.** The contextual line must come from the same sorted reasons as the basis sentence (CMP-F12), not a second list. Warning sign: "Based on your quick estimates" directly above "This uses your quick estimates".
2. **The caveat becomes a hidden imperative.** "Check your figures before buying" fails IMPERATIVE_LEAD and reads as advice. Use "The answer rests on these figures" and "A wrong figure here changes the answer."
3. **A success-rate claim sneaks in.** "Works for most people" needs evidence. Keep the scope framing.
4. **The disclaimer outshouts the answer.** If the caveat is visually louder than the verdict, people learn to ignore both (disuse). Use the footer text style from the §2 tokens, with no new colours.
5. **The label replaces the substance.** Debt lines drifting into "pay X first" become regulated debt counselling in the UK (PERG 17.3), whatever the disclaimer says.
6. **The record is kept after deletion "for defence".** This conflicts with CMP-F06 and the owner's purge promise.
7. **The free-help pointer goes stale.** It is already gated by the 90-day check (05.4-38). The About sheet must read from the same table.
