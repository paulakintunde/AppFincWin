## 1. Summary in plain words

**What stays.** Most of the engine already does what the four analyses ask for. It already runs a day-by-day cash path for 90 days and finds the lowest point. It puts bills and card payments on their real dates and skips bills that are already paid. Pay only counts once it is seen. It does not trust "yes, I can cover it" over the records, and it already has a "low months are tight" caution `[VERIFIED: code]`. So "monthly surplus is not spendable money" is already handled. The cases just need to say how they spread everyday spending: evenly over the days left in the month.

**What changes.** Four things, all from the owner's review:

1. **Safety net.** If nothing is saved, the app no longer says "Not yet". A purchase that fits your money gets the amber answer **"Fits, but no safety net"**. The line under it says 1 month of essential costs is a start and 3 to 6 months is a common goal. Money already in the safety net stays protected. Using it is still "Dips into your safety net". We also recommend **no longer holding back the part of the safety net you have not saved yet**. If we kept doing that, someone with $1 saved would get a worse answer than someone with $0.
2. **Thin margins.** "Fits, but tight" now applies to one-off purchases too, when the money left this month is under a tenth of what you usually spend in a month. This fixes QE-08 ($25 left), QE-01 (£50 left on quick estimates), DB-02 ($100 left) and DB-07 ($300 left).
3. **Overdraft.** Going below $0 inside the limit is "Uses your overdraft", even when the next pay does not bring the account back above $0. In that case the answer also says, as facts, how long the account stays below $0, what that costs and which date keeps it above $0. A new line names repeat use, for example: "Without this purchase, your account still goes below $0 before each of your next 3 paydays." The cost estimate now adds up each day below $0 instead of assuming the deepest day lasts the whole time. That makes it smaller and closer to what a bank charges.
4. **Debts.** DB-09 should not say "Only by borrowing", because Sam has the cash. It becomes **"Fits, but borrowing costs more"**. DB-04 (holiday paid in cash while carrying £6,000 at 24.9%) needs an owner decision. The words can stay as facts only, which keeps D-08. Or the answer can turn amber when money saved from earlier months goes on a want while costly card debt remains. We lean to amber, as a narrow rule.

**Need or want.** The engine already has a "what is it for" field: necessity, for work, nice to have, treat, luxury, invest in me `[VERIFIED: code]`. We recommend an optional "Need or want?" tag that changes the wording and the order of the options, but **never the answer or the maths**. The screens come in 08.1. The engine facts come now.

**Size.** We propose retiring 4 cases and adding 10, which gives 97 cases in total (95 if the need or want pair is left out). About 45 cases in other topics have no safety net in their test data. They should be given a full safety net, so each case still tests only its own rule. This changes the test data, not the answers.

## 4. Essential versus discretionary (need or want)

**What exists.** `CheckV2.purpose` already has six presets: `invest_in_me`, `for_work`, `necessity`, `nice_to_have`, `treat` and `luxury`. There is also an `emergency` flag. Today the only effect: a check marked emergency with purpose necessity or for work may use the emergency fund (`emergency_use`, I38) `[VERIFIED: code]`. The purpose is stored in the check's JSON, so **a tag needs no new column** `[VERIFIED: code, adapters/inputsV2.ts]`. No screen on main asks for it today.

**What comparable tools do.** Budgeting apps and guides group spending by category into needs and wants. Examples are the 50/30/20 split and YNAB's categories. YNAB also frames spending on wants without guilt. The CFPB teaches "needs versus wants" in its youth materials `[CITED: consumerfinance.gov Building Blocks "reflecting needs versus wants"]`. We found **no mainstream app that changes a "can I afford it" answer per purchase based on need or want** (sources are mostly comparison blogs, so LOW confidence) `[CITED-2nd]`.

**Risks of judging the user.**
- A category default misfires: work boots filed as "clothing = want", or a laptop that is a work tool. That reads as the app judging the person `[ASSUMED]`.
- If "need" made answers kinder, people would learn to tap "need" to get a green answer. The tag would then corrupt the maths.
- Copy such as "this is a want" can shame people. CLAUDE.md's declarative voice forbids "you should".
- The owner's examples show real differences. A parking fine is an obligation: waiting usually adds a late fee, so "Not yet" is the least useful answer. A TV or a bag can usually wait at no cost.

**Recommendation (v1).**
- **05.4 (engine only):** the purpose maps to a need/want reading. `necessity` and `for_work` mean need. `nice_to_have`, `treat` and `luxury` mean want. `invest_in_me` or nothing chosen means unknown (no change). The tag **changes facts and their order only**, never the code, the variant or any number. A new invariant locks this: "need/want never changes the code or the figures".
  - Need + Not yet: the earliest way through leads, stated as facts. For example: "Marked as a need. It fits on 27 Nov. Using $220 of your safety net covers it today." For a fine: "Waiting may add a late fee." This is a fact with no amount, shown only when the user ticks "a fine or bill".
  - Need + Dips into your safety net: "A safety net is money kept for needs like this."
  - Want + any caution: the "later or cheaper" fact leads: "Waiting until 1 Dec keeps your safety net whole."
- **08.1 (screens):** one optional question, "Is this a need or a want?", with three equal choices (Need / Want / Skip), never pre-selected (UX-10 equal choices).
- **v1.1:** category defaults (for example fuel = need), only if the reading test shows people want them.
- **Reject:** need/want changing the code or the maths.

**Compliant wording examples.** "Marked as a need." / "A safety net is money kept for needs like this." / "Waiting until 1 Dec keeps your safety net whole." / "It fits on 27 Nov."

## 5. Per-case decisions (43 cases) and new cases

**Shared rules used below (recommended options from section 7):**
- **R1, safety net:** only money already saved in the net is protected. The unsaved part is not held back. A purchase that fits spare money while the net is below its size gets `net_not_full`: "Fits, but no safety net" when nothing is saved, or "Fits, but safety net not full" when part is saved. When protection is off or the size is $0, nothing changes (neutral, per D-06).
- **R2, one-off tight:** `fits_month`/`fits_unspent` gets the `tight` variant ("Fits, but tight") when the money left after buying is under the floor. The floor is the user's line, or a tenth of a usual month's bills plus everyday spending, rounded up (plan 21's `tightFloor`). The 80% share test applies to monthly payments only.
- **Variant precedence on a fit:** `low_month` / `tight` > `costly_debt` > `net_not_full`. A variant that loses still shows as a fact (for example `net_none_start`).
- **Test data:** a fixture with `safetyNet: null` means "nothing saved, protection on, starter size", the real default (decide_settings defaults to protect = true, size = null). This changes SN-01's old meaning.

## 7. Owner decisions still open (plain words, with a recommended option)

**Decision 1: The safety net when it is empty or only part full.**
- **A (recommended).** Only money already in your safety net is protected. The part you have not saved yet is not held back from your spare money. While the net is below its size, a purchase that fits shows amber: "Fits, but no safety net" or "Fits, but safety net not full". The no-net line says "1 month is a start; 3 to 6 months is a common goal."
- B. Amber only when nothing at all is saved. A part-full net gets a plain "Fits" with a progress line.
- C. Keep holding back the unsaved part for part-full nets. *Not recommended:* someone with $1 saved would get a worse answer than someone with $0.
- Also: the money guides we found all say "3 to 6 months", not "2 to 6". **Recommended: use "3 to 6".**

**Decision 2: What counts as leaning on the overdraft "month after month".**
- **A (recommended).** Now: the answer adds a repeat-use line when, even without this purchase, the account goes below $0 before at least 2 of the next 3 paydays. Later (05.5): also look back at the last 3 months.
- B. Wait for 05.5 and look back only.
- C. Any time the account is below $0 today.
- (Already decided by you: inside the limit = "Uses your overdraft", even past payday, with the cost and the waiting date stated; past the limit = "Not yet"; no limit set = say so and ask.)

**Decision 3: Debts.**
- (i) DB-09, when you have the cash but choose a costly plan. **A (recommended):** "Fits, but borrowing costs more". B: keep "Only by borrowing".
