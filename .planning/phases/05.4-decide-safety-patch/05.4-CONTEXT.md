- **Borrowed figures show their source.** For example "From Grow · Visa card", "From Activity · …", "From your Everyday account (checking)". A missing figure is named and linked to its owner. Decide never asks for it inside Decide.
- **The confidence sentence states the level** in plain words: "Based on 2 of the 3 months of records Decide looks for." There is no `ConfidenceLevel` type and no Rough, Fair or Firm. "Sure" is never used alone (CMP-F12).
- **Copy rule.** Declarative, never prescriptive. "Suggestion" is banned (CMP-F25).

### Right-answer table review
- **D-01:** The table is a **published web page**: each case is a short card (situation, expected answer, why), grouped by topic. Approval is asked in chat popups, one topic at a time.
- **D-02:** It is approved in **about 8 topic groups of about 9 cases**: safety net, overdraft, debts, borrowing, tax, running costs, selling the old one, quick estimates. Engine work on a topic may start as soon as its group is approved. QA-02's "table before engine change" rule applies per topic.
- **D-03:** On disagreement, the financial expert writes one short paragraph of reasoning. Then **the owner's answer is final**, and the reason is recorded with the case.
- **D-04:** Cases are about **invented people** first: the 16 audit personas plus new ones. Later, surprises from tester shadow runs become cases, with names and amounts changed. The owner's own records are not used.

### Open money rules (owner questions 4 and 5, answered)
- **D-05:** The safety-net starter stays about 1 month of essential costs. **Anyone can type any size, including very small. There is no one-time quarter or half "smaller starter" offer.** This answers question 4 differently from the fix plan's recommendation. Mockup board C49 must change to match (see Deferred).
- **D-06:** **A $0 safety net is treated exactly like protection off**, and the answer says so plainly.
- **D-07:** **Expensive borrowing caution.** When cash would fit but a loan or plan is chosen, add a caution if the APR is **20% or more**, OR the total interest is **10% or more of the amount borrowed**. The interest is shown next to the cash route. These are FincWin's thresholds, not law (question 5).
- **D-08:** **Debt and housing fact lines are on by default**, and can be switched off in settings. They are facts only and never change the answer (question 5).

### Shadow runs and privacy
- **D-09:** **Each tester is asked first**, with a plain-words yes or no (in-app or written). Anyone who says no is excluded.
- **D-10:** The owner sees, per tester (labelled Tester 1, 2, 3 and so on), **only the answer, its reasons and rounded figures**. No names, no transactions, no account names.
- **D-11:** The shadow check **runs on the tester's phone**. Only the rounded result is sent back. No server code reads testers' records for this.
- **D-12:** **One fixed set of 6 to 8 sample purchases** is checked for every tester. For example: a $60 item, a $500 phone, a $2,000 laptop, and a $9,000 car with cash and with installments.
- **D-13:** It runs **once each time a new engine version reaches testers' phones**.

### Owner sign-off load
- **D-14:** **Changed expected answers:** one page per rule fix lists every changed answer, before and after, with the reason. The owner approves the whole group in one popup, or picks out the ones they disagree with. Every change is shown, not only the looser ones. This is the 05.2 reviewed-diff rule.
- **D-15:** **Changed wording:** one review page near the end of 05.4, after the copy guard has run. One sign-off. This covers the engine's verdict table and reason codes; screen copy is 08.1.
- **D-16:** **Shadow results:** a short summary page per run, with the count of each answer and only the surprises in detail. The owner acts only on surprises, by turning them into right-answer cases.
- **D-17:** **Rhythm:** approvals come **as soon as each piece is ready**. Each carries a progress percentage and what comes next.

### Launch regions and tax
- **D-18:** Tax and currency defaults cover the **United States, Canada, United Kingdom and European Union** at launch. This sets defaults only. Legal launch countries remain owner question 12, for the lawyer.
- **D-19:** **US and Canada:** ask once for the state or province, pre-fill its usual combined rate, and label it an estimate the user can change. **UK and EU:** "price already includes tax" is on by default.
- **D-20:** **Unknown region:** add no tax, and say so plainly ("Tax not included. Set your region to add it"), with a link to settings. Region precedence follows the existing rule: the user's own setting, then the device region, then the time zone. Never IP.
- **D-21:** Example loan and mortgage rates are labelled as examples and never stored as the user's rate (ARC-25, 05.1 D-07).

### "Left this month" meaning (05.3 handoff item 1)
- **D-22:** For a one-off purchase, the big "after you buy" figure is **this month after its plans**: expected pay, bills and usual spending counted, minus the purchase. For example: "November would still leave about $25." Decide Home shows the same figure before the purchase. Monthly payments keep leading with the usual month (carried forward).
- **D-23:** **Expected pay later this month counts on its date.** The 90-day path still checks that bills due before payday can be paid.
- **D-24:** **A test pins Home and the answer together.** For the same month they differ by exactly the purchase, plus any stated reason such as a first-payment date. Otherwise the build fails. The full shared month picture lands in 05.5. 05.4 adds the pinning test and fixes the basis.
- **D-25:** **05.3 handoff item 2:** with no safety net set, no answer claims a safety net is "untouched".

### Overdraft answer detail
- **D-26:** "Uses your overdraft" shows **how far below $0, for about how many days (until payday), and roughly what it costs** at the account's rate, labelled an estimate. For example: "Your Everyday account would go $627 below $0 for about 9 days, until payday. At 22%, that costs about $3."
- **D-27:** **Going past the overdraft limit is "Not yet"**, never a fit of any kind.
- **D-28:** **If no overdraft limit is set, it is treated as $0**, so going below $0 is "Not yet". The answer says no overdraft limit is set and links to the account's settings.

### Tester list and switch
- **D-29:** The named tester list holds **the owner's accounts plus each tester the owner names**. Demo and App Store reviewer accounts are never on it.
- **D-30:** The list is **a file in the repo, holding account IDs, not names**. Every change goes through a PR the owner approves. The release check (BO-06) fails if any account outside this list has `profiles.decide_v3` on.

### Right-answer review round 1 (owner, 2026-11-02; inputs 05.4-OWNER-REVIEW-1.md, research 05.4-RIGHT-ANSWERS-RESEARCH.md)
- **D-35 (replaces FIN-01's starter hold-back and the 'whole starter held back' reading of D-05):** the safety net protects **only money already saved in it**. While the net is below its set size, or there is no net, a purchase that fits the available money gets an **amber caution** (e.g. "Fits, but your safety net is small" / "no safety net"), never "Not yet" for that reason alone. The answer carries the line "1 month is a start; 3 to 6 months is common." A purchase that would use money in the net still says "Dips into your safety net". This is an owner override of fix-plan Theme G (the months line may appear inside the no-net/small-net answer).
- **D-36 (refines D-26/D-27):** below $0 but inside the overdraft limit = "Uses your overdraft". New variant `not_cleared` with a strong warning when the next pay will not bring the account back above $0. Past the limit stays "Not yet" (D-27). Overdraft interest is the sum of each day's balance below $0 times the daily rate (not the deepest day for the whole period).
- **D-37:** repeat-use warning when the account would be below $0 before **2 of the next 3 paydays**, computed from the existing cash path (nothing new stored). A look-back at past months is added in 05.5.
- **D-38 (refines D-28):** below $0 with **no overdraft limit recorded**: the answer says so and asks whether the account has an overdraft; any cost-saving line is a fact (e.g. "Clearing what you owe first lowers the cost").
- **D-39 (debts; changes D-07 and D-08):** (a) when cash fits but a plan is chosen, the answer is "Fits, but borrowing costs more" with the total interest; (b) **amber** when money saved from earlier months is spent while a debt at **20% APR or more** remains, with the debt's monthly interest shown as a fact; (c) **mortgages skip the 10%-of-amount interest test** (still costed and shown).
- **D-40 (tight, extends FIN-07):** "tight" also applies to **one-off** purchases: money left after the purchase under **a tenth of a usual month's bills plus everyday spending**. QE-12 stays "Short each month" and states how many of the payments savings would cover.
- **D-41 (need or want):** an **optional** need/want tag (using the existing check `purpose` field) changes **wording and the order of options only, never the answer or the sums**. Screens in 08.1.
- All app wording stays declarative: no "advice", "recommend" or "you should", even where the owner's notes use those words.

A fit on thin data names what was not checked.
- **D-44 (earlier months):** a case or answer that relies on money left from earlier months states where it went (saved, still in the account, or spent).
- **D-45 (planner item; verify before building):** recurring expenses and income roll over into a new month as its base, and stay **Expected** (app wording) until confirmed paid or received.
- **D-46 (income shortfall runway):** when a usual month is short and savings cover the payments, the answer states how many payments savings cover, how a further shortfall (e.g. another 1,000 a month) shortens that, and, as a fact, that raising income or lowering costs first makes the plan last (declarative; never "you should").

### Right-answer review round 2 (owner, 2026-11-02; quick estimates + topics 5-8)

- **D-47 (replaces FIN-11 'hold back the built-up share'):** for yearly or quarterly bills, hold back **only the part the months before the due date cannot cover** from the usual monthly leftover: hold-back = max(0, bill - usual leftover x whole months until due), capped at the built-up share. A fact line names the coming bill and its date. RC-10 (Hana): hold-back £0, answer "Fits, but tight" (£50 left), with the insurance fact line. Someone with a thin leftover still gets the full built-up share held back. The built-up share resets after each payment.
- **D-48 (every borrowing answer, mortgages included):** shows, as facts, the total interest as a cost ("money that could stay with you"), that a safety net covers surprises while repaying, and that more income or lower costs makes repaying easier. Declarative only; never "recommend", "best to" or "you should".
