# Phase 05.4 Plan 18: safety net Summary

**The safety net is protected by default, at a starter of 1 month of essential costs (bills, essential spending and debt minimums, rounded up). Only money already saved in it is protected, and money above its size counts as spare. A purchase that fits while the net is empty or small is amber: "Fits, but no safety net" or "Fits, but your safety net is small", with "1 month is a start; 3 to 6 months is common." A purchase that only fits with the net's money is "Dips into your safety net", whatever its purpose. Protection off, or a $0 net, counts the net's money like any other money and says so. The owner approved all 47 changed answers (D-14, approve-all, 2026-11-03).**

## What changed

- **Essential costs (ARC-07).** Your share of the usual bills, plus debt minimums, plus typical essential spending, rounded up to a whole unit, returned with its parts.
  - Essential spending is the median of the categories marked essential over the typical month's own months, capped at the typical everyday figure.
  - With no category marked, it is the typical everyday figure.
  - From quick estimates alone the typical figure already holds the bills, so they are taken out once.
- **The net in force.**
  - No record: protection on, the starter, and the emergency fund (or 0) as the money saved.
  - Any size is respected (D-05). Size 0 counts as protection off and adds `net_zero_is_off` (D-06).
  - There is no hold-back field; nothing is held back for the unsaved part (D-35).
- **The judge.**
  - Spare money for a one-off includes money above the size.
  - The ladder runs: earlier money, then money above the size, then the overdraft, then the protected net (all of it when protection is off), else Not yet. The ready date counts money above the size but never the net.
  - A fit (fits_month or fits_unspent) gets the variant from `fitVariant`. The order is low_month first, then no_net / small_net; a losing variant keeps its fact. `net_none_start` / `net_progress` also show under "Uses your overdraft".
  - "Dips into your safety net" leads with its own facts:
    - `need_net_line` for a need, otherwise `wait_keeps_net` (the later of the payday that clears the dip and the month spare money covers the price);
    - then `net_used`, `net_left`, `net_refill`;
    - then `net_progress`, `net_protection_off` or `net_zero_is_off` as the case may be.
  - `emergency_untouched` shows only when something is saved (D-25), with the protected amount.
- **The cash check.** The path opens at spending money, the purchase's own envelope and the money above the size.
  - Past the overdraft floor, `withNet` reads the check again with what the net can lend. If that brings the low point back to 0 or above, the kind is `net`.
  - `net_used` takes what the purchase adds to that dip. A dip that is there without the purchase is not the purchase's (Riley's $60 shoes name $60, not her short month's $660).
- **Purpose (D-41, D-49).**
  - `mayUseEmergency`, `emergencyUse` and the D-63 fund-for-cash gate are deleted. `canUseEmergency` became `isNeedPurpose`.
  - I38 is retired with a documented test; I-D41 replaces it.
  - The "Uses emergency fund" copy is deleted. A stored `emergency_use` answer reads the "Dips into your safety net" copy.
- **Copy (en.ts).**
  - Chips: "Dips into your safety net", "Uses your safety net · protection off", "Fits, but no safety net", "Fits, but your safety net is small".
  - Ten fact strings. "emergency fund" became "safety net" in the strings this plan touched.
  - All wording is declarative.
