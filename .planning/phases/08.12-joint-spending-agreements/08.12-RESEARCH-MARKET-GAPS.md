# 08.12 Research: market gaps and possible blind spots

**Researched:** 2026-11-21
**For:** Phase 08.12 (Joint spending agreements), final research round.
**Question:** What do the top household and budget apps, and the unusual ones, do that FincWin does not already have or plan? Which of these are real blind spots?
**Method:** reading FincWin's planning files first, then web search of app help pages, app store listings, reviews, regulators and research. Every web source was read on 2026-11-21. Confidence:
- **HIGH**: the vendor's own help page, a regulator, or a large survey.
- **MEDIUM**: a third-party review, one survey, or an older source.
- **LOW**: an app store blurb, a competitor's blog, an anecdote, or no direct source found.

Each part keeps **What the evidence says** apart from **What we suggest**.

## 1. What FincWin already covers

Taken from PROJECT.md, REQUIREMENTS.md, ROADMAP.md (all phases, backlog and v2), BUILD-PROMPT.md, the three 08.12 research files, 08.12-OWNER-ANSWERS.md, 08.1-FOLLOWUPS.md, GROWTH-RESEARCH-2026-11-19.md, FEATURES.md and `src/features/` (auth, consent, decide, devHarness, grow, home, household, income, navigation, record, system, you).

**Record and Activity (built)**
- Income, spending, transfers, accounts, own categories with monthly caps, recurring series (skip or end one), clone, paste and bulk add.
- Statement import: CSV, OFX/QFX and text PDF, with format checks, balance reconcile, duplicate guard and transfer pairs.
- Many currencies with the rate stamped per line; overdraft and credit limits; account standing.
- Activity views: day, week, split, balance and calendar; search, filter, sort, bulk delete; Pending, Due, Overdue and Paid tags; 12-step undo.
- Recurring bills found from history and offered as a series (ACT-11); one bill detector (05.5).

**Home (built):** This month card (money left after everything), coming up with Mark paid, accounts summary.

**Decide (built or in 08.1 / 08.2)**
- "Can I afford it?" from the user's own months, with a 90-day day-by-day cash path and a 12-month look.
- Safety net protected; debt minimums, tax, fees and running costs (car, phone, pet and tech templates); total cost; wait-and-save goal; cheaper and skip options.
- Setbacks (income drop, job loss, late invoices, surprise bill), dated life changes, one-off lines, irregular income measured from history, seasonal pay, cushion sized by income steadiness.
- Instalment plans, BNPL, promo rates, variable rates and late fees.
- Open checks, decision record and estimate-versus-actual follow-up, 48-hour cooling-off reminder.
- Named pots (sinking funds) and envelopes protected from every check; "Net free cash flow" hero (a safe-to-spend figure).
- Household money in Decide, partner opt-in, joint accounts counted once.

**Grow (built):** goals with auto-contributions, one emergency-fund goal, investments with cost-basis lots and gain/loss, debts with avalanche and snowball payoff, calculators (loan, savings, compound growth, debt payoff, goal), net worth figure.

**Household (built in 8, more in 08.1 and 08.12)**
- Invites, roles and weights, four split rules, category defaults, shared lines, settle up, mine-versus-household view, name privacy, leave, remove, departure handling, Realtime.
- Spending rule, joint account purposes, shared safety net, partner figure (08.1).
- 08.12 plan: "Both can use all of it" free access, spend limit with safeguards, "Personal money" between members, "Used by both" accounts, top-up from own money, partner cover request, personal credit for the gap, instalments on shared money, and a "Money and safety" help page.

**Later phases already planned**
- **Phase 7 Insights:** net worth over time, cash-flow bars, category breakdown, month against month, health score.
- **Phase 9 Tiers:** 11-question onboarding, four levels, paywall, Solo, Duo and Household plans, "What's next" page with vote counts.
- **Phase 10 System:** biometrics and PIN, alerts (bills due, over cap, goal reached, large spend, low balance) with digest and quiet hours, a partner's large expense as a push, CSV export and import, archive, real account deletion.
- **Phase 11:** compliance and release.
- **Home-buying check** (FIN-30), its own phase after 08.2.
- **Growth plan (G-1..G-5):** paid bank linking later by region; auto-reconcile; statement screenshot OCR; Apple Pay tap to log; receipt snap, email forwarding and an Android notification listener as "coming soon"; "hidden from household" lines soon after launch.
- **Backlog:** split one line across categories (999.1), receipt photo (999.2), envelope rollover (999.3), Coach (999.4), custom payment and account types (999.5).
- **v2:** web app, bank and brokerage feeds, round-ups, widgets, more than one household, per-account sharing, passkeys.

**Deliberately out of scope:** tax calculation and tax reports; trading; lending and credit offers; live security prices; ads or selling data; local-only mode; prescriptive advice wording.

## 2. Ideas FincWin does not have or plan

How common it is: **table stakes** (nearly every app in the group), **common** (many apps), **rare** (a few), **unique** (one app or none in budgeting apps).

**What users complain about most, and why they leave** (MEDIUM unless marked)
1. **Bank sync breaks.** The top complaint for Honeydue, Spendee and most linked apps. FincWin avoids this by design (no bank link at launch), but pays with entry effort.
2. **Manual entry is tiring.** Cited as the top reason people drop expense trackers in the first weeks (LOW: unsourced blogs). One app maker's cancel survey found two reasons: people gave up budgeting, or another app did it better.
3. **Free tiers that punish the household.** Splitwise's daily cap angered groups where only one person pays.
4. **Price.** $13–15 a month feels wrong to people paying down debt.
5. **Nagging alerts that cause guilt.**
6. **Slow support** (Honeydue).
7. **Privacy worries** about linking banks and about data being shared.
8. **The app closes** (Money Dashboard: "no sustainable business model"; Mint). HIGH.

## 3. Top blind spots, ranked

### What we suggest

1. **"Talk it over": share one check with the group, with no limit needed (G).**
   - **Where:** 08.12. It is the same request screen as the spend limit, but started by the asker and answered with "Sounds good" or "Let's talk". Under the 08.12 rules nothing is stored after "Not now".
   - **Decide tie:** it shares the answer card only (the verdict, the price and the "shared money" line). Open checks stay private unless the asker chooses this. It never changes the verdict.
2. **A monthly money meeting (F).**
   - **Where:** a light version in 08.12, a full version later (Phase 7 Insights, or Phase 10 with the digest).
   - 08.12 part: each agreement (limit, personal money, sharing choice) gets an optional "Look at this again on…" date, shown to everyone.
   - Later part: one screen per month with what changed, shared money left, bills that went up, settle-up, pots and open "talk it over" items. No streaks and no nagging.
   - **Decide tie:** it lists open household checks and the money left, so the talk starts from the same numbers.
3. **Bill and subscription price-rise notice, plus a subscriptions list (A, B).**
   - **Where:** Phase 10 (a sixth alert kind beside ALR-01), with the list in Phase 7 Insights or Home.
   - **Decide tie:** a higher bill lowers net free cash flow. The notice says that open checks were judged again: "Phone bill is $4 more each month. Open checks now use the new amount."
4. **Rotating savings clubs (S).**
   - **Where:** its own small phase after 08.2, or a Grow add-on. It is not in 08.12, but 08.12's shared-money and credit rules should not block it.
   - **Decide tie:** contributions are a fixed commitment. The payout is known money in, on a known month. Decide can show "Your club pays out in March: this fits from then" without counting the payout early.
5. **Part-month move-in and move-out (X).**
   - **Where:** a Household follow-up after 08.12. It needs a "from" and "until" date on a member's share.
   - **Decide tie:** a member's share of a bill changes from a date, so the 12-month look must use the new share from that day.
6. **Family support and giving set-asides (T, U).**
   - **Where:** Phase 9 (seed categories in onboarding) plus a small Grow change for a "% of money in" pot.
   - **Decide tie:** marking family support as committed means Decide never treats it as spare. A % pot comes off net free cash flow like any other pot.
7. **Receipt item split for shared lines (M).** Already planned (999.1, 999.2). Suggest raising it to right after launch because Splitwise made it free in Oct 2026. **Decide tie:** none directly; it keeps shared lines right.
8. **Event budgets (W).**
   - **Where:** after the home-buying phase, reusing its many-line check for weddings, moving house, a new baby or a pet's first year.
   - **Decide tie:** it is a Decide check with many lines and dates.

## 4. Ideas that are risky for FincWin

### What the evidence says
- The FTC's case against Cleo AI ($17m, March 2025; $158m+ returned to customers, Oct 2026) covered cash-advance claims and hard-to-cancel subscriptions under ROSCA. HIGH ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/03/cash-advance-company-cleo-ai-agrees-pay-17-million-result-ftc-lawsuit-charging-it-deceives-consumers)).
- UK research with Surviving Economic Abuse found that features built for convenience and security (shared logins, spending alerts) are reused for control. HIGH ([City, Univ. of London evidence](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/38516/1/FIS0025_written_evidence.pdf)).
- Apps that save money for you (Plum) hold e-money under FCA authorisation. HIGH ([Plum help](https://help.withplum.com/en/articles/15924005-new-electronic-money-issuer)).

### What we suggest
| Idea | Why it is risky |
|---|---|
| Bill negotiation and cancelling for the user | It acts for the user with third parties and needs their logins. It charges a fee from the savings. Cancel flows are under FTC ROSCA scrutiny. |
| Switching with referral fees | Paid placement clashes with neutral answers and "no recommendation" copy. Insurance or credit switching can be a regulated activity. |
| Holding or collecting money (savings pots, club pots, kids' cards, round-ups) | Needs e-money or banking authorisation. It breaks the "holds no money" line. |
| AI chat over money | Advice wording and accuracy risk. It is already an open owner decision (Coach). |
| Partner spending alerts, comments on every line, a "why did you buy this?" prompt | Known tools of economic abuse. Only voluntary, symmetric, opt-in sharing is safe. |
| Chores-for-pay and child accounts | Children's data rules (COPPA, UK Children's Code) and store age ratings. It turns FincWin into a kids' bank. |
| Zakat, tax and benefit entitlement calculators | They are religious, tax or benefits rulings. Errors cause real harm, and tax is already out of scope. Record what was paid instead. |
| Credit scores | Needs a credit bureau deal and is regulated data. |
| Shame or roast tone, streak challenges, personality labels | Off the calm, declarative brand. Guilt is a known reason people leave. Quiz labels are unvalidated. |
| "Safe to spend" worded as permission ("You can spend $40 today") | It is prescriptive. Keep it as a fact ("$40 a day left this month"). |

## 6. Gaps and caveats

- Many review pages are competitors' or affiliate blogs. These are marked LOW or MEDIUM.
- "No app does X" claims (G, S inside budgeting apps, X) come from absence of evidence in this search. They are MEDIUM at best.
- Honeydue's in-app chat and reactions were not confirmed in this round.
- No large survey on why people leave budgeting apps was found. The churn reasons are review themes, not measured shares.
- No app store review mining was done at scale; themes come from summaries.
