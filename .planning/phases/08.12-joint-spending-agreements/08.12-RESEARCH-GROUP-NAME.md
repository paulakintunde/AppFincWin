# 08.12 research: what to call the shared group

**Date:** 2026-11-21
**Question (owner):** Today the shared feature is called "Household". With private Rooms coming (Phase 08.13), and groups that are friends or roommates, should it still be called "Household"?
**Short answer:** Keep "Household" as the one product word, and put the group's own name in front of it everywhere ("The Oak St flat"). Add one optional setup question, "Who's in it?", that suggests a name. Do not rename to "Home" (it clashes with the Home tab). If the owner wants friends who do not live together to use the feature, "Group" is the only word that fits them, but it fits Rooms worse.

Confidence marks: **HIGH** = official help page or official definition; **MEDIUM** = store listing, third-party docs or review; **LOW** = judgment or not verified.

**A staged rename is possible.** The word is only in the UI strings. Code, routes, i18n keys and table names can stay `household` forever, and users never see them. A UI-only rename means about 57 English strings, the fallback name in one RPC (or show the fallback from the app instead), the export label, the plan display names, and the store listing. Rough size: one small plan plus a copy review. The risk is in the store and plan names, not in the code.

**Pattern (MEDIUM):**
- Apps for **money tracking over months** (Monarch, YNAB) use "household" or a named group. Monarch, the closest rival, uses "household" exactly as FincWin does.
- Apps for **splitting a bill or a trip** (Splitwise, Tricount, Settle Up, Splid, Revolut) all use **"group"**. These groups do not need people to live together.
- **Family** words (Apple, Google, Cozi) are for families only.
- **Home** words show up in chores apps for people who live together (Flatastic, OurHome).
- Nobody found uses a group word plus "rooms" inside it. Splitwise's "home" type with sub-groups is the nearest, and it uses separate groups instead.

**Official meaning of "household" (HIGH):** the UK ONS defines a household as "one person living alone, or a group of people (not necessarily related) living at the same address who share cooking facilities and share a living room, sitting room or dining area" ([ONS, Families and households statistics explained](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/birthsdeathsandmarriages/families/articles/familiesandhouseholdsstatisticsexplained/latest)). So by the official meaning, **roommates and flatmates are a household**. Friends who do not live together are not.

## 4. Plain-English and inclusion points (judgment, LOW unless marked)

- **"Household" is correct but a little formal.** It is a census and insurance word. People rarely say "my household" about their flat. But it is clear, and Monarch shows it works in a money app. Putting the group's own name first ("The Oak St flat") takes away most of the coldness.
- **"Family" leaves out roommates and friends.** It also can feel painful to people without family. Not suitable as the fixed word.
- **"Home" leaves out friends who don't live together** (a trip, a car), the same as "Household". It is warmer, but **FincWin already has a Home screen** (`appLife/home.ts`, and ROADMAP lists "Activity, Home, Grow and Household"). "Home" for both is confusing: "Go to Home" would have two meanings.
- **"Group" includes everyone** but is plain to the point of cold, and it makes FincWin sound like a bill-splitter (Splitwise, Tricount). The G-1 decision makes FincWin a daily shared tracker, which is closer to Monarch's "household".
- **Friends who don't live together** cannot be served well today anyway: the app allows **one household per person** (`join.ts`: "You're in another household. Leave it first"). A person's car-share friend outside the household cannot be added without making them a household member. So the word matters less than this rule. Inside a household, Rooms already cover "two roommates share a car".
- **Size cap:** "A household holds up to 6 people", and Phase 9 plans are "Household (up to 6)" and "Extended (up to 12)". Any rename must also rename the plans or they will not match.

### Fit with "Rooms"

| Outer word | "… → Rooms" reads as | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Household → Rooms | A house has rooms. Natural. "Rooms in the Oak St household." | Strong |
| Home → Rooms | Natural, warmest. But clashes with the Home screen. | Strong metaphor, weak in this app |
| House → Rooms | Natural, but a UK flat or a US apartment is not a "house". | Medium |
| Group → Rooms | Mixed picture: a group is people, a room is a place. Works like chat apps ("rooms in a server"). | Medium–weak |
| Space → Rooms | Vague. Starling uses "Spaces" for savings pots, which is a different idea. | Weak |

## 6. Suggestion

1. **Keep "Household" as the one fixed product word.** It is correct for roommates by the official meaning, it fits "Rooms" best, it matches the plan names and Monarch, and it costs nothing.
2. **Show the group's own name first, everywhere it reads naturally.** 34 strings already do this. In a later copy pass, change the generic ones where the name reads better: "Rename household" stays (it is about the thing), but cards and headers show "The Oak St flat" with "Household" only as a small label.
3. **Add one optional setup question: "Who's in it?"** with Couple / Family / Flatmates (or Roommates, by region) / Something else. It only suggests a default name ("Us", "The Lee family", "Our flat" / "Our place") and picks example wording on the invite. It does not change UI words, so there is no translation cost.
4. **Use the region's word for sharers** in the few strings that name them: "flatmates" for UK, IE, AU, NZ; "roommates" for US, CA. This follows the existing region rule (user override > device region > time zone).
5. **Leave code, routes, keys and tables as `household`.** If the owner later prefers another word, the UI-only staged rename in section 1 is about 57 strings plus plan names.
6. **Friends who don't live together** (a trip, a car) are a separate product question about "one household per person", not a naming question. Keep it out of 08.13 unless the owner wants it.

## 7. Owner questions (plain English, recommended option first)

**Q1. What should we call the shared part of the app?**
- A. Keep "Household", and show the group's own name first, like "The Oak St flat". *(recommended)*
- B. Rename it to "Group". It fits friends best, but sounds like a bill-splitting app and fits Rooms less well.
- C. Let each group pick its own word (Household, Family, Flat or Home). Most flexible, but costs the most to build and translate.
- D. Rename it to "Home". Warm, but we already have a Home screen.

**Q2. When someone makes a household, should we ask who is in it?**
- A. Yes, one optional question (Couple, Family, Flatmates or Roommates, Something else). It only suggests a name like "Our flat". *(recommended)*
- B. No. Just ask for a name.
- C. Yes, and also change some words around the app to match the answer.

**Q3. Which word should we use for people who share a home but are not family?**
- A. Use the person's region: "flatmates" in the UK, Ireland, Australia and New Zealand; "roommates" in the US and Canada. *(recommended)*
- B. "Housemates" everywhere.
- C. "Roommates" everywhere.

**Q4. Friends who share costs but do not live together (a trip, a car): what should we do?**
- A. Not now. A person stays in one household, and Rooms cover sharing inside it. *(recommended)*
- B. Later, let a person be in more than one household. This is a big change to privacy rules and plans.
- C. Later, add a light "split a trip" feature, separate from households.
