# 08.12 Research: free access to joint money (a shared pot) without counting it twice

**Researched:** 2026-11-21
**For:** Phase 08.12 (Joint spending agreements), owner answer Q2 in `08.12-OWNER-ANSWERS.md`.
**Builds on:** `08.12-RESEARCH-ARRANGEMENTS.md` (cases OE-01..OE-12 and the design red lines), `08.1-22-SUMMARY.md`, `08.1-CASES-SPENDRULE.md` (HH-25..HH-34, the JA-01 fix), and the engine in `src/engine/household/{spendRule,jointMoney,pool,plan}.ts` and `src/engine/decide/v3/householdMoney.ts`.
**Method:** web search of bank terms and help pages, budgeting-app help pages, consumer surveys and one academic paper. Generic topics only. Confidence on each key claim:
- **HIGH**: the provider's own terms or help page, a large survey, or a peer-reviewed study.
- **MEDIUM**: a review site, a secondary report, an older or smaller survey, or a search summary of a primary page.
- **LOW**: anecdote, a forum post, or no direct source.

Each part keeps **What the evidence says** apart from **What we suggest**.

## 0. Summary in plain words

1. **The owner's point is right.** Banks never split a joint balance into slices. Every holder sees the same whole balance and can spend all of it (HIGH). Today FincWin's three rules all split joint money, so a person who really can spend $1,000 may be told they have $500, or $0.
2. **Banks and apps avoid "200%" in two ways, not by slicing.**
   - **One number, shown once.** The balance is one figure that both people look at. It is never added up per person.
   - **First come, first counted.** A card payment lowers the available balance for both holders the moment it is approved, before it settles (HIGH). Budget apps do the same with earmarks: money given a job leaves the "free" pot for everyone (YNAB, HIGH).
3. **Couples handle the rest by talking.** In a September 2026 US survey, over 7 in 10 people in couples say they must check with their partner before a big purchase. 72% have a set amount and 28% have none (HIGH). That is the owner's "pause and ask" switch.
4. **We suggest a fourth way to share joint money: "Both can use all of it" (Free access).** Each member's Just me check counts the whole joint pot. Every answer that uses it says it is shared and who else can spend it. A recorded buy or plan lowers the pot for everyone at once. Household figures count the pot once. This is option (a) plus (b), with a soft "big share" line.
5. **JA-01 still holds, restated.** There can be no *hidden* double yes. Two people may both hear "Fits" for the same shared money, because that is the truth of a joint account, but each answer says the money is shared. Once either records a buy, the other's answer changes. Household totals, the published partner figure and every pool still count the pot once.
6. **Engine change is small.** We need a new rule value (`shared`), a new joint split (`whole`), one Household rule (a shared-pot item is checked at its full price, not "my part"), one copy flag, and one database change (allow `shared` in `household_spend_rules`). No new account flag is needed: the existing joint purpose (everyday), plus the "Used by both" mark from the first research, decides which accounts form the pot.

## 1. How banks, apps and couples show "what's available"

### What the evidence says

**Bank joint accounts: one balance, equal access, no slices**

Both get instant notices when money comes in or either spends. | **Monzo** (UK) | Money is owned jointly and either holder can pay. Pots sit inside the same balance, and both holders can add to and take from them. | HIGH ([Monzo joint account terms](https://monzo.com/legal/joint-account-terms-and-conditions); [Monzo joint pots, 2018](https://monzo.com/blog/2018/12/10/joint-pots)) |
| **Revolut** (EU/UK/other) | Each holder has their own card, and every card draws on "the same central balance". Both see the history, and each can withdraw at any time; the other is told. Ownership rules differ by country. | HIGH ([Revolut joint accounts terms](https://www.revolut.com/en-AU/legal/joint-accounts/); [Revolut help](https://help.revolut.com/help/profile-and-plan/joint-accounts/can-both-account-holders-withdraw-from-a-joint-account/); [Revolut blog](https://www.revolut.com/blog/post/what-is-joint-account/)) |

None of the three shows a per-person share of a joint balance. Pots and Spaces earmark money by purpose, not by person. **HIGH.**

**The bank's own "first come" reserve: authorisation holds**
- When a card payment is approved, the bank reserves the amount. The **available balance drops at once**, while the ledger balance changes only when the payment settles, often 1 to 3 business days later. Banks tell customers to spend against the available balance.
- On a joint account that reserve lowers the one balance both holders see, whoever made the payment.
- **HIGH.** [Citi, current vs available balance](https://www.citi.com/banking/personal-banking-guide/basic-finance/current-balance-vs-account-balance); [CommBank, pending transactions](https://www.commbank.com.au/articles/banking/what-are-pending-transactions.html); [Stripe, authorisation holds](https://stripe.com/au/resources/more/authorization-holds-explained)

**Budgeting apps: one shared plan, earmarks instead of slices**

No source found shows a couples app that hands each partner a separate per-person "available" figure from one joint pot. Each shows one shared number and relies on earmarks and live updates. **MEDIUM** (absence of evidence; help pages do not spell out every screen).

**How couples coordinate: "pause and ask" amounts are normal**
- **CFP Board / Morning Consult, fieldwork 2–4 September 2026, 820 US adults married or living with a partner, ±3.4 points:**
  - "Over seven in 10 Americans in relationships say they must consult their spouse or partner" for a big purchase.
  - The amount at which they check in: **under $100: 19%; $100–$499: 33%; $500–$999: 13%; $1,000 or more: 7%; no set amount: 28%.**
  - **41%** made a major financial decision in the last 12 months without telling their partner (23% more than once).
  - **HIGH.** [CFP Board 2026 "To Have and To Hold" report](https://www.cfp.net/-/media/files/cfp-board/knowledge/reports-and-research/consumer-surveys/2026-to-have-and-to-hold-survey-report.pdf)
- Ameriprise (2016, via CNBC): two-thirds of consumers have an agreement to talk before spending over a set amount; a later Ameriprise survey put the average at $400 ($300 for millennials). **MEDIUM** (dated). [CNBC 2016](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2016/09/21/big-spenders-ignore-spouses-at-their-own-peril.html); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/spending-without-telling-your-partner-could-spell-trouble)
- MoneyLion (2026): 41% think purchases over $100 should be discussed with a partner; 18% think they should not have to disclose their spending at all. **MEDIUM.** [MoneyLion](https://www.moneylion.com/trending/money/money-secrets-many-couples-hide)
- Starling (May 2024): couples set a "rough plan" for what is fine on the joint account. No fixed limit, and grey areas are talked through. **LOW to MEDIUM** (customer stories). [Starling blog](https://www.starlingbank.com/blog/spending-from-the-joint-account-what-s-legit/)

**Spending from shared money changes behaviour**
- Garbinsky & Gladstone (*Journal of Consumer Psychology*, 2019): people spending from a joint account choose more practical and fewer "treat" purchases than from their own account. The cause is a felt need to justify the spend to the partner, so the effect disappears when the treat is easy to justify. The authors warn that never spending on treats has costs too. **HIGH.** [UCL Discovery copy](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/10070465/3/Gladstone%20JCP%20CA%20Main%20Body%2011%2019%2018%20FINAL.pdf); [Notre Dame news](https://mendoza.nd.edu/?p=7598)

### What we suggest

- **Free access is the norm we must model, not an edge case.** Banks give it by default, and the slice rules are FincWin's own planning lens. Keep the slices for couples who want them, but add a choice that matches the bank.
- **Copy the two bank and app mechanisms, not slicing:** one shared number shown once, and a "first come" drop the moment anyone records a buy or plan.
- **The "pause and ask" amount fits real life.** 72% of couples in the 2026 survey have a set amount and the spread is wide ($100 to $1,000+). So the amount must be the group's own, and "no set amount" (switch off) must be a normal, neutral state.
- **Saying "shared" in the answer is useful, not just honest.** People already feel they must justify shared spending (Garbinsky & Gladstone). A plain "this is shared money" line matches that feeling without adding pressure. It must not judge the purchase.

## 2. Design options for "Free access"

All options keep personal money as each person's own (the first research's red lines 2 and 6). They differ only in how joint everyday money, and "Used by both" money, is counted.

Worked example for the table: joint everyday pot $1,000, Alex and Sam, no limit.

| Option | How it works | Pros | Cons | Double-yes risk | How the answer reads (Alex, $600) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Shared pot** | Each member's Just me counts the whole pot. Household counts it once. | Matches the bank and the owner's ask. Fixes the one-earner trap (OE-05) with no $0 share. Simple to explain. | Two people can hear Fits for the same dollars at the same moment. | **Visible, not hidden.** Both can hear Fits, but each answer says it is shared. Nothing updates if nobody records. | "Fits. Uses $600 of the shared $1,000. Sam can spend from it too. Leaves $400 in the shared money." |
| **(b) Shared pot + first come** | As (a). A recorded buy, or a planned item, on the shared account lowers the pot for every member at once (live update). Open answers recompute. | Same as the bank's available balance. Uses lines and realtime we already have. Gives "one partner buys first" a clear answer. | Only works if the buy is recorded against the shared account. Plain checks that are never recorded reserve nothing. | **Low.** The window is between two checks and the first record. After a record, the second person's answer changes. | As (a). After Alex records it, Sam's open $700 check reads: "Not yet. The shared money changed since you checked: it is now $400." |
| **(c) Shared pot + soft guard on open checks** | If both have open checks against the same pot, each answer names the other's check. | Warns before anyone records. | **Breaks privacy:** checks are owner-private today (`decide_checks`, OQ5). Telling Sam what Alex is *thinking* of buying is the kind of spending watch Refuge warns about (red line 1). Creates pressure to drop your own check. | Lowest | "Fits … Alex is also checking an item from this money." **We suggest not doing this.** |
| **(d) Keep slices, add a "you can reach all of it" line** | The verdict uses the slice; a second line says the whole pot is reachable. | No engine change. JA-01 untouched. | The verdict says Not yet while the person can in fact pay. That is a wrong answer in the core feature, and it does not fix OE-05 (Alex's slice is still $0). The two numbers confuse people. | None | "Not yet, short $100 (your share is $500). You can reach all $1,000 in the joint account." |
| **(e) Shared pot + first come + "big share" line** (our pick) | (b), plus a plain line when one item would use more than half of the shared money still free. Wording only; the verdict does not change. | Gives the partner's view without naming anyone's private plans. Uses only numbers both can already see. Works in groups of 3+. | One more line to word and test. The 50% mark is a choice (Q7). | **Low**, and the biggest overlaps get a line | "Fits. Uses $600 of the shared $1,000, more than half. Sam can spend from it too. Leaves $400 in the shared money." |
| **(f) Shared pot with a held-back buffer** | Just me counts the pot less a small buffer (for example 10%), so two near-same-time small buys cannot overdraw it. | Cushion for timing gaps. | Hides real money again (the owner's complaint, just smaller). Another number to explain. The existing floor already does this job. | Low | "Fits … (we keep $100 of the shared money aside)". **Not suggested.** |

**Other patterns looked at and set aside**
- *Per-person earmarks inside the pot* (like Monzo pots per person). This is just slicing again with extra steps.
- *A running "claimed by" list of everyone's checks.* Same privacy problem as (c).

### What we suggest

**(e): a shared pot with first come, plus a "big share" line.** It is (a) and (b) together, which the evidence supports most strongly (banks and envelope apps both work this way). The line adds a gentle cue drawn only from numbers both people can already see. Keep (d) out: it gives a wrong verdict. Keep (c) out: it watches a partner's private checks.

## 3. How the "pause and ask" limit sits on top of free access

### What the evidence says
- Banks put no shared cap on joint holders; limits follow each person's plan (Revolut, HIGH, from the first research).
- Most couples use an agreed amount, but 28% have none (CFP Board 2026, HIGH).
- Spend limits and threshold alerts are named abuse tactics (Refuge, HIGH, from the first research).

### What we suggest

The limit is a **per-item line on a Fits answer** for money taken from the shared pot. It is not a running cap and not a block. All safeguards from section 4.4 of the first research stay as they are:

| Rule | Under free access |
|---|---|
| What it covers | Items paid from the shared pot (joint everyday, and "Used by both" accounts if the owner answers Q12 = A). Never personal money. |
| One amount | One limit for the whole group. No per-person amounts. |
| On / off | On only when every member has said yes ("Waiting for Sam"). Any one member can turn it off at once; the others get a plain notice. Undo sends a new set-up request. |
| Verdict first | Only a Fits answer carries "Needs Sam's OK". Not yet never asks. |
| Per item, no tally | Each item over the limit is asked about on its own. Items under the limit carry no line, however many there are. Nothing counts how much each person spent under it. |
| Spender chooses | A request is sent only when the person taps "Ask Sam". |
| No refusal record | OK or Not now; the request then closes, with no history, count or "went ahead anyway" flag. |
| No spending alerts | The pot drops for everyone when a buy is recorded (that is the shared balance, as in a bank app), but no notice is sent about who spent what. |
| Mixed check | If an item uses own money first and only part of the shared pot, the limit is tested on the shared part only (Q5). |
| Free access off | The limit works the same under a slice rule, against the slice. The two settings are independent. |

Why it layers cleanly: free access answers "can this money cover it?" The limit answers "did we agree to talk first at this size?" One is a fact from the records, the other a recorded agreement. Neither changes the other's maths.

## 4. Names and settings (plain grade-10 English)

### What we suggest

**Setting 1, title: "How you share joint money"**
Helper line: "This changes how each person's 'Just me' check counts the money in joint accounts. It does not move any money."

| Choice (shown in this order) | Engine value | Helper line |
|---|---|---|
| **Both can use all of it** | `shared` | "Each of you can spend the whole joint balance. FincWin counts it once, shows it as shared, and lowers it for everyone when someone records a buy." |
| **Equal shares** | `custom`, equal weights | "Each person counts the same part of the joint money." |
| **Set shares** | `custom`, chosen percentages | "Each person counts the part you set, for example 60% and 40%." |
| **Same as the bill split** | `bill` | "Each person counts the same part they pay of shared bills." |
| **What each person puts in** | `bring` | "Each person counts what they paid in this month. Money from before is shared equally." |

Notes:
- "Free access" is the short name in the docs. On screen, "Both can use all of it" says the same thing in plain words; for 3+ members it reads "Everyone can use all of it".
- "Equal shares" and "Set shares" are one rule in the engine (`custom`). Showing them as two choices makes the common case one tap.
- "What each person puts in" replaces "What each person brings" only if the wording sign-off agrees; otherwise keep the approved name.
- Change notice (existing event): "Sam changed how you share joint money: from Equal shares to Both can use all of it."

**Setting 2, title: "Ask first over an amount"**
- Switch, off by default. Helper line when off: "When it is on, a 'Fits' answer for a big item from joint money adds a line to check with each other first. It never blocks a payment and never covers your own money."
- Amount field: "Items over: $___". One amount for everyone.
- States: "Off" / "Waiting for Sam to say yes" / "On for everyone, items over $200".
- Off line for any member: "Turn off". Notice to others: "Sam turned off 'Ask first over an amount'."

**Answer wording (free access)**
- Fits: "Fits. Uses $600 of the shared $1,000. Sam can spend from it too. Leaves $400 in the shared money."
- Big share (Q7): add "That is more than half of the shared money."
- Changed since checked: "The shared money changed since you checked. It is now $400."
- Limit: "Fits. Needs Sam's OK (items over $200)." Request reply buttons: "OK" / "Not now".
- 3+ members: "Others in the group can spend from it too."

Words not used anywhere above: "advice", "recommend", "you should", "permission", "allowance", "denied", "not allowed".

## 6. Engine impact

### What would change

**`spendRule.ts`**
- `SpendRuleKind` gains `'shared'`.
- `ResolvedSpendRule.joint` gains a third form, `'whole'`: each member counts the whole of the joint money.
- `resolveSpendRule`: `shared` gives `{ personal: BRING, joint: 'whole', sharesOutOfDate: false, rule: 'shared' }`. Personal money stays each person's own.
- `defaultRuleFor` is unchanged unless the owner answers Q2 = B.
- The one-earner suggestion (08.1 small fix) offers `shared` first and Equal shares second. It never switches on its own.

**`jointMoney.ts`**
- `JointSplit` gains `'whole'`. In `slice()`, `'whole'` returns `{ month: monthTotal, cash: cashTotal }` for every member.
- Everyday: the whole balance and month money. Spendable savings: the whole balance as cash. The shared safety net is unchanged: never spendable.
- Bills: only the money above bills due counts, and it counts whole. **A short bills pot keeps splitting its gap by the bill weights** (each person really owes their part of the gap from their own money).
- `JointSlice` gains `shared: boolean` (true under `'whole'`), so the answer can say "shared" and name who else has access. `JointSlices` gains `sharedCash` (the pot counted once), for the copy and the big-share line.
- The header comment "Slices always add up to the account's counted money" becomes: "Under a split, slices add up to the counted money. Under 'whole', each member counts all of it, the slice is marked shared, and every pool counts it once."

**`pool.ts`**
- `spendSlice` / `spendDelta`: no change. Under `shared` the personal rule is `bring`, so `personalDelta` is null and no personal pool is added.
- The partner figure stays personal-only (`withoutJointSlices`), so the pot is never added to a Household pool a second time. This is the key reason JA-01 holds.

**`plan.ts` and `decide/v3/householdMoney.ts`**
- `householdPlan` passes `'whole'` through. `applyJointMoney` needs no change: it already lays each joint account's balance as given.
- **New Household rule:** under `shared`, the part of a Household item paid from the shared pot is checked at its **full price**, not the member's agreed part (FA-07b). Without this, two halves of one sofa could each be checked against the whole pot.
- The answer frame needs a small `sharedPot` block: the pot amount, how much of it this item uses, the others with access (a count, or a name in a pair), and whether it is over half. Wording comes from plan 14's sign-off.

**Builder and data (not engine)**
- First come needs a **recorded buy or plan against the shared account**. "Record as bought" and "Plan it" need a "Paid from" pick (shared money / own money). Lines on a joint account are already shared at once (`hh_share_on_insert`), and `useJointAccountFacts` already has realtime invalidation.
- **Planner check:** `joint_account_month` sums `status = 'paid'` lines only. `JointAccountFacts.monthOut` is documented as "paid, plus planned", so the builder must add the visible planned joint lines, or the RPC must sum them too. FA-03 needs this.
- Open answers must recompute when the joint facts change (SR-B3 already covers rule and purpose changes; extend it to joint lines).
- Plain calculator (`plain.ts`) and `fixtureArb.ts` must draw `shared`. Engine revision bumps (07.1 → next).

### Does free access need a new rule value, a new account flag, or both?

**A new rule value only.** Which accounts form the pot is already decided by the joint purpose (everyday, spendable savings, money above bills) and, from the first research, by the "Used by both" mark on a personal account. A per-account "free access" flag would let one couple have two conflicting answers for the same joint money and would need its own safeguards. If the owner later wants free access on one joint account and slices on another, a per-account override can be added then.

### Does JA-01 hold?

**Yes, restated as three rules,** each testable:
1. **Counted once.** In every Household figure, pool and published partner figure, the shared pot appears once. (Property test: Household total under `shared` equals the total under any split rule for the same records.)
2. **Under a split, slices add up to the pot.** Unchanged: HH-25..HH-34 stay green.
3. **Under free access, no hidden double yes.** Every Just me answer that uses shared money carries `shared = true` and names the others with access. Any recorded buy or plan on the pot lowers it for every member before their next answer (FA-02, FA-03). A Household item from the pot is checked at full price (FA-07b).

What changes is the meaning of HH-25 under the new rule only: each member hears "Fits, $0 left, tight, shared" rather than "Not yet". HH-25 itself, under the split rules, does not move.

### Database changes
- `household_spend_rules.rule`: the check constraint and `set_household_spend_rule` accept `'shared'`. `shares` stays null for `shared`. pgTAP 114 gains assertions for `shared`. This is an additive migration.
- No new account column (see above). The "Used by both" mark (first research Q6) is its own change if approved.
- The spend limit needs its own small table or columns (amount, currency, each member's yes, on/off), plus a short-lived request row that is deleted when it closes (no history). That belongs to the limit work, not to free access.
- No change to `decide_checks` privacy: open checks stay owner-private.

## 7. Open questions for the owner

The suggested option is listed first in each.

**Q1. Do we add "Both can use all of it" (free access) as a choice for joint money?**
- A. **(suggested)** Yes, as a fifth choice next to the others. Each person's Just me counts the whole joint pot, says it is shared, and the pot drops for everyone when someone records a buy.
- B. No new choice. Keep the slices and add a line, "You can reach all $1,000" (option d).
- C. Make it the only way joint everyday money works, and keep slices for bills only.

**Q2. Which choice does a group get before anyone picks?**
- A. **(suggested)** Keep today's defaults, so no approved answer moves. List "Both can use all of it" first when people pick. When one member brings no money, the app suggests it first and Equal shares second, and the group picks.
- B. Make "Both can use all of it" the default for "One joint account" groups. HH-27 and the OE cases would move.
- C. Ask every group to pick during setup, with no default.

**Q3. What does a free-access answer say about the other person?**
- A. **(suggested)** Only that the money is shared and who else can spend it ("Sam can spend from it too"), plus how much shared money is left.
- B. Also show the total already planned from the shared money (an amount, no item names).
- C. Also show the other person's open checks. (This breaks the privacy of checks and looks like spending watch.)

**Q4. When does the shared pot drop for everyone?**
- A. **(suggested)** When anyone records a buy or a planned item paid from the shared account, the same way a bank lowers the available balance at once.
- B. Only when a payment is recorded as paid; planned items do not count.
- C. Also when someone saves a check as "thinking about it".

**Q5. A check that can use both own money and shared money: which goes first?**
- A. **(suggested)** Own money first, then shared money. The answer shows both amounts.
- B. Shared money first.
- C. The person picks each time.

**Q6. How does "Ask first over an amount" work with free access?**
- A. **(suggested)** The same as with slices: one amount for everyone, checked per item on the shared part only, on when all say yes, off when any one says so, no record of answers.
- B. As A, plus a monthly total for small items. (This keeps a tally, which the red lines rule out.)
- C. Only offer the limit with free access, not with slices.

**Q7. Should an answer point out when one item uses most of the shared money?**
- A. **(suggested)** Yes. A plain line when one item uses more than half of the shared money still free. The verdict does not change.
- B. Yes, and the verdict shows as tight.
- C. No line.

**Q8. Who can change how the group shares joint money?**
- A. **(suggested)** Any member, as today. Everyone sees a notice with before and after, and the app never switches on its own.
- B. A change needs every member to agree.
- C. Any member, but a change that lowers someone's count needs that person's OK.

## 8. Gaps and caveats

- Help pages for Monzo, YNAB and Honeydue do not show exactly what each joint holder sees as "available" or "left" on every screen. The claim that no app gives per-person slices of a joint pot rests on what their help pages describe (MEDIUM).
- The CFP Board survey is US-only (820 adults). No comparable 2025–2026 UK, EU or Asia numbers on "check-in amounts" were found.
- The CFP Board report gives a planner's view and encourages professional help; only its survey numbers are used here.
- First come only works if people record buys against the shared account. Bank sync (deferred) would make it automatic. Until then, unrecorded spending leaves the pot too high for both, the same as any manual record.
- FA cases use a $0 floor. The case writer must add the standard floor and re-check answers near the line (FA-03, FA-07).
- Not checked in code: how "Record as bought" picks an account today, and whether planned joint lines are already in `monthOut` (section 6, planner check).
