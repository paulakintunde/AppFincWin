## 0. Summary in plain words

1. **Most of the maths already exists.** The 08.1 engine can already cost these plans:
   - pay-in-4 (every 2 weeks, first payment today, 0%);
   - pay in 30 days (one payment, first payment later);
   - monthly finance at any APR, with setup, per-payment and end fees;
   - card instalment plans that charge a fixed monthly fee instead of interest (a per-payment fee);
   - "0% if paid in full" (BR-24..26).

   The "expensive borrowing" rule decides between "Fits, but borrowing costs more" and "Only by borrowing": 20% APR or more, or interest of 10% or more of the amount borrowed (D-07, D-39a). Each payment already lands on the cash path as a dated line.

   **What is new is where the money comes from:** shared money, one person's own money, or one person's credit.
2. **Top-up.** A Household answer judges the item against the shared pot (and any partner money already counted). When the shared money is short, it states the gap as a plain number. It then offers two options:
   - "Cover it from my own money", when the viewer is the payer;
   - "Ask Sam to cover $300", which reuses the spend-limit request flow.

   A cover counts only after the payer says OK. The asker never sees the payer's balance, or whether the payer borrowed.
3. **Personal credit for the gap** is the payer's own check. It is a Just me check for the gap amount, paid the way the payer chooses, so the existing 08.1 borrowing answers apply unchanged ("Only by borrowing", "Fits, but borrowing costs more", the full cost, the overdraft cost). The repayments sit in the payer's own months. If shared money repays it, the payments become planned lines on the shared pot, and the answer says whose name the credit is in.
4. **Instalments on shared money.** The first payment lowers the shared pot today. Every later payment is a planned line on the shared pot's months:
   - inside the 90-day path, it can change the verdict;
   - after 90 days, inside the 12 months, it can only add the caution line (08.1-13 `below_zero_later_with`), never change the code.

   The whole price, not each instalment, is tested against the "Ask first over an amount" limit.
5. **The law is clear on one point (HIGH in the UK; HIGH for common-law US states).** A credit agreement in one name is owed by that person alone, even when it is paid from a joint account. There are two exceptions:
   - in the nine US community-property states, a spouse can be pursued for debt taken on during the marriage (MEDIUM, by state);
   - a debt in both names makes each person liable for the whole amount ("joint and several").

   So the app must never record a credit plan in someone else's name, never "approve" a partner's credit, and always say whose name a plan is in.
6. **Coerced debt is common enough to design for.**
   - StepChange (2025): about 3% of UK adults, about 1.6 million people, have experienced coerced debt (MEDIUM-HIGH).
   - SEA: 60% of survivors on its project had at least one coerced debt (MEDIUM, service data).
   - California's SB 975 (2022) gives coerced-debt survivors a legal route (HIGH).

   One line on the "Money and safety" page and three firm rules cover it (section 4).
7. **The engine change is moderate.** The work items:
   - a `funding` block on a Household check: shared part, cover part, and who covers;
   - five new fact codes;
   - one owner rule ("only the plan's holder records it");
   - a planner check on whether the shared pot's future months (its pay-ins and bills after this month) reach the 12-month path. Today `householdMoney.ts` lays only this month's joint figures.

   For the database:
   - a request kind `cover` on the spend-limit request table;
   - an optional `late_fee` and a `both_names_confirmed` flag on plan records;
   - nothing else.
8. **JA-01 still holds.** A top-up is a transfer: it is out of the payer's own money (not spending) and into the shared pot, so it is counted once. A plan paid from shared money is counted once, as its planned lines, never also as a debt minimum. 14 right-answer cases are in section 6.

## 1. When the joint account is short: how couples handle top-ups

### What the evidence says

- **No survey measures "who tops up" directly** (absence of evidence; MEDIUM confidence in that absence). The closest evidence is about how couples arrange their money, and it warns about resentment:
  - Credit Karma (US, August–September 2025, 1,005 adults): 43% of married couples pay shared costs from a joint account. Avoiding money talk "creates strain and resentment". **MEDIUM.** [Credit Karma](https://www.creditkarma.com/about/commentary/more-prenups-less-joint-accounts-modern-romance-means-financial-protection-and-independence)
  - Finder (Australia, January 2025, 559 people): 25% hold both personal and shared accounts. The commentary: "Resentment can build quickly if financial issues are left unaddressed." **MEDIUM.** [Finder AU](https://finder.com.au/news/aussie-couples-dont-fully-combine-their-finances-2025)
  - A Canadian guide says unequal spare money between partners can cause "stress, resentment or even secrecy around spending". **LOW-MEDIUM.** [money.ca](https://money.ca/managing-money/budgeting/how-couples-can-fairly-split-household-finances)
  - Starling: a joint account "isn't about putting in exactly equal amounts". **LOW-MEDIUM.** [Starling blog](https://www.starlingbank.com/blog/pooling-your-money-as-partners/)
- **Keeping score is the known harm; talking first is the norm.** These two findings carry over from the earlier research:
  - over 7 in 10 US partners consult each other before a big purchase (CFP Board, September 2026, HIGH);
  - Splitwise-style score-keeping is a poor fit for couples (LOW).
- **Secrecy is common.**
  - 28% to 42% of US adults in couples admit hiding a purchase, a debt or a money secret. The figure depends on the survey: Bankrate, Western & Southern, 2025–2026. **MEDIUM.** [Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/credit-cards/news/financial-infidelity-survey); [GOBankingRates](https://www.gobankingrates.com/?p=2799572)
  - 41% made a major money decision in the last year without telling their partner (CFP Board 2026, carried over, HIGH).

### What we suggest

- Show the gap as a fact ("The shared money is $300 short"), never as an obligation on a named person.
- A cover is the payer's choice. The app asks only when the person checking taps "Ask Sam". It keeps no running tally of who covered what (red line 5), and it never shows the payer's balance to the asker (red line 6).
- Paying back a top-up is optional and visible. It becomes planned lines from the shared pot to the payer, which both members can see. It is never a hidden debt between partners (Q5).

## 2. BNPL and instalment plans, 2025–2026

### What the evidence says

**Size and shape of the market (US)**
- In 2025 US BNPL issuance was about **$156.7bn**:
  - "Pay in 4" was 50% of it ($78.3bn);
  - **about 63% of issuance carried 0% APR**, down from 83% in 2020–21. So 37% (about $58bn) carried interest.
  - Source: Federal Reserve FEDS Note, 5 June 2026. **HIGH.** [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/buy-now-pay-later-beyond-pay-in-4-a-comprehensive-product-overview-20260605.html)
- Product types, from the same note:
  - **Pay in 4:** 1–4 payments, the first charged at approval, the rest about every two weeks, generally 0%.
  - **Pay in 30:** one payment within 30 days.
  - **Monthly instalments:** over 30 days, first payment usually 30 days after purchase, 0% or interest-bearing. Affirm goes up to 36% APR, terms up to 5 years, up to $30,000.

  **HIGH.**

### What we suggest

- Use the YNAB idea, but computed: each future instalment is a **planned line** on the account that pays it, with the whole schedule in plain words. Do not treat a pay-in-4 as a loan balance with a minimum. The Bought screen already writes a plan with a payment series (`debts.series_id`) so the snapshot counts each payment once (05-06).
- **Late fees are a fact, never a cost.** FincWin never assumes that a payment will be missed. The plan step takes an optional "Late fee if a payment is missed", which is shown as a line. When a payment would fall on a day the paying account is below the payment, the existing path already gives a Not yet.
- **Stacking is a fact.** "Already planned on this money: 2 other plans, $250 still to pay." It shows amounts only, never item names. It is not a warning badge.

## 3. Who owes a credit plan, and the economic-abuse angle

### What the evidence says

**Liability**
- **UK:**
  - A debt in one person's name is theirs alone. Marriage or a civil partnership does not make the partner liable.
  - A joint credit agreement makes each signer liable for the **whole** amount ("joint and several liability").
  - Exceptions: joint tenancies and mortgages, council tax, and any debt the partner guaranteed.

  **HIGH.** [Citizens Advice](https://citizensadvice.org.uk/debt-and-money/debt-solutions/bankruptcy/partners-and-bankruptcy/joint-debts-and-belongings-if-your-partner-is-bankrupt); [StepChange](https://www.stepchange.org/debt-info/how-joint-debts-affect-me.aspx); [Experian UK](https://www.experian.co.uk/consumer/guides/partners-debt.html)
- **US, common-law states:** you are liable only for debts in your name or that you co-signed. An **authorised user is not liable.** **HIGH** (consistent across Nolo, Bankrate and Experian). [Nolo](https://nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/am-i-liable-my-spouses-credit-card-debt.html); [Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/credit-cards/advice/liable-for-spouses-credit-card-debt/)
- **US, community-property states** (Arizona, California, Idaho, Louisiana, Nevada, New Mexico, Texas, Washington and Wisconsin; Alaska by opt-in):
  - a spouse can be pursued for debt taken on during the marriage, even with their name not on it;
  - creditors may reach community assets;
  - a debt that benefited only one spouse may be treated differently.

  **MEDIUM** (state law varies). [Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/credit-cards/advice/liable-for-spouses-credit-card-debt/); [Experian](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/when-you-get-married-do-you-share-debt/)
- **Paying a one-name plan from a joint account does not change whose debt it is.** No source treats the payment account as moving liability. Whether a creditor can reach the joint account itself depends on the state and the account. **MEDIUM** (no direct source on this exact case).

**Coerced debt**
- **UK:**
  - About **3% of UK adults (1.6 million) have experienced coerced debt.** 31% of them are in problem debt, against 6% of all adults, and 58% never sought help (StepChange, *Filed Away*, 2025). **MEDIUM-HIGH.** [StepChange](https://www.stepchange.org/policy-and-research/filed-away.aspx)
  - SEA's Economic Justice Project: 95% of the survivors it screened faced economic abuse, and 60% of those had at least one coerced debt (service data, not representative). **MEDIUM.** [SEA statistics](https://survivingeconomicabuse.org/i-need-help/debt/statistics-on-coerced-debt)
- **US:**
  - **California SB 975** (signed September 2022, Civil Code Title 1.81.35) lets a survivor have a debt opened in their name through abuse recognised as coerced, with proof such as a police report, a court order or a professional's letter. **HIGH.** [LegiScan](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/SB975/id/2609157)
  - The CFPB began rulemaking in December 2024 on removing coerced debt from credit reports. **Its status today was not confirmed** (LOW for the current status). [NCLC](https://www.nclc.org/cfpb-to-create-rule-to-aid-victims-of-coerced-debt-financial-exploitation-of-dv-survivors-and-older-americans/)
  - NCLC's October 2025 report (*Disregarded and In Debt*, a survey of more than 200 service providers) documents how hard it is to dispute coerced debt. **HIGH** for its existence and scope. [NCLC](https://www.nclc.org/resources/disregarded-and-in-debt-understanding-barriers-to-relief-for-victims-of-coerced-debt/)
- Carried over from the first research:
  - "Imposing spend limits" and spending alerts are named abuse tactics (Refuge, HIGH);
  - the main threat is a logged-in partner using the normal screens (Bellini et al., USENIX 2023, HIGH).

### What we suggest: credit red lines (extending red lines 1–9)

10. **A credit plan is always recorded by the person whose name it is in.**
    - The plan step reads "This plan is in your name".
    - There is no "in {partner}'s name" choice.
    - A plan "in both names" counts as joint only after the other member confirms it on their own side (Q9). Until then it reads "Waiting for Sam to confirm", and it is treated as the recorder's alone.
11. **No one approves anyone's credit.**
    - The "Ask first over an amount" request covers spending **from shared money** only.
    - A plan paid from your own money, or a credit you take for your own top-up, never triggers a request and is never shown to anyone else.
    - A cover request asks only "can you cover $300?". It never asks how, and the reply carries no hint of it.
12. **Whose name, always said.** Any answer or line where shared money repays a one-name plan carries:

    > "This plan is in Sam's name. The lender asks Sam to pay it, even when shared money pays it."

    The existing `joint_loan_risk` line covers plans in both names. Neither line ever names a law; the help page says rules differ by place.
13. **"Money and safety" page: one coerced-debt line** (owner wording sign-off):

    > "Nobody should take out credit in your name, or push you to take it out, without your free choice. If this has happened, free debt help and domestic abuse services can help."

    It links to the regional support numbers already planned, and it keeps the quick-leave and no-alerts rules.

## 4. Design for FincWin

All copy below is draft wording for the sign-off. It contains no "advice", "recommend", "you should", "permission", "approve", "allowance" or "denied".

### 4.1 Top-up from one person's own money

**The money order for a Household item** (it follows owner answer 5, "own money first", which applies to Just me checks):
- A **Household** item is paid from shared money first. The gap is the only part anyone covers.
- A **Just me** check keeps "own money first, then shared money" (FA-06). It is unchanged.
- The reason: a Household item is "ours". Covering is a separate, named act by one person.

**What the answer says.** The verdict comes only from money the viewer may count:
- the shared pot;
- the viewer's own money, when the viewer covers;
- partner figures already published by opt-in (08.1 D-03);
- a cover that the payer has said OK to.

| State | Verdict | Lines |
|---|---|---|
| No cover asked | From the shared money alone: Not yet, Fits, and so on | "The shared money is $300 short of $1,300." Options: **Cover it from my own money** (the viewer) · **Ask Sam to cover $300** · the usual cheaper and later options |
| Viewer covers | Judged on shared money plus the viewer's own money, with the gap taken from own money | "Uses all $1,000 of the shared money and $300 of your own money." |
| Asked, waiting | Unchanged (it does not count yet) | "Waiting for Sam about $300." |
| Sam said OK | Judged with +$300 counted once as Sam's cover | "Uses all $1,000 of the shared money. Sam covers $300." |
| Sam said Not now | Back to the first row | "Not now for this one." The request closes with no record (red line 5) |

**Privacy (red line 6, owner answer 3).** On Sam's side, the request opens a **Just me check of $300**, already filled in, judged on Sam's own money and visible only to Sam. Sam picks how to pay: own money, a card, an overdraft or a loan. Sam's answer shows the full 08.1 result privately. Alex receives only OK or Not now. Alex never sees:
- Sam's balance;
- Sam's verdict;
- whether Sam borrowed (Q3).

**Counted once.**
- If Sam already counts in Alex's checks (partner opt-in), Sam's published figure is in the pool. In that case an OK adds nothing to the money: it only changes the lines ("Sam covers $300"). Adding it would count Sam's money twice.
- Without the opt-in, the OK adds exactly the cover amount. It goes in as a dated line, the same way `applyHouseholdMoney` lays a pool figure.

**Recording (Q4).** "Record as bought" for a covered Household item writes:
1. **Sam → shared account, $300**, as a member payment (the personal money mechanism, owner answer 9). It is money out for Sam (not spending) and money in for the shared account (a pay-in by Sam). If Sam paid the shop directly instead, the "Paid from" pick records the item as two payment lines, $1,000 shared and $300 from Sam's own account, and no transfer.
2. **The item, $1,300, from the shared account.**

Household spending rises by $1,300 once. The $300 is never counted as spending.

**Paying it back (Q5).** Paying back is off by default. Sam may add "Paid back from shared money": planned lines from the shared pot to Sam, on dates Sam picks. Everyone sees them as shared lines. There is no "owes" badge and no reminder to anyone but Sam (owner answer 12 pattern).

### 4.2 Personal credit for the gap

- The gap check is the payer's own Just me check (4.1), so **every 08.1 borrowing rule applies as approved**:
  - "Fits, but borrowing costs more" when the payer's own cash could pay (D-39a; cash ready within 31 days counts as cash, D-80);
  - "Only by borrowing" when it could not;
  - `interest_is_a_cost` and `save_vs_borrow` show the full cost;
  - the overdraft cost (`overdraft_use`);
  - "0% if paid in full" back-charges (BR-24..26).
- **The repayments belong to the borrower's months.** The schedule is laid on the borrower's own cash path. It never touches the shared pot, unless the borrower picks "Paid from: shared money".
- **When shared money repays it** (TB-06):
  - the payments become planned lines on the shared pot;
  - every member's shared money drops by each payment, counted once;
  - the answer and the line carry red line 12 ("in Sam's name");
  - if "Ask first over an amount" is on, the request covers the **whole plan** paid from shared money, because it is spending from shared money;
  - nobody "approves" the credit itself (red line 11).

### 4.3 Instalments or BNPL paid from shared money

How each plan type maps onto the existing engine. No new maths is needed:

| Plan | `PayV2` today | Lines on the shared pot |
|---|---|---|
| Pay in 4, 0% | `plan`, cadence 2 weeks, count 4, APR 0, firstPayment `today` | Payment 1 lowers the pot now. Payments 2–4 are planned lines 14, 28 and 42 days out (all inside the 90-day path) |
| Pay in 3 (UK), monthly | cadence 1 month, count 3, first payment today | As above, monthly |
| Pay in 30 days | count 1, firstPayment `deferred` (date = +30 days, no interest in the pause) | Nothing today; one planned line |
| Monthly finance at an APR | cadence 1 month, count N, APR, fees | One planned line a month; usually beyond 90 days |
| Card instalment plan with a monthly fee | APR 0, `fees.perPeriod` = the fee | Payment plus fee each month; fees count in the 10% test |
| 0% if paid in full | `deferredInterestTerm`, `promoEnd`, `rateAfter` | BR-24..26 unchanged, on the shared pot |
| Late fee | **new, optional:** `lateFee` on the plan, a fact only | None |

**Rules:**
- **Today versus later.** The pot drops today by the deposit and any payment dated today. Later payments are planned lines, recorded when "Record as bought" writes the plan's payment series on the shared account. This follows owner answer 4: a recorded plan lowers the pot for everyone at once, for this month's lines.
- **Inside 90 days**, a payment that would take the paying account below $0 (below the floor for tight) changes the verdict, as the cash path does today (IN-02).
- **After 90 days, inside 12 months**, a dip adds only `below_zero_later_with` / `below_zero_later_without` (08.1-13, approved). The verdict stays (IN-07).
- **Full cost:**
  - interest and every fee are in `interest_is_a_cost` and `save_vs_borrow`;
  - deferred interest follows BR-24..26;
  - late fees are never added to the cost (Q7).
- **The schedule in plain words** (a new fact, `plan_schedule`):
  - "Pays $200 today, then $200 on 29 November, 13 December and 27 December. $800 in all, no interest."
  - "Pays $222.31 a month from 15 December 2026 to 15 November 2027. $2,667.72 in all, $267.72 of it interest."
- **Spend limit.** "Ask first over an amount" compares the **whole price** with the limit, never one instalment (Q6). Splitting a price into instalments must not get round an agreement. This matches the existing "per item" rule.
- **Big-share line** (owner answer 7): it tests what the item takes from the shared money this month (deposit plus this month's payments). It does not test the whole price.
- **Stacking line** (`plans_running`): when other plans already have planned lines on the same paying account in the next 90 days: "Already planned on the shared money: 2 other plans, $250 still to pay." It shows amounts only.

### 4.4 Engine impact

**Already covered (no change):**
- `plan/schedule.ts`: every 2 weeks, count, first payment today / later / deferred, APR from 0, setup, per-payment and end fees.
- `plan/deferredInterest.ts`.
- `borrowing.ts`: the expensive test, save versus borrow, and the D-48 facts.
- `cash/path.ts`, `purchaseFlowsLabelled`: each payment as a dated outflow.
- `depletion.ts`: the 12-month `below_zero_later_*` facts.
- `debts.ts`: `joint_loan_risk`.

**Changes in `src/engine/decide/v3`:**
1. `types.ts` gains:
   - `CheckV2.funding?`:
     ```
     { paidFrom: 'shared' | 'own';
       cover?: { by: memberKey | 'me'; amount: MinorUnits; state: 'asked' | 'ok' } | null }
     ```
     Absent means today's behaviour.
   - `PayV2.plan.lateFee?: MinorUnits | null`.
   - `PayV2.plan.holder?: 'me' | 'both'`.
2. `householdMoney.ts`: `applyCover(inputs, amount)` lays an OK'd cover as one dated money-in line (as `applyHouseholdMoney`). It applies **only** when the cover's payer is not already in the pool (the counted-once rule). Under `shared`, a Household item checks the shared part at its full price, before any cover (FA-07b).
3. New fact codes in `types.ts` and `copy/verdictTable.ts`:
   - `shared_gap { gap, price }`;
   - `covered_by { member, amount }` (`member` 'me' or a key);
   - `credit_in_one_name { member }`;
   - `plan_schedule { first, firstDate, every, count, last, lastDate, total, interest }`;
   - `late_fee_terms { fee }`;
   - `plans_running { count, left }`.

   All of them are facts only; none changes a code.
4. `judge/judge.ts`: no change to the borrowing decision. Facts are added where `save_vs_borrow` is placed today.

**Changes in `src/engine/household`:**
- `jointMoney.ts` / `plan.ts`:
  - `JointAccountFacts` gains `plannedAhead: { date, amount }[]` for the paying shared account: planned plan payments (and, if the planner confirms the gap below, its usual future pay-ins and bills).
  - `pool.ts` is unchanged.

**Planner checks (not verified in code):**
- **P1.** Do the shared pot's future months reach the 12-month path? `householdPlan` and `applyJointMoney` lay only this month (`monthDelta`, `cashDelta`, balances). Future joint pay-ins and bills reach a member's path only if the joint series are in that member's own `series`. If they are not, an instalment from the joint account is judged against the viewer's own future pay. That would be a wrong answer in both directions. This is the largest engine risk in this file.
- **P2.** `joint_account_month` sums `paid` lines only (FA-03). Planned plan payments on the joint account must reach `monthOut` too.
- **P3.** A plan written by "Record as bought" today creates a `debts` row (creator-only under RLS) plus a payment `recurring_series`. Check that a series on a joint account is shared, so every member's pot drops, while the debt row stays private, and that the debt minimum and the series line are counted once (05-06).
- **P4.** `V3Debt.joint` is never set by any adapter today (only `quick/overlay.ts` sets it to false), so `joint_loan_risk` never fires from real data. 08.12 should set it from `holder = 'both'` once confirmed.

**Plain calculator and fixtures.** `plain.ts` and `fixtureArb.ts` must draw `funding`, `cover` and `holder`. The schema gains `check.funding`, `pay.lateFee` and `pay.holder`. The engine revision bumps.

**JA-01, restated for this file.** Each rule can be tested:
1. A top-up is a transfer. Household money in and out sums are unchanged by it; Household spending counts the item once (TB-04).
2. An OK'd cover adds money only for a payer who is not already in the pool (TB-03, TB-04).
3. A plan paid from shared money is counted once, as its planned lines. It is never counted again as the holder's debt minimum in their own Just me check, and never in the partner figure (IN-08).
4. The free-access rules hold: the pot is counted once in every Household figure, and each Just me answer that uses it says it is shared.

### 4.5 Database impact (all additive)

- **`decide_checks`:** a `funding` jsonb (or columns): paid from, the cover amount, who covers, and the state. It stays owner-private (OQ5). The cover state shown to the asker comes from the request row, not from the payer's check.
- **Request table** (from the spend-limit work, FA §6): add `kind in ('limit', 'cover')` and `amount`. The row is short-lived and deleted when it closes; there is no history (red line 5).
- **Plan records (`debts`):**
  - `late_fee bigint null`;
  - `holder text check (holder in ('me', 'both')) default 'me'`;
  - `both_confirmed_by uuid null` (the other member's confirm, red line 10).

  The holder is always `created_by`. There is no column naming another person as holder.
- **Member payment to a shared account:** this is the personal money transfer type (owner answer 9) with a shared account as the receiver. Check that the existing transfer or line model can carry it before adding a role.
- No change to `household_spend_rules` beyond FA's `shared`.

## 5. Common set-up for the cases

- Today is **15 November 2026**; pay is on the 1st.
- No overdraft unless stated.
- **Floor $0 in the hand sums**, as in the OE and FA cases. The case writer adds the standard floor and re-checks answers near the line (TB-02, IN-01, IN-07).
- **Household:** Alex and Sam, rule **"Both can use all of it"** (free access, owner answer 1). The spend limit is off unless stated.
- **Shared (joint everyday) account:**
  - Sam's pay of $5,000 lands in it on the 1st;
  - shared bills of $3,200 go out on the 1st;
  - $800 of shared everyday spending is spread through the month;
  - so a **usual month leaves $1,000**;
  - **today it has $1,000 left this month** (as FA).
- **Own money:**
  - Sam's own account: $2,000 left after Sam's own plans (private);
  - Alex: $300 of personal money left (private).
- The partner opt-in (08.1 D-03) is **off** unless stated.
- APRs read as US nominal (region null). Money in the tables is shown in dollars. Fixtures use cents.

## 7. Open questions for the owner

The recommended option is listed first in each question.

**Q1. When shared money is too short for a household item, what does the answer show?**
- A. **(recommended)** The answer is worked out from the shared money only. It says how much is missing, and it offers two buttons: "Cover it from my own money" and "Ask Sam to cover $300".
- B. The app quietly adds any member's own money to make it fit.
- C. It shows "Not yet" with no missing amount and no buttons.

**Q2. When does money someone offers to cover count in the answer?**
- A. **(recommended)** Only after that person says OK on their own phone. Your own money counts as soon as you choose to cover.
- B. As soon as the request is sent.
- C. Never. The person must move the money first and record it.

**Q3. When Sam covers the gap, what does Alex see?**
- A. **(recommended)** Only "Sam covers $300". Alex never sees Sam's balance, Sam's answer, or whether Sam used credit.
- B. Also "Sam is borrowing it" when Sam used credit.
- C. Sam's full answer.

**Q4. How is a top-up saved in the records?**
- A. **(recommended)** As money from Sam's own account into the shared account. It is not spending for Sam, and the item is one shared purchase. If Sam paid the shop directly, it is saved as two payments instead.
- B. Always as two payments for the one item (shared plus Sam).
- C. As money the shared account owes Sam, tracked in settle-up.

**Q5. Is a top-up paid back?**
- A. **(recommended)** Not unless the payer adds "Paid back from shared money". Those payback lines are then visible to everyone. There is no "owes" label and no reminders to others.
- B. Always tracked as money owed, in settle-up.
- C. Never. There is no payback option.

**Q6. With "Ask first over an amount" on, what is compared with the limit for an instalment plan?**
- A. **(recommended)** The whole price, so a $600 pay-in-4 is over a $200 limit even though each payment is $150.
- B. Each instalment.
- C. Instalment plans are left out of the limit.

**Q7. How are late fees counted?**
- A. **(recommended)** As a typed, optional "late fee if a payment is missed", shown as a line. It is never added to the cost, because FincWin does not assume a payment will be missed.
- B. One late fee is added to the cost of every BNPL plan.
- C. Late fees are not shown at all.

**Q8. A plan in one person's name is paid from shared money. What happens?**
- A. **(recommended)** It is allowed with no extra step. The answer says whose name it is in and that the lender asks only that person to pay. Its payments show to everyone as shared planned lines. "Ask first" applies to the whole plan if the limit is on.
- B. It needs every member's OK before it can be saved.
- C. It is not allowed: a one-name plan can only be paid from that person's own money.

**Q9. Can someone save a credit plan as "in both names"?**
- A. **(recommended)** Yes, but it counts as "both names" only after the other person confirms it on their own phone. Until then it is the saver's own. Nobody can save a plan in someone else's name alone.
- B. Yes, with no confirm.
- C. No. Plans are always one person's.

**Q10. Do we add a line about credit pressure to the "Money and safety" page?**
- A. **(recommended)** Yes, one plain line: nobody should take out credit in your name, or push you to, without your free choice, and free debt help and domestic abuse services can help. The wording needs your sign-off.
- B. Yes, plus a direct link to the national debt help service for each region on every borrowing answer.
- C. No line.

## 8. Gaps and caveats

- **No direct survey on "who tops up a short joint account".** The design leans on the general resentment and secrecy evidence (MEDIUM).
- **Provider fees and limits change often.** Sources conflicted on Clearpay's cap (£24 or £36) and on Klarna's Pay in 30 limit. FincWin should never ship provider names or fees as defaults. The user types the fee.
- **FICO 10 BNPL take-up** by lenders is unconfirmed (LOW). The **CFPB coerced-debt rule** status after December 2024 is unconfirmed (LOW). Re-check both before any copy cites them.
- **Community-property rules** were summarised from consumer sites, not statutes (MEDIUM). The app never states a law; the help page says rules differ by place.
- **Paying a sole-name plan from a joint account:** no source covers it directly (MEDIUM, by inference from the sole-name rule).
- **Not verified in code:** P1 to P4 in section 4.4. P1 (the shared pot's future months on the 12-month path) could change IN-04, IN-06 and IN-07, so it should be the planner's first check.
- **Floors:** cases use a $0 floor. Answers near the line (TB-02, TB-03c, IN-01, IN-07) must be re-worked with the standard floor.
- **Arithmetic:** IN-04, TB-05 and TB-06 payments are standard amortisation (monthly rate = APR/12, rounded to the cent each month). The engine's final-payment rounding may move the last cent.
