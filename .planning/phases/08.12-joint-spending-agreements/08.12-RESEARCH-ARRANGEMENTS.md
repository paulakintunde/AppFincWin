# 08.12 Research: one-earner households and shared-money arrangements

**Researched:** 2026-11-21
**For:** Phase 08.12 (Joint spending agreements). It covers one-earner households and the approval limits on joint everyday accounts.
**Builds on:** the spending rule, joint money counted once and joint account purposes, from 08.1-22 and 08.1-23. The owner approved those on 2026-11-20 (`08.1-CASES-SPENDRULE.md`). This file does not redesign them.
**Method:** web search of surveys, academic papers, government and charity pages, and app help centres. Confidence is marked on each key claim:
- **HIGH**: a primary source or a large survey.
- **MEDIUM**: one survey, a secondary report or an older source.
- **LOW**: anecdote, a promotional review, or no direct source found.

Each part keeps **What the evidence says** apart from **What we suggest**.

---

## 0. Summary in plain words

1. **Real families use a few common money patterns.** The non-earner gets a set amount for the house (housekeeping). Or they get personal money of their own. Or everything goes in one shared pot. Or some money is shared and some is kept apart ("yours, mine, ours"). The old pattern, where a breadwinner hands over housekeeping money, is fading fast in the UK. Full pooling is now a minority in US surveys, and a mix is common.
2. **Danger sign:** the rules we already have give a non-earning partner **$0** in two of the three spending rules.
   - Under "What each person brings", a joint account's new money is split by who paid it in (owner answer 08.1-22 Q3).
   - Under "Same as the bill split", a 100/0 bill split also gives the non-earner nothing (see HH-24, the parent and child case).

   A stay-at-home partner who spends from the joint account would be told **Not yet** for a $20 item. One-earner households need a different default.
3. **Approval limits and spending alerts are known abuse tools.** UK charity research found that some abusers set up spending alerts on a partner's account and impose spend limits (HIGH). So the approval feature needs firm safeguards:
   - The same limit applies to everyone.
   - Turning it on needs everyone. Turning it off needs one person.
   - It never touches personal money.
   - The app keeps no tally of refusals.
4. **No app on the market gets this fully right.**
   - Monarch shows everything to everyone (MEDIUM).
   - Honeydue lets each person choose what to share. About 40% of its accounts are not shared (MEDIUM).
   - Greenlight-style approval is built for parents and children. It should not be copied between adults.
   - Bank joint accounts (Revolut, Zeta) give both holders equal access. Neither holder needs the other's permission (HIGH).
5. **We suggest:**
   - No new setup type.
   - A new idea of **personal money**: a set amount for each person, recorded as a payment between members, counted once, private to whoever receives it, and never under approval.
   - A one-earner default rule of "A set spending split" at equal shares.
   - A way for an earner to mark their own account "Used by both".
   - Twelve right-answer cases are in section 6.

## 1. Common money arrangements in one-earner couples and families

### What the evidence says

**The standard way to classify them.** Jan Pahl's money-management types (1980s, built on by later researchers) are still the reference (HIGH):

| Type | What happens | One-earner example |
|---|---|---|
| Whole wage | One partner (often the woman, in older UK data) gets the whole wage and runs everything, handing back personal money to the earner | Japan's *okozukai*: the wife holds the pay and the husband gets pocket money |
| Housekeeping allowance | The earner keeps control and hands over a set sum for household costs | "He gives me $X a month for food and the kids" |
| Pooling (joint) | All money goes into one pot that both use | A one joint account family |
| Partial pooling | Some money is shared and each keeps some apart ("yours, mine, ours") | A joint bills account, plus personal money for each |
| Independent | Each keeps their own money and splits costs | Rare when one person has no income |

Sources: [Encyclopedia.com, household allocative system](https://encyclopedia.com/social-sciences/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/household-allocative-system); [Hu 2021, Gender & Society](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/153934/1/HU.Divergent_gender_revolutions.GendSoc.pdf).

**How common each pattern is, by region**

- **UK** (Understanding Society survey, 11,730 couples; Hu, *Gender & Society*, 2021):
  - Among homemaking women, the **housekeeping allowance pattern fell from 30% (born before 1950) to 3% (born after 1980)**.
  - Joint management among top-earning couples fell from 54% to 37%, with more couples "going solo".
  - Breadwinning men who manage the money themselves rose from 12% to 34%.
  - **HIGH.** [Lancaster University news](https://lancaster.ac.uk/news/gender-revolutions-in-who-holds-the-purse-strings); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2021/08/210817111424.htm)
- **US, all couples** (Bankrate, December 2025 survey, published February 2026):
  - **38% fully combine** their money.
  - **36% mix** joint and separate accounts.
  - **26% keep all accounts separate**.
  - **HIGH for the survey, MEDIUM as a general picture.** [Hawaii News Now / Bankrate](https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/bankrate-article/2026/02/06/survey-most-couples-keep-least-some-their-money-separate/)
  - Older data (2002 ISSP) found about 80% "pooled". Different questions give different answers. **MEDIUM.** [Addo & Sassler 2010, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3404834)
- **US, lower-income families with children** (2006, Addo & Sassler):
  - 61–64% have joint accounts only.
  - 17–19% have joint plus separate accounts.
  - 11–12% have separate accounts only.
  - 9% have no bank account at all.
  - **MEDIUM** (dated). [PMC3404834](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3404834)
- **US, how often one partner is at home:**
  - **18% of US parents did not work for pay** in 2021 (26% of mothers and 7% of fathers).
  - About 1 in 5 stay-at-home parents are fathers.
  - **HIGH.** [Pew Research Center, 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/08/03/almost-1-in-5-stay-at-home-parents-in-the-us-are-dads/)
- **US, joint accounts and family size:** couples with more than one earner pool less. Each extra person in the household raises the odds of a joint account by about 20% (University of Georgia, 2023). **MEDIUM.** [UGA News](https://news.uga.edu/?p=89297)
- **Canada:**
  - Single-earner families were **27% of couple families with children in 2014, down from 59% in 1976**.
  - The share held steady between 2005 and 2015.
  - **HIGH but dated.** [Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-630-x/11-630-x2016005-eng.htm)
- **Australia** (Finder, January 2025, 559 people in couples):
  - **59% fully share**.
  - 25% have both personal and shared accounts.
  - 17% keep their money separate.
  - The Australian Institute of Family Studies (AIFS) reports that 78% of couples hold a joint account and 47% hold separate accounts. Both can be true at once.
  - **MEDIUM.** [Finder AU](https://finder.com.au/news/aussie-couples-dont-fully-combine-their-finances-2025); [AIFS](https://aifs.gov.au/node/40841)
- **Japan:**
  - Wives traditionally manage the household money, even when the husband is the only earner.
  - Husbands get monthly pocket money (*okozukai*), averaging about ¥36,835 in a 2026 Meiji Yasuda survey.
  - **MEDIUM.** [KPL / Meiji Yasuda 2026](https://kpl.gov.la/detail.aspx?id=97828); [Japan Times 2011](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2011/07/05/business/okozukai-vs-hesokuri-an-alternate-view-of-home-economics)
- **India** (National Family Health Survey 5, NFHS-5, 2019–21):
  - 78.6% of women have a bank account they use themselves.
  - 14% of employed married women have no say over how their own earnings are spent.
  - **MEDIUM.** [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/blog/economy/what-does-nfhs-5-data-tell-us-about-state-of-women-empowerment-in-india-80920)
- **Rest of the EU:**
  - Much of the research comes from European countries, but it is mostly not nationally representative. Systems "vary widely by country".
  - A 2026 German study compares East and West.
  - **LOW** for EU-wide numbers.

**Culture matters, in both directions.** In Japan, the non-earner often *holds* the purse. In the older UK pattern, the earner held it. The link between pooling and relationship happiness is stronger in the US and UK than in Japan. **MEDIUM.** [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/node/323844)

**Pooling itself is not the problem.**
- Couples who pool all their money report higher relationship quality.
- In an experiment, 230 engaged or newlywed couples were randomly assigned to a joint account or to separate accounts. The joint-account couples reported higher relationship quality two years later (Olson et al., *Journal of Consumer Research*, December 2023).
- **HIGH for the experiment, MEDIUM for the survey links.** [Greater Good](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/article/item/are_joint_bank_accounts_good_for_your_marriage); [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/joint-bank-account)

### What we suggest

- Support all five patterns, but do not label anyone. The non-earner may be the one who runs the money (Japan), so roles must not be fixed as "provider" and "dependent".
- Treat "yours, mine, ours" as normal. It matches our existing "Mixed" setup.
- Do not push couples to split their money. The evidence says sharing is good. The risk is control, not sharing.

## 2. Fairness, wellbeing and abuse

### What the evidence says

**The earner often keeps a hidden sense of ownership.**
- In interviews, money keeps a "psychological label" from whoever earned it, even after it is pooled.
- Personal spending tends to follow "who earned it" (equity) rather than equal shares, so earners often get more say and more personal money.
- **MEDIUM** (small qualitative studies, still widely cited). [Burgoyne, UWE repository](https://uwe-repository.worktribe.com/output/1035639); [Burgoyne 1990, *Sociological Review*](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/1008217540)

**Stay-at-home partners often feel guilty spending on themselves.**
- Personal-finance writers advise against the word "allowance" for a spouse. They suggest "personal expense account" or "my personal budget".
- **LOW to MEDIUM** (advice pieces, not surveys). [Money.com](https://money.com/financial-habits-stay-at-home-parents/?amp=true); [CNBC op-ed, 2024](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/04/13/op-ed-allowances-are-for-kids-not-your-spouse.html/)

**Economic abuse is common, and the law names it.**
- The UK Domestic Abuse Act 2021, s.1(4), defines economic abuse as behaviour that has a "substantial adverse effect" on a person's ability to "acquire, use or maintain money or other property" or "obtain goods or services". **HIGH.** [legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2021/17/section/1)
- A November 2024 Ipsos survey for Surviving Economic Abuse (2,849 UK women) found:
  - **1 in 7 UK women (4.1 million) experienced economic abuse from a partner or ex-partner in the last 12 months.**
  - 23% of them said it stopped them leaving.
  - Named tactics include **"seeking their partner's permission before making any purchases"**, having wages paid into the partner's account, and being left short of money for basics.
  - **HIGH.** [Surviving Economic Abuse](https://survivingeconomicabuse.org/news/one-million-women-trapped/)
- Refuge's warning questions include: *"Does your partner check your receipts or bank statements so they can monitor how much you are spending?"* **HIGH.** [Refuge](https://refuge.org.uk/how-we-can-help-you/economic-abuse/)
- Attitudes (Ipsos for the UK government, June 2026):
  - **19% of men aged 18–24 say controlling how a partner spends is "probably or definitely not abuse"**, against 6% of men aged 45–54.
  - **HIGH.** [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/controlling-spending-is-not-abuse-say-a-fifth-of-young-men)

**Banking features are already used as control tools.** These are the most direct warnings for us.
- Refuge and The Co-operative Bank (December 2021) found:
  - In **9% of cases, abusers set up text alerts when their partner spent a certain amount** from a joint or personal account.
  - A similar share used online banking to watch the partner's online shopping.
  - 12% of survivors said their bank accounts were watched through online banking.
  - **HIGH.** [Refuge press release](https://refuge.org.uk/refuge-and-the-co-operative-bank-launch-commission-into-online-banking-and-economic-abuse/)
- Refuge's chief executive (December 2022): abusers are "setting up text alerts on their partners accounts, **imposing spend limits**", and online banking is "commonly used… to closely track their partner's accounts and monitor their spending". **HIGH.** [Refuge commission review](https://refuge.org.uk/news/online-banking-commission-domestic-abuse-review-dec-2022/)
- Bellini et al. (USENIX Security 2023) audited 13 banking and 17 payment apps:
  - The apps are "ill-equipped" for abusers who simply use the normal screens. Those screens allowed covert surveillance and harassing messages.
  - The key threat is a partner who is a *legitimate, logged-in user*, not a hacker.
  - **HIGH.** [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/bellini)
- A CHI 2024 paper on consumer complaints notes that banking apps "can facilitate the surveillance of a complainant's expenditure through online interfaces or monitoring alerts". **MEDIUM** (abstract only). [arXiv 2403.13944](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13944v1)
- ANZ's design work notes that real-time transaction notices and payment descriptions can be used to stalk or threaten. **MEDIUM.** [ANZ Bluenotes, 2023](https://bluenotes.anz.com/posts/2023/10/anz-banking-preventing-financial-abuse)
- Australia's Centre for Women's Economic Safety ("Designed to Disrupt", 2022) asks the finance sector to adopt **"Safety by Design"**, adapted from the eSafety Commissioner. One recommendation is separate logins and portals for each joint account holder. **MEDIUM** (summary only; the report PDF returned 403). [Parliament of Australia inquiry, ch. 4](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Committees/Joint/Corporations_and_Financial_Services/FinancialAbuse/Report/Chapter_4_-_Safety_by_design_and_industry_wide_options_for_reform)
- A UK co-design study ("Designing Out Economic Abuse", 2025) with survivors and five major banks prioritised two things:
  - detecting unusual account patterns
  - joint account protections, including **needing both customers' consent for certain actions**

  **MEDIUM** (search summary only; the PDF blocked automated access). [City St George's repository](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/36260/1/Designing%20Out%20Economic%20Abuse%20Report%202025.pdf)

**Unpaid care work.**
- Advice pieces stress that the at-home partner's retirement savings and personal money should be agreed in advance, and that a single income leaves little room for error.
- **LOW to MEDIUM.** [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/high-school-student-plan-become-152444144.html)

### What we suggest: design red lines

These follow directly from the warnings above. Each is phrased as a rule FincWin must never break.

1. **The app never sends one partner a notice about the other's individual spending.** Approval requests are the only exception, and the person spending sends them by choice. Refuge names threshold spending alerts as an abuse tactic (HIGH).
2. **No approval or limit ever applies to a person's own account or personal money.** Limits apply only to joint everyday money.
3. **One limit applies to every member equally.** No per-person limits, so a limit cannot target the partner who does not earn. ("Imposing spend limits" is named in Refuge's warning.)
4. **Turning a limit on needs every member to agree. Turning it off needs only one.** Nobody can be locked into an agreement they no longer want.
5. **No record of refusals or of "went ahead anyway".**
   - A "Not now" closes the request.
   - The app keeps no tally, no history list and no "broke the agreement" flag.
   - Wording stays declarative: "Needs {name}'s OK", never "denied" or "not allowed".
6. **Personal money is private.**
   - The other partner never sees its line items.
   - At most, they see a total, and only if the owner chooses to share it (this extends 08.1's existing privacy rule D-17).
7. **Leaving a group never needs anyone's permission.** Your own records leave with you, in line with GDPR portability and erasure.
8. **No words that rank people:**
   - not "provider" or "dependent"
   - not "allowance" or "pocket money" in default copy
   - not "permission"

   Use names and "personal money".
9. **A neutral "Money and safety" help page.** It explains what each person can see, how to leave, and how to reach national support lines by region. The phase owner must approve the wording, and it must not make any claim that the app protects anyone.

## 3. How existing apps handle this

### What the evidence says

| App | How sharing works | Approval or limits | What users and reviewers say | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Honeydue** | Each person chooses, account by account, what the partner sees. About 60% of accounts are shared at some level and 40% are not | Monthly category limits, with a notice at 75% (adjustable) and when over; the listing says "you and your partner" are notified | Praised for privacy controls. Bill splitting is little used (~5%) and is "really just a way to remind your partner". Complaints about bank sync and weak support | MEDIUM ([Experian](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/honeydue-app-review/), [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/finance/learn/honeydue-app-review), [Honeydue help](https://support.honeydue.com/en/articles/3179800-how-do-i-add-a-spending-limit-or-budget)) |

| **Goodbudget** | One shared household login on several devices; manual envelopes | Envelope amounts act as soft limits | Simple, but there is no personal privacy: a shared login means a shared view | MEDIUM ([Goodbudget](https://goodbudget.com/?p=12636)) |

| **Revolut joint** | Both holders have equal access; each gets their own card; each can withdraw or close "without the other's permission"; money is owned in equal parts | Limits follow each person's own plan, with no shared cap | (no review data) | HIGH ([Revolut terms](https://www.revolut.com/legal/joint-accounts/), [help](https://help.revolut.com/help/profile-and-plan/joint-accounts/can-both-account-holders-withdraw-from-a-joint-account/)) |
| **Monzo joint** | Joint account with pots that both holders can add to and take from; either holder can close a savings pot | None found | Long-requested feature, mostly praised | MEDIUM ([Monzo blog](https://monzo.com/blog/2018/12/10/joint-pots)) |

**Patterns:**
- No mainstream adult app offers "partner must OK this purchase" between adults. Approval flows exist only for children. **MEDIUM** (absence of evidence).
- Banks treat joint holders as equals with independent access. So FincWin's approval is necessarily a **recorded agreement**, not a control, which is already decided.
- Privacy choice account by account (Honeydue, YNAB) is valued. All-visible (Monarch) is a known gap that users ask about.

### What we suggest

- Copy Honeydue's per-account sharing idea. FincWin already has it in spirit, since personal lines are private under D-17.
- Do **not** copy Greenlight's decline-with-note or its "child is notified of a decline" framing.
- Copy Revolut's equal footing. Any member can request, any member can answer, and the same rules apply to all.

## 4. Design implications for FincWin

### 4.1 How the existing rules treat a non-earner today

This comes from the approved 08.1-22 answers.

| Rule | A joint account's money for a member who pays nothing in | Result for the non-earner |
|---|---|---|
| What each person brings | New pay-ins are split by who paid them in (Q3 = B); money carried from before is split equally | Only half of the carried money; **$0 of this month's pay** |
| Same as the bill split | Follows the bill split; a 100/0 split gives a $0 share (see HH-24, Tayo) | **$0** if the earner pays every bill |
| A set spending split | The group's shares | Works, if the group sets it |
| Personal accounts | Always the owner's own | An earner's account that the partner uses gives the partner **$0** |

**Conclusion (HIGH; read straight from the approved rules):** with today's defaults, a one-earner couple with one joint account gets a fair answer only under "A set spending split". Every other path tells the at-home partner **Not yet** for small items. That is a wrong answer in our core feature.

### 4.2 Which rule and setup each arrangement maps to

| Real arrangement | Setup | Rule | What is new |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Earner pays a regular sum into the partner's own account ("personal money" or "housekeeping") | Separate accounts | What each person brings | A **payment to a member**: out for the payer (not spending), in for the receiver; counted once in Household |
| B. A set sum given once (cash or a transfer) | Separate or Mixed | What each person brings | Same payment type, one-off; it counts as money carried from before |
| C. Both spend from one joint account | One joint account | **A set spending split, equal shares** (new default when one member brings no pay-ins) | A one-earner default, plus a warning when any member's share is $0 |
| D. Partner spends from the earner's own account (second card or shared login) | Mixed | Any | An **"Used by both"** mark on a personal account, so it counts like joint everyday. The owner sets it, and the other member is told when it changes |
| E. Mix (joint bills + personal money + maybe D) | Mixed | Set split, or What each person brings | All of the above |
| F. The non-earner holds the purse (Japan style) | One joint account or Mixed | Set split | Nothing new. Role-neutral rules cover it |

**Is a new setup type needed?** We suggest **no**:
- One-earner is a fact the engine can see (one member has no pay-ins in the logged months). It is not a separate shape of accounts.
- A new "One earner" setup would label people, which red line 8 forbids.
- What is new:
  1. the **personal money** idea and the member-to-member payment
  2. the one-earner default and the $0-share warning
  3. the "Used by both" mark

### 4.3 How "Just me" should work for a non-earner

- **Their own money** = their personal accounts. That includes personal money received from the other member and any small income of their own (for example a benefit or a side job). It is counted like any personal money, and it is private.
- **Plus their slice of joint money** under the group's rule (with the one-earner default, an equal share).
- **Plus their slice of any "Used by both" account.**
- **If all of this is $0**, the answer does not say "Not yet" with no reason. It says the plain fact, for example: "Your share of the joint money is $0 under the bill split." It offers to open "How spending is shared". It must not suggest the person "should" change anything.
- A one-off set sum with no new money due gives **Not yet** with **no ready date**. The answer says plainly that no money is due in the records.

### 4.4 How approval limits should work, so they cannot be used for control

They apply only to **joint everyday** accounts, and to "Used by both" accounts if the owner answers Q7 that way.

| Safeguard | Rule |
|---|---|
| Mutual set-up | A limit starts only when **every** member has said yes. Until then it reads "Waiting for {name}". |
| Equal | One limit amount for the whole group. No per-person limits. |
| Easy exit | **Any** member can turn the limit off at once. The others get a plain notice ("{name} turned off the spend limit"). Undo does not switch it back on; it sends a new set-up request. |
| Own money is free | Personal accounts and personal money are never checked against a limit. A check paid from personal money never mentions approval. |
| Verdict first | Approval is shown only on a Fits answer. A Not yet answer never asks for an OK. |
| No tally | Replies are OK or Not now. A request closes on a reply or after 7 days (owner to confirm). No history list, no counts, no "went ahead anyway" flag. |
| Who answered | In a group of 3 or more, the requester sees "Not now", not who said it. In a group of 2 it is obvious, so the wording stays neutral ("Not now for this one"). |
| Spender chooses to ask | A request is sent only when the person checking taps "Ask {name}". The app never sends one on its own. |
| No spending alerts | Nothing tells a member when another spends, under or over the limit. |
| Copy | Declarative: "Fits. Needs {name}'s OK." Never "permission", "denied" or "not allowed". |

### 4.5 Personal money (the one new idea)

- The group may set a **personal money** amount per month for each member. By default it is **the same for every member**, the earner included. A different amount per person needs every member to agree.
- It is recorded as a **payment to a member**:
  - The payer's side: money out, not spending, not shown as a purchase.
  - The receiver's side: money in, personal.
  - Household: counted once. The payer's out and the receiver's in cancel.
- Once received, it is the receiver's **private** money: no line items for others, no limits, no approval.
- Missed or late payments are not flagged to anyone except the receiver, as a plain fact in their own records ("No personal money in from Sam this month"). This needs owner sign-off (see Q9).

## 5. Right-answer test cases

**Shared set-up**, as in HH-04:
- pay on the 1st, today 15 November 2026
- no safety net, no overdraft
- Sam earns $5,000 a month after tax; Alex earns $0

**Floor:** the hand sums below set the floor to $0 to keep the sums plain. When these become fixtures, the case writer adds the standard floor (a tenth of a month's pay-in) and re-works any answer near the line, as the 08.1 cases did.

| ID | Situation | Numbers | Check | Expected answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## 6. Open questions for the owner

Each question has 2–4 options. The recommended option is marked **(recommended)**.

**Q1. Do we add a new setup type for one-earner homes?**
- A. **(recommended)** No. Keep the three setups. The app notices when one member has no pay-ins and uses a fairer default (Q2).
- B. Add a fourth setup, "One earner".
- C. Add a fourth spending rule, "Personal money first".

**Q2. What should the default spending rule be when one member brings no money in?**
- A. **(recommended)** "A set spending split" at equal shares, with a line that says so.
- B. Keep today's defaults, but warn when someone's share is $0.
- C. Make the group choose during setup, with no default.

**Q3. How is money paid from one member to another recorded?**
- A. **(recommended)** As a "payment to a member". It is money out (not spending) for the payer and money in for the receiver, and it is counted once in Household.
- B. As ordinary income and ordinary spending.
- C. Not tracked. The receiver types in what they have.

**Q4. What do we call that money in the app?**
- A. **(recommended)** "Personal money".
- B. "Allowance".
- C. "Money for {name}".
- D. "Spending money".

**Q5. Who can have personal money, and how much?**
- A. **(recommended)** Every member, the same amount by default. Different amounts need everyone to agree.
- B. Each member's amount is set freely.
- C. Only members who do not earn.

**Q6. How do we handle a partner who spends from the earner's own account?**
- A. **(recommended)** The account owner can mark it "Used by both". It then counts like joint everyday money, and the other member is told if the mark is removed.
- B. Not supported. The partner's "Just me" is only their personal money.
- C. The partner can add the account as joint themselves.

**Q7. Who can turn a spend limit on or off?**
- A. **(recommended)** Turning it on needs every member. Any one member can turn it off. One limit applies to everyone.
- B. Any member can turn it on or off alone.
- C. Only the group owner.

**Q8. What happens after someone answers "Not now"?**
- A. **(recommended)** The request closes. Nothing else is stored: no history, no count, no "went ahead anyway" flag.
- B. Keep a list of past requests and answers for both people.
- C. Remind the requester later.

**Q9. Should anyone be told when personal money does not arrive?**
- A. **(recommended)** Only the receiver, as a plain fact in their own records.
- B. Both members.
- C. Nobody.

**Q10. Do we add a "Money and safety" help page?**
- A. **(recommended)** Yes. It covers what each person can see, how to leave the group with your records, and national support lines by region. The wording needs owner sign-off.
- B. Yes, plus a quick-exit button on that page.
- C. No. Privacy policy text only.

**Q11. How is a non-earner's small own income counted (a benefit, or a side job)?**
- A. **(recommended)** As their own money, wherever it lands, like any pay-in.
- B. The group decides per income line whether it is shared.
- C. Benefits for children always count as joint money.

**Q12. Can spend limits apply to an account marked "Used by both" (Q6), not just joint accounts?**
- A. **(recommended)** Yes, with the same safeguards (all must agree; any one can turn it off).
- B. No. Joint everyday accounts only.

## 7. Gaps and caveats

- No current, comparable EU-wide data on how couples manage money (LOW).
- No survey found on how common a regular personal sum ("allowance") is among stay-at-home partners today. The UK figure of 3% for women born after 1980 covers the housekeeping pattern only.
- Two key reports could not be read in full: the CWES "Designed to Disrupt" PDF (403) and the City St George's 2025 "Designing Out Economic Abuse" PDF (blocked). Their claims here come from summaries and the Parliament of Australia inquiry, so they are marked MEDIUM.
- App features change often. Re-check Honeydue, Zeta, Monarch and YNAB before quoting them in copy or marketing.
- No source checked whether FincWin's own records can already represent a payment between members. The planner should check the code.
