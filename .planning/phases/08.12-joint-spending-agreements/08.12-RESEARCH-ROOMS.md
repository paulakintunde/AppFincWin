# 08.12 Research: rooms inside a household (private and selective sharing)

**Researched:** 2026-11-21 (fifth research round for 08.12)
**For:** the owner's idea of 2026-11-21. A household can hold smaller private groups, like rooms in a house. Each room is seen only by its members. Money must still be counted once across rooms that overlap, and in Decide.
**Builds on:** Phase 8 (Household), 08.1-22 and 08.1-23 (spending rule, joint money counted once, joint purposes), and the four 08.12 research files with `08.12-OWNER-ANSWERS.md` (free access, personal money, the spend limit, top-ups, credit, talk it over, and the safety red lines).
**Method:** reading the repo (`C:/dev/fincwin-081b`) and web searches on generic topics. Confidence on each key claim:
- **HIGH**: the provider's own help page or terms, a statutory code, or a peer-reviewed paper.
- **MEDIUM**: a review site, an app-store listing, a news report, or a help page that looks older than the app.
- **LOW**: a forum post, a single user review, or no direct source.

Each part keeps **What the evidence says** apart from **What we suggest**.

---

## 0. Summary in plain words

1. **Today a person can be in only one household, and a line is either personal or shared with the whole household.** There is no way to share a line or a joint account with only some members. So the parents' private arrangement and the two-roommate joint account cannot be written down without everyone in the household seeing them.
2. **Other apps solve this in one of three ways.** Group apps (Splitwise, Revolut Group Bills, Tricount, Splid) let a person be in many separate groups. Couples apps (Honeydue) let each person choose what to share, account by account. Family plans (YNAB Together) share a subscription, with each budget shared only with the people chosen. Monarch shares everything with everyone and cannot hide an account (HIGH, its own help page).
3. **The known pain points are:** balances that differ between the group view and the "all my balances" view (Splitwise, HIGH), "simplify debts" confusing people (Splitwise, HIGH), and private settings that do not really hide anything (Monarch, HIGH).
4. **Safety matters more here than anywhere else.** Watching a partner's spending through shared banking access is a named form of economic abuse (HIGH). A room that one person can always see into, or a list of "who is in which room", would be a monitoring tool.
5. **We suggest "rooms", built so that today's household is simply the first room.** Every household gets one "everyone" room. "Shared" stops being a yes/no switch and becomes "shared in room R". Each room has its own members, joint accounts, spending rule, split rule, spend limit, personal money and settle-up. A line or an account lives in exactly one room, so it is counted once.
6. **Nobody sees a room they are not in.** Not its name, not a count, not its money. The household owner has no power over rooms. Leaving a room never needs anyone's permission.
7. **Decide gains rooms as scopes.** The scope picker becomes "Just me", then one choice per room you are in. "Household" becomes the everyone room. A Just me check counts your part of every room's joint money once, because each account is in one room only.
8. **Size: L.** It touches the row security of every shared table, the events, the export and the privacy test. We suggest its own phase after 08.12. But 08.12's new tables (spend limit, personal money, requests) should be keyed by room from day one, with only the everyone room in use, so nothing has to be moved later.

## 1. What exists today and what it can't express

### 1.1 What exists (read from the repo)

**One household per person.**
- `household_members` has the primary key `(household_id, user_id)`, so the table itself would allow more than one household (`20260922000100_household_of_one.sql`). But the rules forbid it: a person in a household with other members must leave first (08-CONTEXT D-06), and `redeem_invite` refuses with `in_other_household` (`20261122000500_household_invites.sql:842`).
- More than one household per person is deferred as HHX-01, and per-account sharing levels as HHX-02 (`REQUIREMENTS.md:428-429`).
- At most 6 members per household (D-10). Roles are labels only; only the owner has powers (invite, remove, rename, currency) (D-31).

**Personal unless shared, enforced by the database.**
- `transactions`, `accounts` and `recurring_series` carry `is_shared`. Row security reads `household_id in (select user_household_ids()) and (is_shared or created_by = auth.uid())` (`20261122000100_household_privacy_wave1.sql:64-89`).
- Debts, dismissals and payer prompts are creator-only. Statement periods follow their card account (D-43).
- `user_household_ids()` appears about 300 times across 57 migrations, and there are 73 `create policy` statements and 117 pgTAP files. This is the size of the row-security surface that rooms would touch.

**Joint accounts and splits.**
- A joint account is `accounts.is_shared = true`. Its new outgoing lines start shared; its balance is seen by every member (D-13, 08-10). Money paid into a joint account is not auto-shared (08-10).
- A shared line stores its own split in `transaction_splits` (largest remainder, 08-03). Category defaults are in `household_split_defaults`. Each member can set their own category on a shared line (`transaction_member_categories`).
- A shared line paid from a joint account makes no owing. Owing comes only from shared lines one person paid from their own money (D-17).

**Settle-up.**
- Balances are shown per pair, with no debt simplification (D-23). A settle-up covers chosen lines, is pending until the receiver confirms, lapses at day 14, and can be undone for 24 hours (D-19..D-24, D-42). Tables: `settlements`, `settlement_lines`, `settlement_nudges` (08-11).

**Events and notices.**
- `household_events` has an `audience uuid[]` column: null means every member, otherwise only those users (`20261122000200_household_members_v2.sql:174-183`). This is a ready-made way to send a notice to only some members.

**Names and privacy.**
- Each member picks a display name or a colour name (`name_hidden`, D-32). Everything that names a member goes through one resolver.
- The "not shared" member in Decide: a partner counts in a Household check only with both opt-ins (`decide_partner_optins`, pair based) and then as one rounded personal figure (`partner_figures`). Anyone else is named in the caveat "Only your money is counted. Sam hasn't shared theirs." (08.1-08, 08.1-10).

**Decide scopes.**
- Two scopes: "Just me" and "Household · {name}". Decide always reads the "Mine" rows (my records plus my share of shared lines) (08-25).
- 08.1-22/23 added one spending rule per household (`household_spend_rules`, primary key `household_id`, rules bring / custom / bill, setups separate / joint / mixed), joint purposes (bills / savings / everyday), and a joint safety net. A joint account is counted once and split by the rule.
- 08.12 adds (owner answers): "Both can use all of it" (rule `shared`, split `whole`), personal money, the spend limit (one for everyone, all agree to start, any one stops it), top-ups, personal credit for the gap, instalments, "talk it over" and the "Money and safety" page.

**Leaving.** A leaver's personal records move to a new household of one, and each shared line they were on becomes a personal copy of their share (D-26). Open balances stay as "Sam (left)" (D-25).

**Privacy test.** pgTAP 101 is a two-user suite (426 lines, 147 assertions): member B cannot read, edit, get live updates for, RPC-read, view-read or export A's personal rows, while A's shared rows stay visible (08-07). `src/data/exportManifest.ts` is tied to 101's table list by a Jest guard.

### 1.2 What it can't express

| Owner's example | Why today's model can't hold it |
|---|---|
| Parents' private joint arrangement that the kids don't see | Any shared line or joint account is seen by every member, kids included. The only private choice is "personal", which belongs to one person, not two. |
| A whole-family pot shared with the kids, next to the parents' own pot | Both pots would be visible to everyone, and one spending rule covers the whole household. The parents' pot and the family pot can't have different rules or limits. |
| Four roommates: all split rent, two share a joint account | The two-person joint account would be seen by all four, and its everyday money would be split by the flat's rule. |
| Two other roommates share a car or a subscription | Their line, if shared, shows to all four. A 50/50 split with the others at 0% is possible, but the others still see it. |
| Overlapping pairs (Xia shares with Wes and also with Yan) | No grouping below the household exists, so overlap can't be stated at all. |
| Someone leaves the pair arrangement but stays in the flat | Leaving exists only for the whole household. |
| Joining a second, separate household (for example a parent's household and a flatshare) | D-06 forbids it. Not part of this idea, but option (b) below would address it. |

## 2. How other apps model groups inside groups

### What the evidence says

**Many separate groups per person (group-split apps)**
- **Splitwise.** A person can be in many groups, and can also add "non-group" expenses with one friend. Non-group bills count in the person's total on the home screen but in no group's total, so a friend's combined balance and each group's balance can differ. The current listing says you can see total balances with a person across multiple groups and private expenses. (HIGH for the help-page rule, MEDIUM for the listing.) [S1, S2, S3]
- **Splitwise "simplify debts"** changes who pays whom within a group and across friendships while keeping each person's total the same. It is off by default per group, users find it hidden, and Splitwise itself says it "has had trouble" making it clear and that debt-transfer features "can sometimes cause serious confusion". (HIGH, Splitwise's own help and forum replies.) [S4, S5, S6]
- **Revolut Group Bills.** You create a named group with its own currency, add members (including people without Revolut), add bills, and settle by in-app payment or "Mark as paid". **You can leave a group only when you neither owe nor are owed.** (HIGH, Revolut help and terms.) [S7, S8]
- **Tricount and Settle Up.** Per-group tools; each group settles with the fewest transfers. No cross-group total was found in their listings. (MEDIUM.) [S9, S10, S11]
- **Splid.** Groups for splitting bills, offline or synced, multi-currency; extra groups are sold as packs. (MEDIUM, store listing.) [S12]
- **Flatastic** (flatshare chores, shopping and expenses for the whole flat) and **Homey** (family chores and allowance, parent account protected by a password). Both treat the home as one group; no sub-groups found. (MEDIUM.) [S13, S14, S15]

**Per-account sharing (couples apps)**
- **Honeydue.** For each account, the owner picks "Share both balances and transactions", "Share balances only" or "Nothing at all", and can hide single transactions. (HIGH, Honeydue help.) [S16, S17] The first 08.12 research found that about 40% of Honeydue accounts are not shared (MEDIUM).
- **Monarch.** Every household member sees every connected account and transaction; you cannot hide an account. A "hidden" account is still visible from settings, and Monarch says it is "exploring" adding this. (HIGH, Monarch help.) [S18, S19]
- **Copilot.** A partner can be given access to the same account; a joint bank account should be added once to avoid duplicates. Users are still asking for selective sharing and separate shared vs personal budgets. (HIGH for the help page, MEDIUM for the requests.) [S20, S21, S22]

**Many budgets, each shared with chosen people**
- **YNAB Together.** Up to 6 people share one subscription; each has their own login and can create plans and share each plan with chosen members. YNAB's example: a "Household Plan" shared with a partner but kept private from a grandparent and the teenagers. A shared plan gives full edit access, and any bank account added to it is seen by everyone it is shared with. Members must be 13+ in the US and 16+ elsewhere. (HIGH, YNAB pages.) [S23, S24]

**Bank features**
- **Monzo.** Shared Tabs (now "Monzo Split") are group expense trackers between a set of people, separate from joint accounts. Pots on a joint account are seen by both holders. (MEDIUM: much of the detail is from 2018-2019 posts.) [S25, S26, S27]
- **Starling.** Spaces are pots inside an account. A joint account (two people only) has shared Spaces. A personal Space can be given to someone else through a "Connected Card" that spends only from that Space. (HIGH for the feature pages, MEDIUM for how they combine.) [S28, S29, S30]
- **Revolut and other banks:** joint holders see the same whole balance (from the free-access research, HIGH).

**Families and kids**
- **Greenlight.** Parents see the child's card transactions, block categories, and turn the card on or off. Kids see their own balances. (MEDIUM, reviews.) [S31, S32]
- **FamZoo.** Parents see every child account and set rules per child; kids can ask for money with a reason. (MEDIUM.) [S33]
- **Apple Family Sharing.** Under 13, a child can't create an account alone; a parent or guardian makes it. **Any member over 13 can leave the family group at any time**, unless they are 13-17 with Screen Time set up, when they need the organiser's permission. Age rules vary by country. Ask to Buy is on by default under 13. (HIGH, Apple support.) [S34, S35]
- **Google Family Link.** At 13 (or the local age), a child is told they can choose to manage their own account; under 18 a parent's approval is still needed to stop supervision. This was publicly disputed in January 2026 and Google appears to have changed course. (HIGH for the help page, MEDIUM for the dispute.) [S36, S37, S38]
- **Google family payment method.** Only the family manager controls it, and the manager sees every purchase made with it through Play billing. (HIGH, Google help.) [S39]

### What users complain about

| Complaint | Where | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Balances differ between the "all" view and the group view, so people think the app is wrong | Splitwise help article "my dashboard balances are different than my group" [S3] | HIGH |
| Simplifying debts across people is hard to understand and hard to turn off | Splitwise forum and help [S4, S5, S6] | HIGH |
| Settling through a friend creates an untidy "non-group" bucket | Splitwise user review [S1] | LOW |
| "Hidden" account is not really hidden | Monarch help [S19] | HIGH |
| No way to keep shared and personal budgets apart with a partner | Copilot feature requests [S21, S22] | MEDIUM |
| A partner already using the app must delete their account to join yours | Honeydue review [S40] | MEDIUM |
| Joint account added twice gives double transactions | Copilot help [S20] | HIGH |

### Privacy and safety

- **Monitoring spending is a named form of abuse.** NNEDV's Tech Safety lists watching spending history through shared banking access. Surviving Economic Abuse lists "economic restriction", including monitoring spending through an account. (HIGH.) [S41, S42]
- **Fintech is used for it.** Peer-reviewed work documents abusers using monitoring alerts and online interfaces to watch a partner's spending, and calls on fintech to design against it. (HIGH.) [S43, S44]
- **It is common among young people.** An Ipsos survey for Surviving Economic Abuse found 19% of UK women and girls aged 16-24 had faced economic abuse through a digital financial product from a partner or ex in the past year. (MEDIUM, news report of the survey.) [S45]
- **Agreed budget vs tracking.** Australian government guidance separates a shared, agreed budget from closely tracking a partner's spending on a set amount, which it calls financial abuse. (HIGH.) [S46]
- **Children.** The UK Children's Code asks services to respect "the child's evolving capacity", treats age bands as a guide, and says that if you offer parental controls you must give the child age-appropriate information about them. Its notes say 10-12 year olds vary a lot in how they take parental involvement, and that some 13-15 year olds push back against it. (HIGH, ICO statutory code.) [S47, S48]
- **Roommates.** No study was found on roommates hiding two-person money from the rest of a flat. The group-split apps all give private groups by default (a group is seen only by its members), which is indirect evidence that people expect it. (LOW, no direct source.)

### What this means for FincWin

- The pattern that works across group apps is **named groups seen only by their members**. FincWin's twist is that the groups live inside one household and must not count money twice. No app found does that; Splitwise comes closest by adding up a friend's balance across groups.
- The per-account choice (Honeydue) is good for privacy but has no place for a group's rules or settle-up.
- The safety evidence argues against any role that sees all rooms, and against showing who is in which room.
- The child evidence argues for telling a child what a parent can see, and for privacy that grows with age.

## 3. Model options

### What the evidence says

Summarised from part 2: named groups per person are the norm in split apps (HIGH); per-account sharing works for couples (HIGH); "share with chosen people" per budget is YNAB's family model (HIGH); full visibility (Monarch) is a known limit (HIGH).

### Options for FincWin

| | (a) Rooms inside a household | (b) Several separate households per person | (c) Per-account (and per-line) member lists | (d) Rooms, with today's household as the "everyone" room (our pick) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| How it works | Named sub-groups inside one household. Each has members, joint accounts, spending rule, split rule, spend limit, personal money, settle-up. | Lift D-06. A person joins 2+ households. Nothing links them. | Each joint account and each shared line has its own list of members who can see it. No named group. | As (a), but the whole household is itself a room (`kind = everyone`). "Shared" becomes "shared in room R". |
| Fits the owner's idea | Yes | Partly. Parents would need a second household just for two of them, with its own owner, currency and invite. | No names, no rules per group. | Yes |
| Counting once | Easy: each account and line is in one room. | Hard. Nothing links the households, and a person's personal records live in only one of them (every row has one `household_id`). Decide Just me would have to read across households. | Easy per account; but rules (spend rule, limit) have no home, so each account would carry its own. | Easy, same as (a). |
| Privacy | Room members only | Strong (separate households) | Strong but scattered: many small lists | Room members only |
| Subscription and pricing | One household plan (Household(6)) | Each household is a separate plan, or a person pays twice. Breaks the 09-CONTEXT tiers. | One plan | One plan |
| Migration cost | M-L. New tables; `room_id` on shared tables; backfill. | L. Move-in, leave, Decide, export, currency, invites all assume one household; personal rows need a "home household". | M. | Row-security complexity | One helper `my_room_ids()`; every shared-row policy changes from `is_shared` to `room_id in (my rooms)`. | `user_household_ids()` already returns a set, so policies mostly hold, but every RPC that picks "the" household must change. | Array membership checks on every table (`auth.uid() = any(members)`), harder to index and test. | Same as (a). |
| Reuse of 08.1 / 08.12 work | Spending rule etc. move from household to room. | Reused per household. | Rule per account; the group rule idea is lost. | Spending rule etc. keyed by room; today's rows become the everyone room's. |
| Main risk | Leaks through side channels (events, totals, caveats, export). | Personal records split across households; double counting across households; pricing. | Rules and settle-up with no home; many tiny lists to manage. | Same as (a). |

**Other patterns looked at**
- **Splitwise-style "non-group" pair lines.** A line shared with one person and no group. In FincWin this is just a two-person room created in two taps from the line. Worth offering as a shortcut inside (d), not as a separate model.
- **Starling-style card on a pot.** Lets someone spend from one pot only. That is a bank feature; FincWin records, it does not hold money.
- **Admin who sees every room.** Rejected on safety grounds (part 4).

### What we suggest

**(d): rooms, with the household as the everyone room.** It matches the owner's picture, keeps one household, one plan and one currency, and turns today's yes/no "shared" into "shared in this room" without changing the meaning of any existing row. Every 08.1 and 08.12 setting (rule, purpose, safety net, free access, limit, personal money, talk it over) becomes a room setting. The everyone room behaves exactly like today's household.

Keep (b) (HHX-01) for a different need: one person in two unrelated homes. It is not needed for the owner's examples.

## 4. Visibility rules

### What the evidence says
- Split apps show a group only to its members (MEDIUM, from their design).
- Watching a partner's spending, and a third party seeing into private arrangements, are abuse risks (HIGH, part 2).
- Children should be told what parents can see, and privacy should grow with age (HIGH, ICO).
- Over 13, Apple lets a member leave a family group at any time unless Screen Time is set (HIGH).

### What we suggest

**Who sees what**

| | Member of the room | Household member not in the room | Household owner not in the room |
|---|---|---|---|
| That the room exists | Yes | **No** | **No** |
| Room name | Yes | No | No |
| Room members | Yes | No | No |
| Joint accounts in the room and their balances | Yes | No | No |
| Lines shared in the room | Yes | No | No |
| Room spending rule, spend limit, personal money settings | Yes (personal money line items stay private to the receiver, as today) | No | No |
| Balances between members inside the room | Yes, per pair | No | No |
| Room notices | Yes (audience = room members) | No | No |

- **No "2 private rooms" line.** A count tells a controlling partner that something is happening, and tells kids that the parents have a room. It also helps nobody: you can't act on a room you are not in. We suggest showing nothing.
- **The household owner has no power over rooms.** Owner powers stay as they are: invite, remove, rename, currency.
- **The everyone room** is seen by everyone in the household and behaves like today.
- **A personal line stays personal.** Rooms add sharing; they never widen personal visibility.
- **Names.** One display name and colour per person across all rooms (D-32). A per-room name would let a person be "Rust" in one room and "Sam" in another, which reveals the link anyway.
- **The "not shared" caveat** names only members of the room being checked.

**Creating and joining a room**
- Any household member can create a room and invite household members to it. **Nobody is added without saying yes on their own phone.** (Same pattern as "a plan in both names" in round 3.)
- Rooms hold 2 to 6 people (the household cap).

**Leaving and removal**
- Anyone can leave a room at any time without permission (red line 7). Room members get one notice ("Xia left Wes and Xia"). Nobody outside the room is told.
- Removal from the household removes the person from every room.
- A person cannot be removed from a room by another member, only from the household by the owner. (A room "kick" would be one more lever for control; leaving and re-forming the room covers the honest case.)

**Parents and kids**

The existing model has no age rule and no parent role (roles are labels, D-31). We suggest:
- **The same privacy for everyone in the app, kids included.** A parent cannot see a child's personal records or a room the parent is not in. This matches FincWin's core rule (personal unless shared) and the Children's Code idea of evolving capacity.
- **Children too young for their own login appear only as a person named on lines** (for example a "School trips" category), not as members. They have no account and no data of their own.
- **The minimum age for a login** is an owner question (Q4). Evidence: Apple and Google treat 13 as the line for own accounts; YNAB requires 13+ in the US and 16+ elsewhere; in the EU the age of digital consent is 13-16 by country. The safest launch choice is 16+, then revisit.
- **A teen in a room with parents** has the same rights as any member: they see the room, can leave it, and can't be watched outside it. The app tells the teen, in the room's info, exactly what the other members can see (Children's Code).

**Safety checks**
- No one has a view across all rooms. There is no "who is in which room" screen.
- The "Money and safety" page gains one line: "A room is seen only by its members. You can leave a room at any time." (Wording to sign-off; no "should".)
- A room cannot be made secretly: every member said yes.

## 5. Counting once and Decide

### What the evidence says
- Banks show a joint balance once, to every holder (HIGH, free-access research).
- Splitwise shows a friend's total across groups, and people get confused when it differs from a group's figure (HIGH).

### What we suggest

**The one rule that keeps money counted once:** every account, line and series is either personal or in **exactly one room**. A joint account belongs to one room. A line paid from a joint account is in that account's room. A shared line paid from personal money is in the room the person picks.

**Decide scopes**
- The picker becomes: **Just me**, then **one choice per room you are in**, with the everyone room labelled with the household name ("Home"). This replaces "Household". No separate "This room" concept is needed, because the household scope is just the everyone room's scope.
- **Just me** counts: your personal money, plus, for each room you are in, your part of that room's joint money under that room's rule ("Both can use all of it" counts the whole pot). Since each account is in one room, no account is counted twice.
- **A room scope** counts: that room's joint money, plus opted-in members' personal figures, under that room's rule. It never counts joint money from another room, even if the viewer is in that room too.
- **Answers never add across scopes.** A room answer and a Just me answer are separate questions. JA-01 holds per pot: two people may both hear "Fits" for the same shared money only when the answer says it is shared, and a recorded buy lowers the pot for every member of that room.

**Room settings (from 08.1 and 08.12, now per room)**
- **Spending rule and setup:** per room (bring, custom, bill, shared).
- **Joint purposes and the joint safety net:** per account, so already per room.
- **Free access:** per room. "Sam can spend from it too" names only members of the account's room.
- **Personal money:** an agreement inside a room, paid between two members of that room. It stays private to the receiver.
- **Spend limit:** one per room, same for every member of that room. All members of that room agree to start it; any one of them stops it. It applies only to money from that room.
- **Mixed checks:** own money first, then shared money. When two rooms' pots could pay, the "Paid from" pick decides; with no pick, the check uses the room whose money the person chose last, and the answer names it. (Owner detail for planning.)
- **Top-up asks:** go only to members of the account's room.
- **Talk it over:** a check can be shared only with a room whose members can already see every pot the check used. A Just me check that used the parents' room money can be shared with the parents' room, not with the family room. This stops a shared check from leaking another room's money.
- **"Not shared" caveat:** names only that room's members.

**Home figures**
- "Mine" stays my records plus my share of shared lines, across all my rooms.
- "Household" becomes per room: each room shows its own shared lines. The everyone room is the default.

## 6. Settle-up and splits across rooms

### What the evidence says
- Splitwise keeps group balances separate and adds them up per friend; simplifying across people confuses users (HIGH).
- Revolut won't let you leave a group with an open balance (HIGH). FincWin already chose differently: leaving with a balance is allowed after a warning (D-25).

### What we suggest
- **Balances are per pair, per room.** Inside a room, a pair's balance is already net of both directions (`pairBalances`).
- **Your pair view with one person shows one row per room you share,** and a total. Example: "Yan owes you $59: Flat $50, Xia and Yan $9."
- **Other room members see only their room's part.** Wes sees "Yan owes Xia $50" in Flat, never $59.
- **One payment can settle several rooms**, if both people choose "Settle both rooms together". The app writes one settlement record per room (each room sees only its own part), and the money moves once between the two accounts. No settlement is ever simplified through a third person (D-23 stays).
- **Netting across rooms is allowed only between the same two people and only when both say yes**, because otherwise a room's record would not match what the person thinks they paid.
- **Splits:** a line's split is among that room's members only. Members can be set to 0% (D-30).

## 7. Right-answer cases (RM-)

**Common rules for these cases**
- Figures are "money left" in the person's home currency, before the standard floor and safety net. The planner re-works near-the-line cases with the standard floor, as for TB- and IN-.
- Rooms and rules are as listed in each setup.

### Family setup (F)
- Household "Home": Ada and Ben (parents), Cal (16, own login). Dee (11) has no login.
- Room **Home** (everyone room): Ada, Ben, Cal. Joint account "Family pot" $600, purpose everyday, rule "A set spending split": Ada 45%, Ben 45%, Cal 10%.
- Room **Parents**: Ada, Ben. Joint account "Parents joint" $4,000, purpose everyday, rule "Both can use all of it". Spend limit $500 (both agreed).
- Personal money left: Ada $2,000, Ben $1,000, Cal $200.

### Flat setup (R)
- Household "Flat 4B": Wes, Xia, Yan, Zoe.

## 8. Engine, data and row-security impact

### Data (all additive first, then a guarded switch)

**New tables**
- `rooms`: `id`, `household_id`, `kind` (`everyone` or `room`), `name`, `created_by`, `closed_at`, `version`. One `everyone` room per household, made by a backfill and by the household-creation trigger.
- `room_members`: `(room_id, user_id)`, `joined_at`, `left_at`. Everyone-room membership mirrors `household_members` through a trigger.
- `room_invites` (or reuse `household_invites` with a `room_id`): the yes-on-your-own-phone step.

**New column `room_id` (null = personal)**
- `accounts`, `transactions`, `recurring_series`: `room_id`. Backfill `room_id = everyone room` where `is_shared`. Keep `is_shared` as a generated column (`room_id is not null`) for a release, so old clients and views keep working.
- `transaction_splits`, `transaction_member_categories`: follow their line's room (no own column needed; policies join to the line).
- `household_split_defaults`, `household_spend_rules`, `safety_net_shares`: re-key from `household_id` to `room_id` (spend rules' primary key moves from `household_id` to `room_id`).
- `settlements`, `settlement_lines`, `settlement_nudges`: `room_id`. A cross-room settle-up is one group id with one settlement per room.
- 08.12's own new tables (spend limit, limit requests, personal money agreements, cover asks, talk-it-over shares): **key by `room_id` from the start.**

**Row security**
- New helper `my_room_ids()` (security definer, empty search path, like `user_household_ids()`).
- Every "own or shared" policy changes from `is_shared or created_by = auth.uid()` to `created_by = auth.uid() or room_id in (select my_room_ids())`. About 73 policies exist; the shared-row ones (transactions, accounts, series, splits, member categories, statement periods via card account, settlements, joint balances RPC, spend rules) are the ones that change.
- Every security-definer RPC that checks "is this row shared" (`apply_patches`, `share_transaction`, `bulk_share_transactions`, `make_line_private`, `set_line_split`, `hh_pair_owed`, `joint_account_balances`, `joint_account_month`, settle-up RPCs, undo RPCs) must check room membership instead. Refusals stay "not found" for rows in a room you are not in, so an error never reveals that a room exists (08-05 pattern).
- Realtime: `postgres_changes` already goes through row security; re-run the two-client spike with a three-client room case.

**Events and notices**
- Every `hh_event` call must pass `audience` = the room's members when the event is about a room's line, account, rule or settle-up. Today many calls pass null (everyone). **Each call site needs an audit** (plan task). A null audience on a room event is a leak.
- New kinds: `room_created`, `room_joined`, `room_left`, `room_closed`, `room_renamed`.
- Notice text names rooms only to members.

**Privacy test**
- pgTAP 101 grows a sister suite (for example 1xx_household_rooms_three_user): users A, B, C; rooms Everyone {A, B, C} and AB {A, B}. C cannot read, update, RPC-read, view-read, get live updates for, or export any AB row, account, split, settlement, event, rule or limit, and cannot learn AB exists (rooms select returns nothing, refusals are "not found"). After B leaves AB: B loses new AB lines and keeps only personal copies. Meta-test 102 classifies every table by room scope.

**Export manifest**
- Add `rooms` and `room_members` (only rooms the person is or was in), `room_id` on accounts, transactions and series. The Jest guard ties the manifest to the new suite's table list.

**Engine (pure)**
- `household/spendRule`, `jointMoney`, `pool`, `plan` and `decide/v3/householdMoney` take a room instead of a household. Just me loops over the viewer's rooms; a room scope takes one room. Property test: across all scopes, each account's money appears in at most one room's pool.
- `balances.pairBalances` groups by room; a new `pairAcrossRooms` adds the rows; `settleAcrossRooms` makes the per-room parts and the net amount.
- Talk-it-over leak check: `roomsThatCanSee(check)` = rooms whose members can see every pot the check used.

**UI**
- Rooms list and room screen (members, accounts, rules, balances); create-room and accept flows; leave-room confirm; scope picker with rooms; pair view with per-room rows; "Settle both rooms together"; move a line or account to another room (creator only, and only if no settle-up covers it, like D-15).

### Size

**L** (about 12-16 plans): migration and backfill, row-security rewrite, RPC audit, events audit, three-user privacy suite, engine changes, data layer, five or six screens, copy and sign-off.

### Where it belongs

- **Not inside 08.12's main build.** 08.12 is already large (free access, personal money, limit, top-ups, credit, instalments, talk it over, safety page), and a row-security rewrite in the same phase would put the privacy gate at risk.
- **But 08.12 should lay the floor (size S):** create `rooms` with one everyone room per household, and key every new 08.12 table by `room_id`. Then the rooms phase adds more rooms without moving 08.12's data.
- **Then its own phase** (for example "Household rooms"), right after 08.12, before launch if the owner wants it in v1.

## 10. Gaps and caveats

- No direct evidence was found on roommates hiding two-person money from a flat, or on how often families want a parents-only arrangement. The case rests on the owner's examples and on group apps' default privacy (LOW).
- Several sources are app-store listings or reviews of unknown date (Monzo Shared Tabs, Splid, Homey, Greenlight). Behaviour may have changed.
- Google's teen supervision rules were disputed in January 2026; the current rule was not confirmed.
- No minimum age for FincWin was found in the planning files. Adding child logins brings COPPA (US, under 13), GDPR consent ages (13-16) and the Children's Code. This needs the Phase 11 compliance check.
- RM-04's verdict is left to the engine on purpose; the case tests what is counted, not the number.
- The everyone-room backfill must run with user triggers off, like the 08-01 created_by backfill.
- The event audit (null audience) is the most likely leak. It needs its own test that fails on any room-scoped kind with a null audience.
