## Round 2 (2026-11-21): all answered, recommended option each time

These answers come from 08.12-RESEARCH-FREE-ACCESS.md (FA questions 1-8) and the questions held back from 08.12-RESEARCH-ARRANGEMENTS.md.

1. **Free access is a fifth choice: "Both can use all of it".**
   - It uses rule value `shared` and joint split `whole`.
   - Each person's Just me check counts the whole joint pot.
   - Household totals and the partner figure count the pot once.
   - JA-01 is restated: two people can both get "Fits", but only when the answer says the money is shared.
2. **Defaults stay as approved in 08.1, so no approved answer moves.**
   - When only one member brings money, "Both can use all of it" is suggested first, and the group picks.
   - This replaces the 08.1 small-fix wording "suggest an equal set split". In 08.1 the fix suggests a set split, because free access is not built until 08.12; in 08.12 the suggestion becomes free access first.
3. **About the partner, the answer says only that the money is shared and who else can spend it.** Open checks stay private.
4. **The shared pot drops for everyone when a buy or a planned item is recorded.** "Record as bought" gets a "Paid from" pick.
   - Still to check: whether planned joint lines reach `monthOut` today (FA-03).
5. **Mixed checks count the person's own money first, then the shared money.**
6. **The spend limit uses the safe version:**
   - one limit, the same for everyone;
   - it starts only when all agree, and any one person turns it off;
   - it applies per item paid from shared money, never from personal money;
   - "Not now" leaves no record.
7. **When one item uses more than half of the shared money, the answer adds a line, but the verdict does not change.** Example: "This uses more than half of the shared $1,000."
8. **Any member can change the sharing choice.** Everyone gets a before-and-after notice with Undo.
9. **The name is "Personal money".** It is a payment from one member to another: out (not spending) for the payer, in for the receiver, counted once, private to the receiver, and never under the limit.
10. **Every member gets personal money, the same amount by default (the earner too).** The group can change the amounts.
11. **The earner can mark their own account "Used by both".** It is then counted like a joint account under the group's choice. Without the mark, the partner's slice is $0.
12. **When personal money is missed, only the person receiving it is told.**
13. **A "Money and safety" help page.**
    - It covers healthy shared money and support numbers for economic abuse.
    - It is linked from the limit and personal money settings.
    - It is easy to leave fast and sends no alerts.
14. **A non-earner's small income of their own counts as their own money,** never shared unless they choose.
15. **The spend limit also applies to "Used by both" accounts,** with the same safety rules.

## Added scope (2026-11-21, owner)

Include in 08.12, after research (08.12-RESEARCH-TOPUP-CREDIT.md):
- **Top-up from own money.** The joint account is short, and one member pays the gap from their own separate account.
- **Personal credit for the gap.** One member pays the gap with their own credit: a credit card, an overdraft or a personal loan.

## Round 3 (2026-11-21): top-up, personal credit and instalments. Option A was chosen for every question.

Source: 08.12-RESEARCH-TOPUP-CREDIT.md section 7.

1. **The shared money is short.** The answer works from the shared money only. It names the gap ("The shared money is $300 short") and offers two choices: "Cover it from my own money" or "Ask Sam to cover $300". The ask reuses the spend-limit request flow.
2. **When cover counts.** Your own cover counts as soon as you choose it. Another member's cover counts only after they tap OK on their own phone.
3. **What the asker sees.** The asker sees only "Sam covers $300". They never see Sam's balance or answer, or whether Sam used credit.
4. **How a top-up is recorded.** It is a transfer from the payer's own account into the shared account, not spending for the payer, and the item stays one shared purchase. If the payer paid the shop directly, the item is recorded as two payments.
5. **Paying it back.** Nothing is owed unless the payer adds "Paid back from shared money". Those lines are visible to everyone. There is no "owes" label and no reminders.
6. **Spend limit and instalments.** The limit is compared with the whole price of an instalment plan, not each payment.
7. **Late fees.** A late fee is an optional typed line ("late fee if a payment is missed"), shown but never added to the cost.
8. **A one-name plan paid from shared money.** This is allowed. The answer names the person on the plan and says the lender asks only them to pay. The payments are shared planned lines, and the limit applies to the whole plan.
9. **A plan "in both names".** It counts only after the other person confirms on their own phone; until then it is the saver's own. Nobody can put a plan in someone else's name alone.
10. **The "Money and safety" page.** It gets one plain line on credit pressure, with free debt help and domestic abuse services named. The wording goes to sign-off, and it needs a compliance check: the research draft says "nobody should", but no "should" is allowed in copy.
