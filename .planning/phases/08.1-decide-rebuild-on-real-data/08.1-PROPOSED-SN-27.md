## Situation

Same Riley, same car: $9,000 over 48 months at 7.9%, $219.30 a month, bought 15 November 2026, first payment 15 December 2026. Her usual month ends **$100 over**: pay $2,500 on the 1st, bills $1,500, everyday spending $900 ($500 of it on essentials). November so far: $2,500 in, bills $1,500 paid, $450 of everyday spending, with $450 more still to come. Her everyday account holds **$600 today**: the $50 she started November with plus this month's $550 so far. It will be about $150 at the end of November. Safety net $2,000, full, locked; no overdraft recorded.

## How the engine reaches 1 December (judge.ts)

1. **One-off (cash) part:** the everyday account goes below $0 inside 90 days with no overdraft, so `not_yet`, ready on the **next payday**, 1 December.
2. **Monthly part:** the usual month after the payment is -$119.30, so `monthly_short` with **no** ready-by date.
3. **Combining (judge.ts ~line 930):** `not_yet` outranks `monthly_short` (CODE_PRECEDENCE). The monthly part's date is used only when the monthly part is also `not_yet`, so the cash part's 1 December is kept.
4. **`planReadyBy` (05.4-22, FIN-05):** a plan waits until spare money covers day one plus the **first payment** ($219.30). At $600 spare money covers it, so 1 December stays. (In the first draft, at $500, it gave January 2027: two months of $100 to cover the gap.)
5. **The guard (judge.ts ~line 1125)** drops the date only when the usual month is **already** short (SN-18). Nothing drops it when **the plan makes the month short**.

So the date answers "when can you afford the first payment", not "when does this purchase fit".

On those later dates the engine stopped at a question (the day-1 fixture triggers one, and it was not answered), so the table shows the engine's cash path, not a final code.

The date is wrong twice:
- **Wrong date:** on 1 December the cash path still goes $203.58 below $0. The cash check first passes around March 2027.
- **Wrong promise:** even then, every month is still $119.30 short with the car, so the best answer Riley can ever get is "Short each month" (a No). A ready-by date reads as "wait and it will fit". It never will.

No approved case covers this combination. BR-13 (the 05.4-22 source) has a plan that does **not** make the month short; its date waits for an overdraft to clear, which is a fair wait.

## Options for the owner

**(a) No ready-by date when the plan makes the usual month short.** Extend the line-1125 guard from "already short" to "short after the payment" (the monthly part is `monthly_short`). Riley gets "Not yet" with no date, and the depletion lines lead (`plan_makes_short`: about $119.30 short; `balanced_month_room`). Small, safe, matches SN-18. *Recommended.* It is an engine change: tests first, revision bump, changed-answers review.

**(b) Show the real cash date** (about March 2027) instead. Not recommended: it still implies "Fits" later, and the monthly part stays short.

**(c) Keep the engine and write the date into SN-27.** Not recommended: it signs off an answer the re-checks show is wrong.
