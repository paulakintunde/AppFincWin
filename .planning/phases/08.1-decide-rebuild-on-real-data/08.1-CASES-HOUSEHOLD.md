# Case sheet: household money in a Decide check (plan 08.1-08)

Status: **drafted 2026-11-20, waiting for the owner.** Cases HH-07 to HH-13 are `draft` in `cases/household.json`.
The right-answer runner only accepts a knownRed entry for an approved case, so on approval the status changes to `approved` and
the cases the engine does not yet meet go into `knownRed/household.json` (fixes FIN-19, UX-21, CMP-F04, CMP-F19) until Task 3
makes them pass. The older HH-01 to HH-06 stay `to_do` (not touched here).

All money is worked by hand. Shared set-up: pay on the 1st, today 15 November 2026, usual bills $1,200, everyday $800, no safety net,
no overdraft. A person with "$1,000 left" has $1,000 spare this month (HH-04's set-up).

**Pooling rule these cases assume (owner question A below):** when two partners have both opted in, the household money is the
viewer's own money left this month plus the partner's rounded figure. The viewer counts only their agreed share of that pool
(the 08.1-03 `sharedMoneyFor` slice). So the two slices always add up to the pool.

Ali and Noa split 60/40. Ali has $1,000 left; Noa has $4,000 left. Pool: $1,000 + $4,000 = $5,000. Ali's slice $3,000, Noa's slice $2,000.

## HH-07: Just me ignores the partner (CMP-F19, D-02)

**Situation.** Ali picks "Just me" and looks at a $2,000 purchase. Noa has opted in, with $4,000.
**Answer.** Not yet. Ready by 1 December. No household line.
**Why.** Just me counts only Ali's $1,000. Not enough for $2,000. Same as HH-02 and as today's app. On 1 December he has $1,000 + $1,000 spare = $2,000.

## HH-08: Household, both opted in (D-03)

**Situation.** Ali picks "Household" and looks at a $2,500 purchase.
**Answer.** Fits this month, $500 left. No "not shared" line.
**Why.** Pool $5,000. Ali's 60% = $3,000. $3,000 - $2,500 = $500.

## HH-09: Household, Noa has not shared (D-03, D-37)

**Situation.** Ali picks "Household", $2,000 purchase. Noa has not turned sharing on.
**Answer.** Not yet. Ready by 1 December (same as Just me). One line under the answer: "Only your money is counted. Noa hasn't shared theirs."
**Why.** Only Ali's $1,000 is counted, and nothing of Noa's is read. The answer is still given.

## HH-10: Noa checks too, and the two never spend the same money twice (08 D-37, CMP-F19)

**Situation.** The same day Noa picks "Household", $1,500 purchase. She has $4,000; Ali has shared his $1,000.
**Answer.** Fits this month, $500 left.
**Why.** Same pool, $5,000. Noa's 40% = $2,000. $2,000 - $1,500 = $500.

| | Slice of the pool | Buys | Left of slice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ali (HH-08) | $3,000 | $2,500 | $500 |
| Noa (HH-10) | $2,000 | $1,500 | $500 |
| Both | $5,000 | $4,000 | $1,000 |

The slices add to exactly the pool, so two Fits answers can never spend more than the pool. If each counted the whole pool, each could be told Fits for up to $5,000: $10,000 from $5,000.

## HH-11: A joint account's money is used (UX-21)

**Situation.** Ali's everyday account is in both names. $1,000 spare, $500 purchase, Just me.
**Answer.** Fits this month, $500 left, plus one line naming the account: money in an account in both names is counted.
**Why.** $1,000 - $500 = $500. Noa can also spend from that account, so the answer says so.

## HH-12: A late settle-up shows the free help line (CMP-F04)

**Situation.** Eve (US) owes Dan $200 for settling up. Dan asked 40 days ago; it is unpaid. She has $1,000 left, $500 purchase.
**Answer.** Fits this month, $500 left (unchanged), plus the free help line for the US (NFCC).
**Why.** $1,000 - $500 = $500. A late settle-up as the person who owes is a sign the pointer covers. The pointer never changes the answer.

## HH-13: She owes more than she can spend (CMP-F04)

**Situation.** Eve (US) owes Dan $1,500 (not late). She has $1,000 left, $500 purchase.
**Answer.** Fits this month, $500 left (unchanged), plus the free help line for the US.
**Why.** $1,500 owed is above the $1,000 she can spend.
