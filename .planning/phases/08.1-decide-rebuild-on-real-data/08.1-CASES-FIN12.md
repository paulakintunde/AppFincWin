# 08.1-11 FIN-12 draft cases: "0% if paid in full"

Status: draft, waiting for the owner (plan 08.1-11 Task 2). Cases live in
`src/engine/decide/v3/__tests__/rightAnswers/cases/borrowing.json` as BR-24, BR-25, BR-26 (status `draft`; they are
not in knownRed because knownRed only holds approved cases; they move to approved + knownRed on "approve").

Shared set-up: a $1,200 sofa bought 15 Nov 2026. Deal: 0% if paid in full by 15 Nov 2027 (12 months). Usual month: pay $3,000,
bills $1,400, everyday $1,200, so $400 is left. Floor a tenth of $2,600 = $260. Safety net full.

Back-charge method (to approve): the stated rate is applied from day one, each month on the balance left after the
earlier payments, summed to the end date and charged on that date. The balance still owed then (price left plus the
back-charge) carries on at the stated rate while the same payments continue.

## Case a

BR-24. The user pays $120 a month: 10 payments, finished 15 Sep 2027, two months before the end date. Back-charge: none.
Cost: $1,200 (the price). Fact: names the end date (15 Nov 2027). Month after buying: $400 - $120 = $280 (above the floor).
Answer: Fits (fits_month).

## Case b

BR-25. The user pays $75 a month: 16 payments, but the promotion ends after 12. Rate if not paid in full: 24.99%.
On 15 Nov 2027 $300 is still owed. Back-charge: $196.80 (24.99% a year = 2.0825% a month on the balance left each month,
12 months). Then $300 + $196.80 carries on at 24.99%: 8 more payments, $43.47 more interest. Total interest $240.27, total
paid $1,440.27. Month after buying: $400 - $75 = $325. Cash ($500 in the account) does not cover the sofa; interest is above
a tenth of the price and the rate is above 20%, so the answer is "Fits only by borrowing" (fits_by_borrowing), with the
back-charge fact: "If not paid in full by 15 November 2027, about $197 of interest is charged back."

## Case c

BR-26. The user has not typed the rate charged after the promotion. The back-charge cannot be worked out, so the answer asks
for the rate (needs_input). It never assumes 0%. The same ask happens when the end date is missing. No cost figure shows.
