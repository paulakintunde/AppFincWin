# Part A

## Phase 08.1: Code Review Report, part A (engine and data layer)

**Reviewed:** 2026-11-22
**Depth:** standard (with call-chain tracing into the household scope builder where the listed files depend on it)
**Files Reviewed:** 39

### Summary

The review covered the household money engine (joint slices, personal pool, spending rule, zero-share), the Decide v3 additions
(stretched cash horizon, ready-by search, deferred-interest costing, depletion lines, stage), and the data layer (joint facts,
spend rule, partner opt-ins, Go-ahead save, export manifest, query keys).

Things that held up:
- **Engine boundary:** no file under `src/engine/` imports from db, state, services, ui, features or react. Verified by grep.
- **Banned verdict words:** none of "advice", "recommend" or "you should" appears in `verdictTable.ts` or `caveats.ts`.
- **Splits:** every split goes through `allocate`, which uses whole-number BigInt maths, so joint slices add back to the
  account's counted money.
- **Weights:** the weights reaching `allocate` are whole numbers. `household_members.weight` is an `integer` column, custom
  shares are checked as whole numbers that add up to 100, and paid-in weights are whole minor units.
- **Joint queries:** every joint read is scoped to the household with `.eq('household_id', …)`, and the RPC checks
  `user_household_ids()`.

The serious problems are in how household money is wired into the answer, not in the splitting itself:
1. **Joint money can be counted in full.** The shared Decide build ignores the "still loading" flag. If the joint-facts read
   is slow or fails, it counts the whole joint balance as the viewer's.
2. **Pooled personal money can be counted twice.** The pool only adds up correctly when every member is in Household scope.

The other findings are about ready-by dates, consistency with the 90-day path, and money maths that uses decimals.

### Critical Issues

#### A-CR-01: The shared Decide build counts the whole joint balance while joint facts are loading or after the read fails

**File:** `src/data/queries/decideV3.ts:173-174` (with `src/features/household/decide/useHouseholdScopeSource.ts:40-47,64` and `src/features/decide/screens/engine/useCheckEngine.ts:97-106`)

**Issue:** `useDecideV3Live` reads only `useHouseholdScopeSource(false).source` and drops `.pending`. `withHouseholdSource`
returns the snapshot unchanged when that source is `undefined`, and the snapshot holds the viewer's own record of the joint
account at its full balance. `status` becomes `'ready'` anyway. This happens in two situations:
- **While loading:** `rule.isPending || jointRead.isPending` makes `source` undefined, but `useDecideV3` does not wait.
  Home, overview and the Decide home empty-check evaluation are judged on the whole joint balance, and the answer flashes.
- **After a failed read:** when the joint-facts read has failed (RPC error, or a first offline open with nothing cached),
  `jointRead.data` is undefined, so `hasJoint` is false. `active` is false and `waiting` is false, so the hook returns
  `NOT_ON` (`source: undefined, pending: false`).

  `useCheckEngine` then keeps `state.prepared` for a Just me check (`heldHousehold === null`), and nothing marks the answer
  as waiting. The Just me check is judged on the whole joint balance with no warning.

**Failure scenario:** Two partners each have the joint everyday account in their records. It holds $2,000, split 50/50. The
`joint_account_month` RPC fails, for example on an older server or a transient 500. Each partner types a $1,500 purchase on a
Just me check and each gets "Fits". Together they have been told they can spend $3,000 of $2,000. This is the exact
"two partners both told Fits for the same joint money" bug class.

**Fix:**
- While the joint facts are pending, `useDecideV3Live` should report `loading`.
- If the viewer holds a joint account (`accounts.is_shared`, which the snapshot already knows) and the joint facts could not
  be read, it should report `error`, or set the joint accounts to zero. It should never fall back to the full balance.

```ts
const hh = useHouseholdScopeSource(false);
const viewerHasJoint = snapshot.source?.accounts.some((a) => a.is_shared === true) ?? false;
const jointUnknown = viewerHasJoint && hh.source === undefined;
const status = failed ? 'error' : judged === null || hh.pending || jointUnknown ? 'loading' : 'ready';
```

In `useHouseholdScopeSource`, also count a failed joint read as "has joint, unknown" instead of `hasJoint = false`. For
example, set `hasJoint` from the viewer's account rows and not from the read that failed.

#### A-CR-02: Pooled personal money is counted twice when one partner checks in Household scope and the other in Just me

**File:** `src/engine/household/plan.ts:178,184`; `src/engine/household/pool.ts:186-200`

**Issue:** `spendSlice` gives the viewer `w_me × (own + each partner's rounded figure)`. The comment at `pool.ts:157-160`
says "the slices of everyone in the pool add up to the pool, so two 'Fits' answers can never spend the same money twice".
That only holds if every member in the pool is also judged on their slice.

`householdPlan` sets `personalDelta` only when `a.scope === 'household'`. The partner's Just me checks, home and overview
(`useHouseholdScopeSource(false)` → `scope: 'me'`) still count 100% of the partner's own personal money. Publishing only
every hour (`PUBLISH_EVERY_MS`) widens the gap: the viewer keeps counting a share of a partner figure the partner may already
have spent. Rounding down to $10 steps does not cover staleness.

**Failure scenario:** Under a set 50/50 spending split, A has $0 left and B has $1,000 left. Both have opted in. A runs a
Household check for $500 and gets "Fits", because A's slice is 50% of $1,000. B runs a Just me check for $900 and gets
"Fits", because B's own money is $1,000. Together they are told $1,400 of $1,000 fits.

**Fix:** Pick one rule and enforce it in `householdPlan`. Either:
- whenever both opt-ins are on and the rule is `shares`, apply the personal-pool delta in every scope, including the
  published-figure owner's Just me path; or
- count only the partner's surplus that the partner has explicitly set aside for the pool.

At the very least, the Household answer must carry a caveat that the partner's own checks still count all of their money.
Add a property test that runs A's Household view and B's Just me view together and asserts that the two together fit inside
the pool.

### Warnings

#### A-WR-01: The ready-by date can be pulled earlier than the safe start day the search found

**File:** `src/engine/decide/v3/judge/judge.ts:976-978`

**Issue:** When `firstSafeStart` finds a later day, the code stores only the month: `readyBy = { kind: 'month', month: yearMonthOf(found.date) }`.
`readyKey` then reads a month as `YYYY-MM-01` (`judge.ts:684`). Most candidate start days are paydays in the middle of a
month, so the date given can be earlier than `found.date`. It can even be earlier than the date as first judged. That breaks
D-05's own rule (`judge.ts:969`): "never earlier than the date as judged".

**Failure scenario:**
- The first ready-by is the date 2027-02-10, and the search finds 2027-02-28 (a payday).
- The answer becomes the month 2027-02, which reads as the 1st.
- A user who checks again on Feb 1 still gets Not yet.
- Any screen that formats a month ready-by as "from February" is promising a date the engine has already found unsafe.

**Fix:** Keep the exact day:
```ts
} else if (found !== null && found.date > readyKey(readyBy)) {
  readyBy = { kind: 'date', date: found.date };
}
```

#### A-WR-02: The stretched horizon builds bills differently from the 90-day path (no `expected` map)

**File:** `src/engine/decide/v3/cash/horizon.ts:217`

**Issue:** `buildHorizon` calls `openCashFlows` without `expected`. The main path (`prepare.ts:237`, D-09) counts
expected-line bill dues by `expectedDueCounts`, but the horizon counts each of those bills at the series amount. This
matters in three places:
- The ready-by search with `start = asOf` reads a different first-month path from the one the cash check judged.
- `laterDipOf` / `firstBelowZero` read that same different path.
- If an expected due counts for more than `s.amount`, the horizon is more hopeful than the path that produced the Not yet. A
  ready-by could then be given for a day the main path would still reject.

**Fix:** Pass `expected: input.expected` into the first window's `openCashFlows` call. It only matches keys in the current
month, so later windows are not affected. Add a test that the horizon and `openCashPath` agree day by day over the first 90
days.

#### A-WR-03: Laying the joint slice wipes out the viewer's own unsynced joint lines (offline queued spend disappears)

**File:** `src/engine/decide/v3/householdMoney.ts:263-271`; `src/engine/household/jointMoney.ts:134-135`; `src/engine/household/plan.ts:153-161`

**Issue:** `applyJointMoney` replaces each joint account's balance with `lay.balances[id]`. That slice is worked out only
from the server's joint facts (`fetchJointAccountFacts`). `cashDelta` is the slice less `viewerBalance`, taken from the
viewer's local records, so whatever the local records hold beyond the server copy is cancelled out. A joint spend the viewer
logged offline is still in the write queue, so it is in the local snapshot but not in the server facts. It drops out of the
spending balance entirely. The same happens in the gap between an optimistic insert and the refetch.

**Failure scenario:** Offline, the viewer logs an $800 grocery spend on the joint everyday account, then opens Decide. The
joint slice still reflects the balance before the spend, so the check counts $400 (their half) more than they really have.
The unsaved-writes caution is the only signal.

**Fix:** Lay the slice over the server facts adjusted by the viewer's pending, unsynced joint lines: add the local-only lines
on joint accounts to `balance` and `monthIn`/`monthOut` before slicing. Or, while `unsavedWrites > 0` touches a joint
account, keep the viewer's local balance for that account and do not use the slice.

#### A-WR-04: The shared joint safety net's saved amount is worked out and then dropped (`netSaved` is never used)

**File:** `src/engine/household/jointMoney.ts:126-132,141-145`; `src/engine/household/plan.ts:165-167`

**Issue:** The header comment (lines 7-8) promises: "when it is the shared safety net it is the net's saved amount, whole,
once for both". `jointSlices` works out `netSaved`, but `layOf` keeps only `monthDelta`, `cashDelta` and `balances`. No
production code reads `netSaved` (checked by grep), and the joint safety-net account is laid into the viewer's inputs at
balance 0 with `use: everyday`.

So a household whose only safety net is a joint savings account gets a missing net in Decide:
- no `fits_uses_net`;
- `no_net` / `small_net` caution lines;
- a different ready-by date.

None of these match the owner's Q10 decision.

**Fix:** Carry `netSaved` through `JointLayShape` and have `applyJointMoney` set or raise `inputs.safetyNet.saved`, still
locked and never spendable. Otherwise, remove the field and correct the comment so nobody relies on it.

#### A-WR-05: The viewer's share of joint money out uses decimal maths

**File:** `src/engine/household/plan.ts:160` (`myBill` is computed as a decimal fraction in `src/features/decide/snapshot/v3/householdScope.ts:142`)

**Issue:** `Math.round(j.monthOut * myBill)` multiplies minor units by a decimal fraction (for example 1/3). That breaks the
project rule of whole minor units with explicit rounding, and it bypasses `allocate`. Across members, the rounded shares
need not add up to `monthOut`. For three equal members and `monthOut = 100`, each gets 33, which adds up to 99. One unit of
`viewerOwnMonth` per member is then wrong, and that error flows straight into `monthDelta`. Ties also round toward +∞, not
by the project's half-up-on-absolute rule.

**Fix:** Pass the bill weights, not a fraction, and use the same split as everything else:
```ts
viewerOwnMonth: ownMonth ?? minorUnits((j.paidInBy?.[myIndex] ?? 0) - sharedMoneyFor(j.monthOut, billWeightsOrEqual, myIndex)),
```

#### A-WR-06: A bills-purpose joint account counts money carried from earlier months as this month's money

**File:** `src/engine/household/jointMoney.ts:104-105`

**Issue:** For `purpose === 'bills'`, both the month slice and the cash slice are set to `s.cash`, the viewer's slice of the
whole balance above the bills due, carried money included. The everyday branch uses `monthIn - monthOut` for the month. As a
result, a bills account with a large carried-over buffer inflates the viewer's "money left this month". The month line is
put in the current month (`withMonthLine`), so a purchase can be labelled `fits_month` ("from this month's money") when it
really comes from money saved in earlier months. That also hides the `fits_unspent` / earlier-money facts.

**Fix:** Use the same month and carried split as everyday. Take the month figure as `min(above, max(0, monthIn - monthOut))`
and leave the rest as cash only:
```ts
const monthPart = Math.max(0, Math.min(above, a.monthIn - a.monthOut));
const s = slice(above, monthPart, a.carried, split, a, n, idx);
return { month: s.month, cash: s.cash };
```

#### A-IN-03: `householdLeft` / `householdDelta` are no longer used, and the opt-in reads are not scoped to the household

The main problem is in the household builder. `jointToHome` works out a joint account's "carried" money from a `monthOut` that `jointWithPending` has already raised by pending outflows. - The off-fast switch runs as two independent hook instances, and a cached "off" is ignored on first paint.

### Critical Issues

#### B-CR-01: Joint "carried" money is overstated by pending outflows, so joint money is split wrongly under "What each person brings"

**File:** `src/features/decide/snapshot/v3/householdScope.ts:170-183` and `:186-209` (called from `src/features/decide/snapshot/v3/buildInputs.ts:551`)

**Issue:** `jointWithPending` adds pending money-out to `monthOut` ("joins the money paid out so far as the month's planned outflow", line 179). `jointToHome` then computes
`carried: minorUnits(balance - (monthIn - monthOut))` (line 203) from that raised `monthOut`. The engine defines `carried` as "the balance less this month's net so far (paid in less paid out)" (`engine/household/jointMoney.ts:34`). `balance` comes from paid lines only (`scopeSource.ts` jointSourcesOf: opening balance plus paid lines), so pending money has not left it. The result is `carried = true carried + pending`.

In `slice()` under the `paidIn` split (`jointMoney.ts:83-86`), `carriedPart = min(carried, cashTotal)` is split equally and only the rest is split by pay-ins. Every pending bill therefore moves joint cash from the pay-in split into the equal split.

**Failure scenario:** A joint everyday account holds 2,000. This month A paid in 1,500, B paid in 0, nothing has been paid out, and a 1,200 rent line is pending. The true carried amount is 500, so A's cash slice should be 250 + 1,500 = 1,750 and B's should be 250. With the bug, carried = 2,000 - (1,500 - 1,200) = 1,700. B's slice becomes 850 and A's becomes 1,150. B's Decide answer counts 600 that B does not have and can say Fits for a purchase B cannot afford. The "How spending is shared" view (`spendRuleView.ts:168`) calls `jointToHome` without pending rows, so it shows different figures from Decide for the same household.

**Fix:** Work out `carried` from the paid-only figure, and keep the pending outflow as a separate field:
```ts
// jointWithPending: keep the paid-only month-out separately
return { ...j, billsDueBeforeNextIn: due, monthOut: out, monthOutPaid: j.monthOut };
// jointToHome
const paidOut = toHome(j.monthOutPaid ?? j.monthOut, j.currency);
...
carried: minorUnits(balance - (monthIn - paidOut)),
```
Add a builder test with a pending joint outflow under the `paidIn` rule. Assert carried equals balance minus (paid in minus paid out) and that the member slices match the hand-worked figures above.

### Warnings

#### B-WR-01: The off-fast switch runs as two independent instances that can disagree

**File:** `src/features/decide/useDecideServerSwitch.ts:36-78`; callers `app/(app)/_layout.tsx:56` and `app/(app)/(tabs)/decide.tsx:19`

**Issue:** Each caller mounts its own `useDecideServerSwitch()`. Each has its own fetch, 3 s timeout, cache read, `lastRead` and AppState listener. A failed read keeps that instance's last value, so after one fetch times out and the other succeeds, the two hold different values until a later refresh. The refresh only runs after 15 minutes in the background.

**Failure scenario:** The switch is flipped to `'true'` while the network is poor. The layout's read succeeds, closes the `Stack.Protected` routes and turns off the notification observer. The tab's read times out, so the tab keeps rendering `DecideHome`. Tapping an open check or "Check it" then pushes `/decide/answer/[id]`, a route that is now guarded, which is a navigation dead end. If the failures are the other way round, the tab shows the paused card while notification taps still route into Decide.

**Fix:** Read the switch once and share it. Hoist it into a provider or a TanStack Query (`useQuery({ queryKey: ['app_config','decide_off'], ... })` with `placeholderData` from the cache), and have both the layout and the tab read that one source.

#### B-WR-02: A cached "off" is ignored on first paint, so Decide and the notification route open before the cache loads

**File:** `src/features/decide/useDecideServerSwitch.ts:37` and `:60-64`

**Issue:** `useState(false)` starts every cold start "on". The cached `'true'` is applied only after `AsyncStorage.getItem` resolves. On the first render the layout enables `Stack.Protected guard={DECIDE_OPEN && !decideOff}` and `DecideNotificationObserver enabled`, and the tab mounts `DecideHome`, which starts its reads. A launch from a notification tap is handled at this point. Fail-open was chosen for a *failed server read* (Q6). Overriding a known "off" during the cache window is not the same thing.

**Failure scenario:** Decide was switched off because of a wrong answer. A user taps a "bill due" Decide notification while offline. The app cold-starts, the observer routes to `/decide/check/[id]`, and the user sees an answer from the engine that was switched off. The cache flips `off` a few milliseconds later, but the screen is already open. Offline, the server never answers to correct it.

**Fix:** Add a third "unknown" state (`off: boolean | null`). Hold the guard and the observer closed, or show a loading state, until the cache read settles. Only fall back to "on" after both the cache and the server fail. Separately (see B-IN-09), consider a timed foreground re-check so a long session picks up an "off".

#### B-WR-03: The household "not shared" caveat can say things that are false

**File:** `src/features/decide/snapshot/v3/householdScope.ts:153-155` and `:244`; copy at `src/i18n/locales/decideScreens/answer.ts:32-33`; rendered by `src/features/decide/screens/logic/sayResult.ts:241-250`

**Issue:** `notShared` lists every partner who is not in `pool`. A partner is left out of `pool` for three reasons: their opt-in is off, they have no published figure, or they **did** share but in another currency (`p.currency === home`). The copy is "Only your money is counted. {{name}} hasn't shared theirs."

**Failure scenarios:**
- In a household of three or more, A shares and B does not. A's money is pooled into the answer, but the caveat says "Only your money is counted. B hasn't shared theirs." The first sentence is false and describes the answer wrongly.
- Partner C has both opt-ins on but publishes in EUR while the viewer's home currency is USD. The answer says "C hasn't shared theirs." C did share. The caveat blames a household member for something they did, and hides the real reason (no conversion).

**Fix:** Split the reason in the engine caveat values, for example `{ names, counted: pool.length > 0 ? 'some' : 'none' }`, and list currency mismatches separately (`currencyNames`). Use different copy: "{{name}}'s money isn't counted" when some partners are counted, and "{{name}} shared in {{currency}}, which isn't counted" for a currency mismatch.

#### B-WR-04: The installment-plan sheet can disable "Use" with no visible reason after switching to "0% if paid off in time"

**File:** `src/features/decide/screens/sheets/InstallmentPlanSheet.tsx:182`, `:233`, `:372-395`

**Issue:** `rateBad` comes from the plan-rate field (`d.rateText`). Turning the deferred switch on hides that field, its error note (`rateBad && !d.deferred`) and the example line. But `canUse = !rateBad && ...` still includes `rateBad`, and in deferred mode the typed plan rate is never used (`apr: percent('0')`).

**Failure scenario:** The user types "150" in the rate field (above MAX_RATE, so `rateBad` is true) and then turns on "0% if paid off in time", fills in the end date and the rate after the promotion. The Use/Save pill stays disabled. No error shows because the field that has it is hidden, and `need` only covers "covered by cash" and the first payment.

**Fix:**
```ts
const canUse = !(d.deferred ? afterBad : rateBad) && !covered && firstPayment !== null;
```
Alternatively, clear `rateText` when the switch turns on.

#### B-WR-05: `jointWithPending` skips overdue pending bills and does not apply the builder's row filters

**File:** `src/features/decide/snapshot/v3/householdScope.ts:176`; caller `buildInputs.ts:551`

**Issue:**
1. `r.local_date < today` skips any pending outflow dated before today. A pending bill whose date has passed but that has not cleared, which is exactly what the readiness "Did it happen?" rows ask about, is still going to leave the joint account. It is left out of both `billsDueBeforeNextIn` and `monthOut`.
2. The builder dedupes rows by id and drops `deleted_at !== null` (`buildInputs.ts:372-375`) for every other use. The joint call passes the raw `[...source.rows, ...source.upcomingRows]`, and `JointRow` has no `deleted_at`, so a soft-deleted pending joint line still counts as money due. ### Critical Issues

#### C-CR-01: A shared safety net keeps leaking to the old household after its owner leaves, and the leaver cannot turn it off

**File:** `supabase/migrations/20261125000200_decide_partner_optins.sql:132-150, 154-168, 171-188` (together with `20261122000800_household_leave_remove_delete.sql:469` and `20261122000500_household_invites.sql:165-166`)

**Issue:** `safety_net_shares` is on the "never moves" list (`hh_move_tables` comment), and `hh_depart` never deletes or disables it. `savings_goals` *does* move with the leaver (`hh_move_personal_rows`).

After A leaves household H1:
- The share row stays as `household_id = H1, enabled = true`. Its goal is still live, but it now belongs to A's new household H2.
- `shared_safety_net()` filters only by `s.household_id in (my households)` and `g.deleted_at is null`. It never checks that `s.owner_id` is still a member of `s.household_id`. So B (still in H1) keeps receiving A's safety-net target (`size`) and `saved` amount.
- `publish_safety_net_saved()` still matches (`owner_id = uid and enabled`). `AppPartnerFigurePublisher` uses `fetchOwnNetShares`, which has no household filter, so A's device keeps pushing an updated saved figure into H1 every hour. A's personal savings keep flowing to a household A has left.
- A cannot stop it:
  - `set_shared_safety_net` now looks up `g.household_id` (H2, one member) and returns `no_partner` before it reaches the upsert.
  - `SharedSafetyNetRow` hides itself unless `memberCount === 2`.
  - If A later shares the net in a new household, `on conflict (goal_id) do update` does not update `household_id`, so the row stays pointed at H1.

This breaks the rule that household data must never cross households. It is also a GDPR problem (data keeps going to people the user has deliberately left).

**Fix (new forward migration, do not edit 20261125000200):**
```sql
-- 1. Tidy what is already wrong in production.
delete from public.safety_net_shares s
 where not exists (select 1 from public.household_members m
                    where m.household_id = s.household_id and m.user_id = s.owner_id);
-- 2. Read side: the owner must still be in the household, and the goal must still be in it.
create or replace function public.shared_safety_net() ... 
   where s.enabled and s.owner_id <> uid
     and exists (select 1 from public.household_members o where o.household_id = s.household_id and o.user_id = s.owner_id)
     and g.household_id = s.household_id
     and s.household_id in (select m.household_id from public.household_members m where m.user_id = uid) ...
-- 3. Write side: publish only while the goal and owner are still in the share's household.
--    In set_shared_safety_net: on conflict (goal_id) do update set household_id = excluded.household_id, ...
--    and allow p_enabled = false without the "< 2 members" check.
-- 4. Departure: an AFTER DELETE trigger on household_members (like hh_strip_spend_share) that deletes
--    safety_net_shares / partner_figures where owner_id = old.user_id and household_id = old.household_id.
```

#### C-CR-02: Decide counts a departed member's tombstone account (and archived joint accounts) as an "everyday" joint account

**File:** `src/db/jointAccounts.ts` `fetchJointPurposes` (read by `src/data/queries/jointAccounts.ts:18` → `src/features/household/decide/scopeSource.ts:97-116` → `useHouseholdScopeSource.ts:41-63`). Also `supabase/migrations/20261125000700_joint_account_purpose.sql:72-82` (`joint_account_month`).

.is('deleted_at', null)
.is('tombstone_of', null)
.is('archived_at', null)
