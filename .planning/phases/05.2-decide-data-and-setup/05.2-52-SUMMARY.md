Two further facts the fixture pins:
- Typical left = typical income - bills - e - set aside. Since bills and set aside cannot be negative, a typical left of 16,280.94 with e 4,780.16 means the engine's typical income was at least 21,061.10.
- A steady earner whose income swings (here one month with two pay sources) is treated as variable until they confirm "steady"; typical income is then the median of the window's recorded income (400,000), and a brand-new pay series is not yet in it. Once confirmed steady, typical income is the live pay series sum (776,744). Both follow D-34 / D-36.

### The two walkthrough gaps
- **Home In $203,974.77 vs Decide "$6,428.54 in" (Oct 2026):** Home counts every paid, non-transfer, non-refund positive row (`totals.ts`); Decide counts only paid income that is a pay series, tagged as available, or (variable profile) any income. In the fixture Home In minus Decide money in = the test inflow (20,000,000) + the untagged positive GBP row (2,510), exactly. A difference of this size (about $197,546) therefore means large untagged positive rows (the test account's deposits) that Decide, correctly for a salaried profile, does not treat as the user's income. Different by definition, now labelled on the Summary.
- **Typical income of about $21,061:** none of the six candidate causes alone reproduces it. The only documented routes from a $4,000 salary + $3,767 GBP series (about $7,767) to 21,061 or more are (1) a mixed profile with a steady amount of about $13,300 or more added to the pay series (e), or (2) a variable-treated window whose median of recorded income is that high. Both need data not in the repo (the owner's saved `steady_amount`, `income_types`, `variable_method`, or pay series). No production database was queried, per the plan's rules.

**Open item for the owner (not a code change):** in Settings > Decide income check the saved income types, counting method and "steady amount".
