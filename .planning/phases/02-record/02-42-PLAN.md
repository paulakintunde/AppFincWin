What fx-monitor did, and where each duty goes now (decision item 2 removes it):
- Staleness alerts (`findStale`, `currencies.staleness_limit_days`): REMOVED by the decision. The column stays (squawk's ban-drop-column needs a contract-ok marker backed by a min_supported_version raise in an earlier migration. It gets a `comment on column … 'Retired 2026-10-07 …'` and no code reads it. A later migration that already raises the floor for another reason can drop it.
- `fx_auto_accept_holds()` (D-12 auto-accept after 2 days): KEPT. resolve-rate calls it lazily on each fetching call (plan 02-44), so a held rate still auto-accepts without a cron.
- `fx_restamp_pending()` (re-stamps due pending rows daily, including future-dated rows once their day arrives): DROPPED. The client pending-rate sweep (plan 02-47) replaces it. That keeps decision item 6 ("filled in when the device is online again") true without a daily job.
- `fx_pending_rows_count()` (digest line "N pending rows"): DROPPED. The only alert left is an on-demand fetch failure (decision item 7).
- The digest email and its Resend secrets: the email moves into resolve-rate and fires only on fetch failure (plan 02-44). `fx_alerts` and `emailed_at` stay.
- Vault rows `fx_sync_url`, `fx_sync_secret`, `fx_monitor_url`, `fx_monitor_secret`: deleted in the production rollout (plan 02-50), not in this migration. Locally they never exist, and keeping vault writes out of migrations matches how they were created (by hand at deploy time).
