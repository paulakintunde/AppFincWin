# Decide: the major changes, in plain words

Written 2026-10-25. Each change shows what Decide does **today**, what it does **next**, and **what you'll see** in the mockup ("Can I Afford It — Decide mockup").

---

### 1. A short setup the first time you open Decide
- **Today:** Decide guesses from whatever you've logged.
- **Next:** a few questions, one per screen: how you're paid, your regular payments, your everyday spending, your emergency fund, your envelopes and your unspent money. Only 5 steps are needed. You can skip the rest or finish later.
- **You'll see:** a welcome screen, a progress bar of five sections, and "Needed" in place of "Skip" on the steps that matter.

### 2. Regular pay and bills come first
- **Today:** spending is averaged from history, so one odd month can swing the answer.
- **Next:** Decide finds your regular payments (rent around the 1st, a phone bill around the 12th) and asks you to confirm each one. Only confirmed payments count. If an amount changes, it asks: "Rent looks like $1,850 now. Update?"

### 3. Money in vs money out, every month
- **Today:** a "free" figure that could look healthy early in the month and be wrong by the end.
- **Next:** one bar shows what's gone out so far, what's still to come, and what's left. The big number is **what October is on track to leave**, after the spending you haven't done yet.

### 4. Unspent money, not your bank balance
- **Today:** cash in the bank is treated as available.
- **Next:** **unspent money** is your balance minus bills still due, minus expected spending, minus envelopes, minus your emergency fund. You confirm it once. After that it **moves on its own** as money comes in and goes out, and Decide asks again only if it drifts.

### 5. Your emergency fund stays out of the way
- **Today:** a "cushion" can be used to make a purchase look affordable.
- **Next:** your emergency fund is a separate goal. Ordinary purchases never touch it. Only a purchase you mark as an emergency, like replacing a broken work laptop, can use it, and the answer shows how long it takes to rebuild.

### 6. Saving with a purpose: buckets
- **Today:** saving is one number.
- **Next:** on the saving step you can, if you like, split your saving into **buckets**: college, a house down payment, a new car, a trip, starting a business, a wedding, or something else. Each has a monthly amount and an optional goal, and it shows when you'll reach it ("At $100 a month, reached by June"). Buckets become envelopes, so their money is kept for its purpose.

### 7. Income can be any shape
- **Today:** one rule for everyone.
- **Next:** you say how you're paid: salary, commission, freelance, your own business, a pension, or no fixed income. If your income changes, you pick how to count it: as it comes in, spread over the months a big payment covers, as a steady amount you pay yourself, or by your bank balance. Side income and dividends are marked "I spend it" or "reinvested". Pay on the 1st and 15th is counted correctly as 24 payments a year.

### 8. Activity helps with income
- **Next:** if you haven't set up how you're paid, Activity shows a card asking. Any income can be tagged with its type. When a new kind of income shows up (an Etsy payout, say), Activity asks once what it is.

### 9. Questions that grow with the price
- **Today:** a $5 coffee and a car go through the same five steps.
- **Next:** the price puts the purchase into one of **15 bands**, from under $50 to over $10M. Small buys get an instant answer. Bigger ones also ask:
  - "What is this for you?" (an investment in you, for work, a need, nice to have, a treat, a luxury);
  - what it's worth to you;
  - whether you looked at alternatives.

  Cars, homes, tuition and similar items ask for their usual extra costs: insurance, fuel, tax and fees. Decide never fills in the amounts for you.

### 10. Payment plans you can trust
- **Next:** pay all at once or in **payments**: weekly, every 2 weeks or monthly. You enter the rate or APR, the loan length, the down payment and fees, and Decide works out each payment. "The whole plan" shows the price, tax, fees, interest and running costs, adding up to one total.

### 11. Clear answers, one sentence each
- **Today:** the headline could say "It fits" above a card that said it doesn't.
- **Next:** there are six answers, each with one plain sentence and a colour:
  - **Fits this month** (green);
  - **Fits from your unspent money** (teal);
  - **Not yet, covered by {month}** (rust);
  - **This payment could leave you short each month** (rust);
  - **Uses your emergency fund**, for emergencies only (amber);
  - **A little more is needed** (grey), with a "Help me check this" form.

  A strip under the answer shows exactly **where the money comes from**.

### 12. "What could I do instead?"
- **Next:** one tap away, four choices side by side:
  - buy it now;
  - keep the money;
  - put it in a **fixed-rate deposit** at the rate your bank offers;
  - buy it later.

  A slider shows how spending a little less each month brings the date closer. The app shows the trade-offs, and **the choice is yours**.

### 13. Decide gets sharper over time
- **Next:** after about three months of records, a card says "**Decide can be sharper now**". It shows what's changed, for example "Your pay looks like $4,150 now" or "A new regular payment: Gym $45". You choose "Use it" or "Keep mine" for each. This matters most for new users who started with little data.

### 14. Easy to change anything
- **Next:**
  - every setup answer can be changed on its own from Decide settings;
  - every check can be edited, copied with another price, deleted or reopened;
  - Record as bought, Plan it and Not buying can all be undone;
  - everything works offline and syncs later.

### 15. Words that don't judge
- **Today:** "free", "overspent", "Go ahead", "Pass".
- **Next:** "unspent money", "envelopes", "typical month", "Not yet", "Record as bought", "Plan it", "Not buying". Forecasts say "could" or "based on your records". The app never says "you should".

### 16. While the new Decide is being built
- **Next:** the Decide tab shows "**A better Decide is on its way**". Today's Decide is hidden, because it can give a wrong "fits" early in the month.

---

**What doesn't change:**
- Your logged transactions, accounts, regular payments, envelopes (today's pots) and past decisions are kept.
- Past decisions are never recalculated.
- The look stays FincWin's: the same fonts, colours, card shapes and five tabs.
